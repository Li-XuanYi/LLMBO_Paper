\section{Proposed LLMBO-MO}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Motivation and Design Principle}

For the constrained problem in Eq.~\eqref{eq:cmop}, LLMBO-MO treats
large-language-model (LLM) output as an advisory preference rather than as an
objective observation.
The asymmetric interface assigns complementary roles: contextual LLM
preferences guide candidate placement, while simulator evaluations supply the
objective and feasibility evidence used by the Pareto set and Gaussian-process
(GP) surrogate.
The framework realizes this separation through two controlled, temporary
touchpoints: a screened warm-start portfolio constructed before the first BO
iteration, and weight-conditioned regional preferences expressed during an
early guidance window of $T_{\mathrm L}$ iterations.
Parsing, protocol-domain, and covariance gates determine whether each advisory
signal is activated; the remaining optimization pipeline stays numerical.
This separation addresses the central engineering risk: contextual knowledge
may guide a search, but it cannot certify an objective value, feasibility, or
Pareto membership.

\subsection{Overall Framework}

Figure~\ref{fig:framework} illustrates the two advisory paths and their
relationship to the common multiobjective BO backbone. The warm-start path
shapes the initial portfolio, while the early-guidance path supplies validated
preferences to the acquisition stage. In both cases, the simulator remains the
sole source of objective and feasibility evidence.
\input{figures/fig_llambo_mo_framework}

The two advisory paths are temporary and recover the ordinary numerical search
whenever guidance is unavailable or rejected. This separation allows
contextual knowledge to influence candidate placement without changing the
evidence used to fit the GP or construct the Pareto set.

\subsection{Main Steps}

\subsubsection{Protocol-Domain-Screened LLM Warm Start}
\label{sec:warm_start}

A central challenge in BO is the cold start: before task-specific observations
accumulate, the surrogate is shaped primarily by the initial design and the GP
prior.
Random initialization, while unbiased, can waste a substantial fraction of the
evaluation budget on unpromising regions when the design space is large and
constrained.
Model-based priors can mitigate this~\cite{refPrBO} but require per-cell,
per-condition calibration.
LLMBO-MO instead uses the LLM to propose an initial candidate set from a
natural-language description of the task.
Because the LLM output is not assumed to be physically accurate, every
candidate passes through a protocol-domain screening pipeline before
evaluation.

The warm-start prompt supplies the cell-model context, variable bounds,
protocol geometry ($K$-stage constant-current, SOC-domain representation), and
battery-inspired scheduling heuristics such as decreasing current profiles and
avoiding extreme terminal spans.
The LLM returns numerical candidate protocols, each accompanied by a
self-reported confidence score.
The repair-and-screen pipeline processes the raw LLM output: non-numerical
entries are discarded, duplicate and non-finite values are removed,
box-violating coordinates are clipped, and candidates that cannot satisfy the
dependent terminal SOC span $\Delta s_K\geq\Delta s_K^{\min}$ are rejected.
These checks establish protocol-domain validity; operational voltage and
temperature feasibility is determined only by simulation.

Let $h_{\mathrm{ws}}(\btheta)\in[0,1]$ denote the LLM-reported candidate score,
which is used only for ranking.
A soft penalty discourages a terminal SOC span below the preferred boundary:
\begin{equation}
p_{\mathrm{soft}}(\btheta)=
\operatorname{clip}\!\left(
\frac{\Delta s_K^{\mathrm{soft}}-\Delta s_K}{w_{\mathrm{soft}}},0,1
\right).
\label{eq:soft_penalty}
\end{equation}
Here $w_{\mathrm{soft}}>0$ controls the transition width of the penalty.
Let $\mathbb I_{\mathrm{mono}}(\btheta)$ indicate a nonincreasing current
sequence, and let $\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sel}}$ denote the candidates already
selected for the warm-start portfolio.
The nonnegative coefficients $\omega_p$, $\omega_m$, and $\omega_d$ weight the
soft-penalty, monotonicity, and diversity terms, respectively.
The selected portfolio is built greedily using
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
J_{\mathrm{ws}}(\btheta)
&=h_{\mathrm{ws}}(\btheta)
-\omega_p p_{\mathrm{soft}}(\btheta)
+\omega_m\mathbb I_{\mathrm{mono}}(\btheta)\\
&\quad+\omega_d d_{\min}(\btheta,\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sel}}),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:portfolio}
\end{equation}
where the soft penalty is defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:soft_penalty}.
Normalized decision coordinates are denoted by a hat, and the diversity term is
\begin{equation}
d_{\min}(\btheta,\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sel}})=
\begin{cases}
0, & \mathcal C_{\mathrm{sel}}=\varnothing,\\[2pt]
\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf c\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sel}}}
\|\widehat{\btheta}-\widehat{\mathbf c}\|_2,
& \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:portfolio_diversity}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:portfolio} therefore balances the LLM score, a scheduling
heuristic, the soft penalty, and the diversity term in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:portfolio_diversity}.
The zero-distance convention in Eq.~\eqref{eq:portfolio_diversity} ensures that
the first selected candidate receives no artificial diversity bonus.
Random protocol-domain-valid points complete the initial design to maintain
exploration coverage, and only simulator-evaluated candidates are appended to
the optimization database $\bD$.

This warm-start design has two properties that distinguish it from both random
initialization and model-prior methods.
First, it requires no per-cell calibration: the same natural-language prompt
template is used across experiments, with only the variable bounds and
cell context updated.
Second, protocol-domain screening guarantees that every LLM-sourced initial
point satisfies the same box and SOC-completion constraints as the random
points, ensuring a fair initialization comparison.

\subsubsection{Objective Transformation and ParEGO Backbone}
\label{sec:parego_backbone}

LLMBO-MO shares its scalarization and surrogate backbone with
ParEGO~\cite{ref4} to enable a controlled fixed-budget comparison.
The three objectives span different numerical ranges (charging time in seconds,
temperature rise in Kelvin, degradation proxy in arbitrary units).
A logarithmic transform is applied to charging time and the degradation proxy
to compress their dynamic range; temperature rise remains on its physical
scale:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\tilde{\mathbf f}(\btheta)&=
[\ell_t(\btheta),\,\Delta T_p(\btheta),\,\ell_D(\btheta)]^\top,\\
\ell_t(\btheta)&=\log_{10}\!\max(t_c/t_{\mathrm{ref}},\varepsilon_t),\\
\ell_D(\btheta)&=\log_{10}\!\max(D_{\mathrm{chg}}/D_{\mathrm{ref}},
\varepsilon_D).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:objective_transform}
\end{equation}
Here $t_{\mathrm{ref}}$ and $D_{\mathrm{ref}}$ are positive reference scales,
while $\varepsilon_t$ and $\varepsilon_D$ are positive numerical floors.
The transformation in Eq.~\eqref{eq:objective_transform} is applied to both
LLMBO-MO and ParEGO, so differences in
hypervolume are not artifacts of different objective scalings.
For objective $i$, let $R_{i,t}^{\mathrm{hist}}$ be the current observed range
and define the global transformed range by
$R_i^{\mathrm{glob}}=\tilde y_i^{\mathrm{ref}}-\tilde y_i^{\mathrm{ideal}}$,
where the ideal and reference coordinates are mapped through the same
objective transformation.
For a fixed threshold $\rho_R\in(0,1)$, the scale used by the implementation is
\begin{equation}
s_{i,t}=
\begin{cases}
R_i^{\mathrm{glob}},&
R_{i,t}^{\mathrm{hist}}<\rho_R R_i^{\mathrm{glob}},\\
R_{i,t}^{\mathrm{hist}},&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:hybrid_scaling}
\end{equation}
The hybrid rule in Eq.~\eqref{eq:hybrid_scaling} uses the full global range
when the early observed range is
too narrow, preventing premature exploitation of an incorrectly estimated
scale.

Following ParEGO~\cite{ref4}, the three objectives ($m=3$) are combined through
an augmented Tchebycheff target,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\bar f_{i,t}(\btheta)
&=\frac{|\tilde f_i(\btheta)-\tilde z_{i,t}^{\ast}|}{s_{i,t}},\\
g_t(\btheta\mid\bw)
&=\max_i w_i\bar f_{i,t}(\btheta)
\\[-2pt]
&\quad+\rho_{\mathrm{aug}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}
w_i\bar f_{i,t}(\btheta),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:parego_target}
\end{equation}
In Eq.~\eqref{eq:parego_target}, $\tilde z_{i,t}^{\ast}$ is the transformed
ideal coordinate, $\rho_{\mathrm{aug}}>0$ is the augmentation coefficient, and
$\bw\geq\mathbf0$ with $\mathbf1^\top\bw=1$.
The weight set is initialized on a Das--Dennis simplex lattice and dispersed
by projected Riesz-energy relaxation~\cite{ref15}; weights are visited without
replacement before reshuffling.

Standardized scalar targets are modeled by an ARD Mat\'ern-$5/2$ GP with a
white-noise term on normalized decision variables.
Kernel parameters are estimated by marginal likelihood with multiple restarts.
The fitted posterior mean $\mu_t$, standard deviation $\sigma_t$, and
covariance are the only statistical quantities used by the acquisition
function.

\subsubsection{Weight-Conditioned LLM Region Preference}
\label{sec:region_pref}

The second LLM touchpoint operates during an early guidance window of
$T_{\mathrm L}$ iterations.
After the initial portfolio has been evaluated and the GP surrogate has
accumulated some task-specific evidence, the LLM is queried with richer context
to elicit weight-conditioned search preferences.

The prompt at iteration $t\leq T_{\mathrm L}$ includes the current
scalarization weight $\bw^{(t)}$, the per-objective observed ranges and current
best values, a selection of recently evaluated protocols with their objective
values, and a summary of optimization progress.
The LLM response schema permits three outcomes: a raw-coordinate point, a
hyperrectangular region defined by lower and upper bounds, or an explicit
declaration of no preference.
Providing a schema with a no-preference option is deliberate: when the LLM
does not identify an informative region for a particular weight configuration,
the optimizer retains unmodified EI.

A received point or region is processed through the same repair-and-screen
pipeline as the warm-start candidates.
Coordinates are parsed, clipped to variable bounds, repaired to
$\Theta_{\mathrm{proto}}$, and validated.
A point preference is converted to a local hyperrectangle by adding a
fixed-width margin in each coordinate dimension.
All accepted proposals are then subjected to width and volume checks: a region
whose span in any dimension falls below a minimum width, or whose normalized
volume exceeds a maximum threshold, is rejected.
These checks prevent degenerate (single-point) and vacuous (full-space) regions
from entering the acquisition stage.

Anchors are drawn uniformly from each accepted box and serve two independent
roles.
First, they act as external restarts for numerical EI maximization, providing
alternative starting points to the standard multi-start L-BFGS-B procedure.
Second, they may enter the bounded posterior-covariance coupling described in
Section~\ref{sec:bounded_coupling}.
These roles are separable: accepting a region does not force a GP-mean
adjustment, and disabling the coupling leaves the restart-augmented EI search
well defined.

The finite horizon concentrates contextual guidance in the data-scarce phase.
After $T_{\mathrm L}$ iterations, the optimizer continues as ordinary ParEGO
and relies on the accumulated simulator evidence.

\subsubsection{Bounded Posterior-Covariance Preference Coupling}
\label{sec:bounded_coupling}

When an accepted region passes all validation checks, its anchors may be used
to construct a bounded adjustment to the acquisition mean.
The design objective is to make regions preferred by the LLM marginally more
attractive to EI, without ever letting the adjustment magnitude exceed a fixed
fraction of the posterior standard deviation.
The coupling proceeds in three stages: anchor scoring, covariance projection,
and gated attenuation.

\textbf{Anchor scoring.}
Let $\bG_t=\{\mathbf g_{j,t}\}_{j=1}^{M}$ be the accepted anchors drawn from
validated regions.
Each anchor is scored by a weighted combination of its EI value, posterior
uncertainty, and novelty relative to the evaluated design.
For compactness, let $\mathrm{EI}_{j,t}=\mathrm{EI}_t(\mathbf g_{j,t})$ and
$\sigma_{j,t}=\sigma_t(\mathbf g_{j,t})$.
With $z(\cdot)$ denoting a clipped across-anchor standardization,
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{EI}}>0$ a numerical floor, and $T_v>0$ the softmax
temperature,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
n_{j,t}
&=\min_{\mathbf x\in\bD_t}
\|\widehat{\mathbf g}_{j,t}-\widehat{\mathbf x}\|_2,\\
s_{j,t}
&=\eta_{\mathrm{EI}}z\!\left(
\log\max\{\mathrm{EI}_{j,t},\varepsilon_{\mathrm{EI}}\}\right)
\\[-2pt]
&\quad+\eta_\sigma z(\sigma_{j,t})+\eta_n z(n_{j,t}),\\
v_{j,t}
&=\frac{\exp(s_{j,t}/T_v)}
{\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}\exp(s_{\ell,t}/T_v)} .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:anchor_weights}
\end{equation}
If anchor scoring is numerically unavailable, the implementation uses uniform
weights $v_{j,t}=1/M$.
Thus, with $\mathbf v_t=[v_{1,t},\ldots,v_{M,t}]^\top$, the normalized weights
in Eq.~\eqref{eq:anchor_weights} satisfy
$\mathbf v_t\geq\mathbf0$ and $\mathbf1^\top\mathbf v_t=1$.

\textbf{Covariance projection.}
Let $\boldsymbol\Sigma_{GG,t}$ be the posterior anchor covariance and
$\boldsymbol\Sigma_{\theta G,t}$ the candidate-to-anchor covariance.
With positive covariance jitter $\varepsilon_K$ and variance floor
$\varepsilon_v$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
V_{G,t}
&=\mathbf v_t^\top
(\boldsymbol\Sigma_{GG,t}+\varepsilon_K\mathbf I)\mathbf v_t,\\
\rho_t(\btheta)
&=\frac{\boldsymbol\Sigma_{\theta G,t}\mathbf v_t}
{\sigma_t(\btheta)\sqrt{\max\{V_{G,t},\varepsilon_v\}}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:covariance_projection}
\end{equation}
The scalar $\rho_t(\btheta)$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:covariance_projection} lies in
$[-1,1]$ and is the posterior correlation between the
candidate point and the weighted anchor portfolio.
A positive value indicates that the candidate lies in a region positively
correlated with the LLM-preferred anchors; only positive correlations are used
for the mean adjustment.

\textbf{Gated attenuation.}
Three independent factors gate the adjustment magnitude.
The region-quality gate combines anchor concentration and score dispersion:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
H_t&=-\sum_{j=1}^{M}v_{j,t}\log(v_{j,t}+\varepsilon_H),\\
\bar H_t&=\frac{H_t}{\max\{\log M,\varepsilon_H\}},\\
q_t&=\operatorname{clip}\!\left[
\begin{aligned}
&\eta_H(1-\bar H_t)\\[-2pt]
&\quad +(1-\eta_H)\tanh\!\bigl(\operatorname{std}(\mathbf s_t)\bigr)
\end{aligned}
,\,0,\,1\right].
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:quality_gate}
\end{equation}
The accepted guidance includes an LLM-reported confidence
$h_t^{\mathrm{LLM}}\in[0,1]$, from which
$c_t=\operatorname{clip}(h_t^{\mathrm{LLM}},0,1)$,
and the optimizer-maintained trust state $\tau_t\in[0,1]$ (updated from
observed optimization outcomes) complete the gate product
$r_t=c_t\tau_tq_t$, where $q_t$ is defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:quality_gate},
$\mathbf s_t=[s_{1,t},\ldots,s_{M,t}]^\top$, $\varepsilon_H>0$, and
$\eta_H\in[0,1]$.
The early-window attenuation $a_t=\operatorname{clip}(1-(t-1)/T_{\mathrm L},
0,1)$ linearly ramps the influence to zero by the end of the guidance window.
The acquisition-time adjustment is then
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\delta_t(\btheta)
&=\operatorname{clip}\!\left(
a_t\lambda_{\max}r_t[\rho_t(\btheta)]_+,0,\delta_{\max}
\right),\\
\mu_t^{\mathrm L}(\btheta)
&=\mu_t(\btheta)-\delta_t(\btheta),\\
(\sigma_t^{\mathrm L})^2(\btheta)
&=\sigma_t^2(\btheta).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:bounded_coupling}
\end{equation}

Because the scalar Tchebycheff target in Eq.~\eqref{eq:parego_target} is
minimized, reducing the GP mean near
positively correlated anchors makes those regions more attractive to EI.
In Eq.~\eqref{eq:bounded_coupling}, $[x]_+=\max\{x,0\}$,
$\lambda_{\max}$ sets the maximum coupling strength, and $\delta_{\max}$ is the
pointwise shift cap. The explicit clipping guarantees
$0\leq\delta_t(\btheta)\leq\delta_{\max}$ in standardized target units, which
places an explicit pointwise bound on the regional-guidance contribution.
The operation preserves three critical invariants: it does not alter fitted GP
targets, it does not modify posterior uncertainty, and it does not create
pseudo-observations.
If any gate is zero ($q_t=0$, $c_t=0$, or $\tau_t=0$), then $r_t=0$ and the
acquisition function reduces exactly to standard EI.
Section~\ref{sec:experiments} reports the fixed numerical settings used to
instantiate these operators in all comparisons.

\subsection{Complete Optimization Procedure}
\label{sec:complete_proc}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:llmbo} summarizes the complete procedure. Accepted guidance
modifies only the acquisition stage; otherwise the method recovers the
standard GP mean and ordinary EI. Every selected candidate is repaired to the
protocol domain and its operational feasibility is determined by simulation.

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{LLMBO-MO}
\label{alg:llmbo}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Simulator $\bS$, LLM $\bL$, BO budget $T$, weights $\bW$,
guidance horizon $T_{\mathrm L}$
\Ensure Evaluated nondominated set $\bP$
\State Query $\bL$ for a warm-start pool; repair and rank by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:portfolio}
\State Add selected LLM proposals and random protocol-domain-valid points
\State Evaluate the initial portfolio and initialize $\bD$
\For{$t=1$ to $T$}
    \State Select $\bw^{(t)}$; transform and re-scalarize feasible data
    \State Fit the GP to $g_t(\cdot\mid\bw^{(t)})$
    \If{$t\leq T_{\mathrm L}$}
        \State Query $\bL$ for a point/region and validate its anchors
        \State Add accepted anchors as restarts; attempt
        Eq.~\eqref{eq:bounded_coupling}
    \EndIf
    \State Maximize EI using $\mu_t^{\mathrm A}$ and select $\btheta_{t+1}$
    \State Evaluate $\bS(\btheta_{t+1})$; update $\bD$ and feasible set $\bP$
\EndFor
\State \Return $\bP$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
