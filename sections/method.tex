\section{Proposed LLMBO-MO}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Framework}
\label{sec:framework}

The battery model in Section~\ref{sec:model_section} makes it possible to
evaluate a charging protocol, but each evaluation requires an
electrochemical--thermal--aging simulation. The optimization problem is
therefore governed by a limited simulation budget. Bayesian optimization (BO)
is suitable for this setting because it uses previously evaluated protocols to
guide subsequent sampling. Its main difficulty appears at the beginning of the
search, when only a few task-specific observations are available and the
surrogate is strongly affected by the initial design.

LLMBO-MO uses battery knowledge expressed in natural language to improve this
early stage while retaining a numerical multiobjective BO procedure as the
optimizer. The LLM is used in two places. Before sequential BO, it proposes
candidate protocols for the initial design. After the initial database has
been formed, it can provide a temporary preference over the decision space
according to the current multiobjective trade-off. In both cases, the LLM
affects only where the optimizer searches. Objective values, feasibility,
surrogate fitting, and Pareto updates are always determined from
simulator-evaluated protocols.

The overall procedure is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}. An initial database
$D$ is first constructed from a mixture of screened LLM proposals and random
valid protocols. During iteration $t$, a trade-off weight $\mathbf w^{(t)}$
defines the current scalar subproblem. A Gaussian process (GP) is fitted to
the scalarized observations, and expected improvement (EI) is optimized to
select the next protocol. During the first $T_{\mathrm L}$ iterations, an
accepted LLM region can modify the acquisition ranking through the posterior
covariance of the fitted GP. The selected protocol is then evaluated by the
battery model and appended to $D$, after which the feasible Pareto archive
$P$ is updated. This loop continues until the simulation budget is exhausted.

\subsection{Decomposition-Based Multiobjective BO}
\label{sec:parego_backbone}

The numerical backbone follows a decomposition-based multiobjective BO
strategy~\cite{ref4}. A set of nonnegative weight vectors $W$ is
generated in advance to represent different trade-offs among charging time,
temperature rise, and aging. At iteration $t$, one vector
$\mathbf w^{(t)}=[w_1^{(t)},w_2^{(t)},w_3^{(t)}]$ is selected from $W$, where
$\sum_{i=1}^{3}w_i^{(t)}=1$.

Because the three objectives have different numerical ranges, charging time
and aging are first transformed,
\begin{equation}
y_1(\theta)=\log\!\frac{t_c(\theta)}{t_{\mathrm{ref}}}, 
y_2(\theta)=T_p(\theta),
y_3(\theta)=\log\!\frac{Q_s(\theta)}{Q_{\mathrm{ref}}}
\label{eq:objective_transform}
\end{equation}
where $t_{\mathrm{ref}}$ and $Q_{\mathrm{ref}}$ are fixed positive reference
values. A small positive floor is used before taking the logarithm. For each
objective, the scaling factor $s_{i,t}$ is taken from a prescribed global
range during the first few iterations and is replaced by the observed range
once sufficient variation has been seen. This avoids over-amplifying an
artificially narrow range at the start of the optimization.

The normalized distance from the current best observation is
\begin{equation}
\bar f_{i,t}(\theta)=\frac{|y_i(\theta)-z_{i,t}^{*}|}{s_{i,t}}\qquad i=1,2,3
\label{eq:objective_scaling}
\end{equation}
where $z_{i,t}^{*}$ is the best observed value of the $i$th transformed
objective. The three objectives are then combined using the augmented
Tchebycheff function,
\begin{equation}
g_t(\theta)=\max_{i=1,2,3}\!\left\{w_i^{(t)}\bar f_{i,t}(\theta)\right\}
+\rho\sum_{i=1}^{3}w_i^{(t)}\bar f_{i,t}(\theta)
\label{eq:tchebycheff}
\end{equation}
where $\rho>0$ is a small augmentation coefficient. Changing
$\mathbf w^{(t)}$ across iterations therefore directs the search toward
different parts of the Pareto front.

An ARD Mat\'ern-$5/2$ GP is fitted to the scalarized observations. Let
$\mu_t(\theta)$ and $\sigma_t(\theta)$ denote its posterior mean and standard
deviation. For the minimization problem, EI is written as
\begin{align}
    \mathrm{EI}_t(\theta) &= \bigl(g_t^{\mathrm{best}}-\mu_t(\theta)\bigr)\Phi(u_t)
        +\sigma_t(\theta)\phi(u_t) \\
    u_t &= \frac{g_t^{\mathrm{best}}-\mu_t(\theta)}{\sigma_t(\theta)}
    \label{eq:ei}
\end{align}
where $g_t^{\mathrm{best}}$ is the best scalarized value among the evaluated
protocols, and $\Phi(\cdot)$ and $\phi(\cdot)$ are the standard normal
cumulative distribution and density functions. Maximizing
$\mathrm{EI}_t(\theta)$ gives the next protocol to be evaluated.
\subsection{LLM-Informed Initialization}
\label{sec:warm_start}

Before the first GP is available, BO must rely on a small initial set of
evaluated protocols. Random initialization provides broad coverage but does
not use readily available knowledge about multistage charging. We therefore
use the LLM to convert this qualitative knowledge into candidate protocols.
The prompt specifies the battery context, MCC structure, decision-variable
bounds, optimization objectives, and charging-related prior knowledge. The LLM
returns a pool of numerical candidates together with a heuristic score used
only for ranking.

The generated protocols are checked before any battery simulation is carried
out. Non-numerical, non-finite, and duplicate outputs are removed; values
outside the decision bounds are repaired; and protocols that cannot complete
the prescribed SOC interval are rejected. These operations only verify the
protocol representation. Voltage and temperature constraints are still
determined by the battery model.

The remaining candidates are selected using
\begin{equation}
J(\theta)=h(\theta)-\omega_p p(\theta)+\omega_m m(\theta)+\omega_d d(\theta)
\label{eq:portfolio}
\end{equation}
where $h(\theta)$ is the LLM ranking score, $p(\theta)$ penalizes an
undesirably small terminal SOC span, $m(\theta)$ represents the preferred
current ordering, and $d(\theta)$ measures diversity with respect to protocols
already selected for the initial set. Random valid protocols are added to the
selected LLM proposals so that the initial design does not depend entirely on
the language prior.

All initial protocols are then evaluated by the battery model. Only these
simulator-evaluated records are added to $D$ and used for GP fitting and
Pareto updating. Thus, the initialization mechanism changes the locations of
the first evaluations without introducing LLM-generated objective values.

\subsection{Posterior-Covariance Region Lift}
\label{sec:region_pref}

The initialization mechanism is used before sequential BO begins. Once the
initial database has been formed, the LLM can also use the current optimization
state to provide a short-lived regional preference. At iteration $t$, the
prompt contains the active trade-off weight $\mathbf w^{(t)}$, the observed
objective ranges, current best values, and representative evaluated protocols.
The weight vector is included because the preferred region should change with
the scalar subproblem currently being optimized.

The LLM may return a point, a bounded region, or no preference. A returned
point is expanded to a local region, while a returned region is clipped to the
valid decision space. Invalid, excessively small, or excessively broad regions
are ignored. The reported confidence $\widehat c_t$ is used only as a
guidance-strength multiplier rather than as a calibrated probability; if it
falls below a prescribed threshold, standard EI is used without modification.

An accepted region is represented by $M$ Sobol anchors
$\{\mathbf g_{1,t},\ldots,\mathbf g_{M,t}\}$ with equal weights
$v_{j,t}=1/M$. These anchors are not evaluated by the battery model. Instead,
the fitted GP determines how strongly an arbitrary candidate is related to the
preferred region. Let $\widetilde{\boldsymbol\Sigma}_{GG,t}$ denote the
posterior covariance among the anchors and
$\widetilde{\boldsymbol\Sigma}_{\theta G,t}$ the posterior cross-covariance
between a candidate $\theta$ and the anchor set, both in the standardized
scalar-GP space. The posterior variance of the weighted regional aggregate is
\begin{equation}
V_{G,t}=
\mathbf v_t^{\top}
\widetilde{\boldsymbol\Sigma}_{GG,t}
\mathbf v_t
\label{eq:region_variance}
\end{equation}
If $V_{G,t}$ is numerically negligible, the regional preference is discarded.

Using the GP posterior cross-covariance to propagate the regional preference
\cite{ref5}, the raw regional mean shift is defined by
\begin{equation}
\delta_t^{(0)}(\theta)=
\alpha_c\widehat c_t a_t\,
\frac{\widetilde{\boldsymbol\Sigma}_{\theta G,t}\mathbf v_t}
{\sqrt{V_{G,t}}},\quad
a_t=\max\!\left(1-\frac{t-1}{T_{\mathrm L}},0\right)
\label{eq:region_raw_shift}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_c$ calibrates the strength of the regional preference and
$a_t$ linearly decreases its influence during the guidance window and becomes
zero for $t>T_{\mathrm L}$. The covariance term is signed: candidates
positively correlated with the preferred region receive a favorable shift,
whereas negatively correlated candidates are discouraged.

To prevent the regional preference from dominating the data-driven model, the
raw shift is rescaled by
\begin{equation}
\kappa_t=
\min\!\left\{
1,
\frac{B_{\mathrm{mean}}}{\bar\delta_t+\epsilon_\delta},
\frac{B_{\max}}{\delta_t^{\max}+\epsilon_\delta}
\right\}
\label{eq:region_budget_scale}
\end{equation}
where $\bar\delta_t$ and $\delta_t^{\max}$ are, respectively, the mean and
maximum absolute raw shifts evaluated at the anchors, and
$\epsilon_\delta>0$ is a small numerical constant used to avoid division by
zero. The bounded shift is
$\delta_t(\theta)=
\operatorname{clip}(\kappa_t\delta_t^{(0)}(\theta),
-B_{\max},B_{\max})$.

The shift is used only when the acquisition function is evaluated. If
$s_{g,t}$ denotes the standardization scale of the scalar GP target, the
acquisition-adjusted mean is
\begin{equation}
\mu_t^{\mathrm L}(\theta)
=
\mu_t(\theta)-s_{g,t}\delta_t(\theta),\quad
\sigma_t^{\mathrm L}(\theta)
=
\sigma_t(\theta)
\label{eq:region_lifted_posterior}
\end{equation}
EI is then evaluated using $\mu_t^{\mathrm L}$ in place of $\mu_t$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ei}. The adjustment is used only for acquisition ranking: the
GP itself is not refitted, no pseudo-observations are introduced, and the
posterior variance is unchanged. The full decision space also remains
available to the acquisition optimizer. If the LLM abstains, the region fails
the admissibility checks, its confidence is too low, or $V_{G,t}$ is
degenerate, then $\delta_t(\theta)=0$ and the method reduces to standard EI.
Standard EI is also used after $t>T_{\mathrm L}$.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:llmbo} summarizes the complete procedure.

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{LLMBO-MO}
\label{alg:llmbo}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Battery simulator, LLM, total budget $N$, initial budget $n_0$, weight set $W$, guidance horizon $T_{\mathrm L}$
\Ensure Feasible Pareto archive $P$
\State Generate, screen, and rank LLM candidate protocols
\State Combine selected LLM proposals with random valid protocols
\State Evaluate the $n_0$ initial protocols; initialize $D$ and $P$
\For{$t=1$ to $N-n_0$}
    \State Select $\mathbf w^{(t)}\in W$ and compute $g_t$ using Eq.~\eqref{eq:tchebycheff}
    \State Fit the GP and construct standard EI using Eq.~\eqref{eq:ei}
    \If{$t\le T_{\mathrm L}$}
        \State Query the LLM with $\mathbf w^{(t)}$ and the current BO state
        \If{an admissible region is returned}
            \State Generate regional anchors and compute $\mu_t^{\mathrm L}$ using Eqs.~\eqref{eq:region_variance}--\eqref{eq:region_lifted_posterior}
        \EndIf
    \EndIf
    \State Maximize the active EI over the full decision space and select $\theta_{t+1}$
    \State Evaluate $\theta_{t+1}$ with the battery model and append the result to $D$
    \State Update the feasible Pareto archive $P$
\EndFor
\State \Return $P$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
