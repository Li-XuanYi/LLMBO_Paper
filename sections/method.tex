\section{Proposed LLMBO-MO}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Framework}
\label{sec:framework}

As illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}, LLMBO-MO couples two forms of
LLM guidance with a decomposition-based multiobjective Bayesian optimization
(BO) loop. Before sequential optimization, the LLM proposes charging protocols
for an informed initial design. During the early BO iterations, it can
additionally suggest a region according to the current objective trade-off.
The numerical BO module scalarizes the three objectives, fits a Gaussian
process (GP) surrogate, and selects the next protocol through expected
improvement (EI). Each selected protocol is then evaluated by the battery
simulator and used to update the data set $D$ and the feasible Pareto archive
$P$.

The LLM therefore provides search guidance at two stages, while numerical
objective values and feasibility are obtained from battery-model evaluations.
This loop continues until the simulation budget is exhausted.

\subsection{Decomposition-Based Multiobjective BO}
\label{sec:parego_backbone}

The numerical backbone follows a decomposition-based multiobjective BO
strategy~\cite{ref4}. A set of nonnegative weight vectors $W$ represents
different trade-offs among charging time, temperature rise, and aging. At
iteration $t$, one vector
$\mathbf w^{(t)}=[w_1^{(t)},w_2^{(t)},w_3^{(t)}]$ is selected from $W$, with
$\sum_{i=1}^{3}w_i^{(t)}=1$.

Because the three objectives have different numerical ranges, charging time
and aging are first transformed as
\begin{equation}
 y_1(\theta)=\log\!\frac{t_c(\theta)}{t_{\mathrm{ref}}},
 y_2(\theta)=T_p(\theta),
 y_3(\theta)=\log\!\frac{Q_s(\theta)}{Q_{\mathrm{ref}}}
 \label{eq:objective_transform}
\end{equation}
where $t_{\mathrm{ref}}$ and $Q_{\mathrm{ref}}$ are fixed positive reference
values. A small positive floor is applied before taking the logarithm. Each
objective is further scaled by $s_{i,t}$ to avoid one objective dominating the
scalarization because of its numerical range.

Let $z_{i,t}^{*}$ denote the best observed value of the $i$th transformed
objective. The normalized distance is
\begin{equation}
 \bar f_{i,t}(\theta)=
 \frac{|y_i(\theta)-z_{i,t}^{*}|}{s_{i,t}},\qquad i=1,2,3.
 \label{eq:objective_scaling}
\end{equation}
The objectives are combined using the augmented Tchebycheff function
\begin{equation}
 g_t(\theta)=
 \max_{i=1,2,3}\!\left\{w_i^{(t)}\bar f_{i,t}(\theta)\right\}
 +\rho_{\mathrm{aug}}\sum_{i=1}^{3}w_i^{(t)}\bar f_{i,t}(\theta)
 \label{eq:tchebycheff}
\end{equation}
where $\rho_{\mathrm{aug}}>0$ is a small augmentation coefficient. Varying
$\mathbf w^{(t)}$ across iterations directs the search toward different parts
of the Pareto front.

An ARD Mat\'ern-$5/2$ GP is fitted to the scalarized observations. Let
$\mu_t(\theta)$ and $\sigma_t(\theta)$ denote its posterior mean and standard
deviation. For minimization, EI is
\begin{align}
 \mathrm{EI}_t(\theta)
 &=\bigl(g_t^{\mathrm{best}}-\mu_t(\theta)\bigr)\Phi(u_t)
 +\sigma_t(\theta)\phi(u_t)\\
 u_t&=\frac{g_t^{\mathrm{best}}-\mu_t(\theta)}{\sigma_t(\theta)}
 \label{eq:ei}
\end{align}
where $g_t^{\mathrm{best}}$ is the best scalarized value among the evaluated
protocols, and $\Phi(\cdot)$ and $\phi(\cdot)$ are the standard normal
cumulative distribution and density functions.

\subsection{LLM-Informed Initialization}
\label{sec:warm_start}

Before the first GP is available, BO relies on a small set of evaluated
protocols. Random initialization provides broad coverage but does not exploit
readily available knowledge about multistage charging. We therefore prompt the
LLM with the battery context, MCC protocol structure, decision-variable bounds,
optimization objectives, and charging-related domain knowledge. The LLM
returns a pool of numerical candidate protocols together with a preference
ranking.

The generated candidates are screened before simulation. Non-numerical,
non-finite, duplicate, or invalid protocol representations are removed, and
out-of-bound values are repaired. A subset of highly ranked candidates is then
selected while maintaining diversity in the decision space. Random valid
protocols are also included to preserve broader coverage. The resulting
protocols form the initial simulation design and are evaluated by the battery
model before entering the BO data set.

\subsection{Region-Lift Guidance}
\label{sec:region_pref}

Initialization uses LLM knowledge only before sequential BO begins. To retain
such information during the early search, the LLM is additionally queried with
the current scalarization weight $\mathbf w^{(t)}$ and the observed optimization
state. It returns a preferred region $R_t$ in the decision space for the
current trade-off. Because directly restricting the search to $R_t$ would
ignore the correlation structure learned by the GP, Region-Lift instead uses
GP posterior covariance to convert this regional preference into a soft
adjustment of the EI ranking. This design follows the region-lifted preference
idea of LGBO~\cite{ref5}, adapted here to the decomposition-based
multiobjective setting.

A valid region is represented by $M$ Sobol anchors
$G_t=\{\mathbf g_{1,t},\ldots,\mathbf g_{M,t}\}$ with equal weights
$\mathbf v_t=(1/M)\mathbf 1$. Let
$\widetilde{\boldsymbol\Sigma}_{GG,t}$ denote the GP posterior covariance among
these anchors and $\widetilde{\boldsymbol\Sigma}_{\theta G,t}$ the posterior
cross-covariance between a candidate $\theta$ and the anchor set. The posterior
variance associated with the region is
\begin{equation}
 V_{G,t}=\mathbf v_t^{\top}
 \widetilde{\boldsymbol\Sigma}_{GG,t}\mathbf v_t
 \label{eq:region_variance}
\end{equation}
The cross-covariance term then propagates the regional preference from the
anchors to other candidates. We define the covariance-based adjustment as
\begin{equation}
 r_t(\theta)=
 \lambda_t\,
 \frac{\widetilde{\boldsymbol\Sigma}_{\theta G,t}\mathbf v_t}
 {\sqrt{V_{G,t}}},
 \quad
 \lambda_t\propto
 \widehat c_t\max\!\left(1-\frac{t-1}{T_{\mathrm L}},0\right)
 \label{eq:region_adjustment}
\end{equation}
where $\widehat c_t$ is the LLM-reported guidance score and $T_{\mathrm L}$ is
the early guidance horizon. The fixed implementation scale is absorbed into
$\lambda_t$. Positive posterior correlation with the preferred region favors a
candidate, whereas negative correlation has the opposite effect.

In implementation, $r_t(\theta)$ is rescaled according to its magnitude over
the regional anchors and clipped to a fixed bound, yielding a bounded
adjustment $\Delta_t(\theta)$. If $s_{g,t}$ denotes the scale used for the
scalar GP target, EI is evaluated with
\begin{equation}
 \mu_t^{\mathrm{acq}}(\theta)
 =\mu_t(\theta)-s_{g,t}\Delta_t(\theta),
 \quad
 \sigma_t^{\mathrm{acq}}(\theta)=\sigma_t(\theta)
 \label{eq:region_acq_mean}
\end{equation}
Thus, Region-Lift changes the acquisition ranking without changing the fitted
GP. When no valid regional preference is available, or after the early guidance
window, the method uses standard EI.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:llmbo} summarizes the complete procedure.

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{LLMBO-MO}
\label{alg:llmbo}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Battery simulator, LLM, total budget $N$, initial budget $n_0$, weight set $W$, guidance horizon $T_{\mathrm L}$
\Ensure Feasible Pareto archive $P$
\State Generate and screen LLM-informed initial protocols
\State Combine selected LLM proposals with random valid protocols
\State Evaluate the $n_0$ initial protocols; initialize $D$ and $P$
\For{$t=1$ to $N-n_0$}
    \State Select $\mathbf w^{(t)}\in W$ and compute $g_t$ using Eq.~\eqref{eq:tchebycheff}
    \State Fit the scalar GP and construct EI
    \If{$t\le T_{\mathrm L}$}
        \State Query the LLM for a preferred region under $\mathbf w^{(t)}$
        \If{a valid region is returned}
            \State Apply Region-Lift using Eqs.~\eqref{eq:region_variance}--\eqref{eq:region_acq_mean}
        \EndIf
    \EndIf
    \State Maximize EI and select the next protocol $\theta_{t+1}$
    \State Evaluate $\theta_{t+1}$ and append the result to $D$
    \State Update the feasible Pareto archive $P$
\EndFor
\State \Return $P$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
