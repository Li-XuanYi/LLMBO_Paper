\section{LLM-Augmented Multiobjective Bayesian Optimization}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Design Principle and Overall Workflow}

LLMBO-MO treats large-language-model (LLM) output as an advisory preference,
not as an objective observation. The LLM is used at two controlled
touchpoints: it first proposes candidates for a diverse initial portfolio and
then, during an early optimization window, expresses a weight-conditioned
preference for a point or region. Protocol-domain repair, simulator evaluation,
surrogate fitting, and acquisition maximization remain numerical operations.
Consequently, unevaluated LLM output never becomes a Gaussian-process (GP)
target.

Figure~\ref{fig:framework} shows the two advisory paths and the common
multiobjective Bayesian-optimization (BO) backbone. At iteration $t$, a
trade-off weight $\bw^{(t)}$ defines a scalar subproblem. Feasible historical
observations are transformed and re-scalarized, the GP is fitted, and expected
improvement (EI) selects the next simulator evaluation. If an LLM response
cannot be parsed, repaired, or validated, its numerical influence is exactly
zero and the procedure uses ordinary EI.

\subsection{Protocol-Domain-Screened LLM Warm Start}

The warm-start prompt supplies the cell-model context, variable bounds,
protocol geometry, and battery-inspired scheduling heuristics. The interface
repairs numerical formatting, rejects nonfinite and duplicate entries, clips
box violations, and removes candidates that cannot satisfy the dependent
terminal SOC span. These checks establish protocol-domain validity; operational
voltage and temperature feasibility is determined only by simulation.

Let $h_{\mathrm{ws}}(\btheta)\in[0,1]$ denote the LLM-reported candidate score,
which is used only for ranking. A soft penalty discourages a terminal SOC span
below the preferred boundary,
\[
p_{\mathrm{soft}}(\btheta)=
\operatorname{clip}\!\left(
\frac{\Delta s_K^{\mathrm{soft}}-\Delta s_K}{w_{\mathrm{soft}}},0,1
\right).
\]
Let $\mathbb I_{\mathrm{mono}}(\btheta)$ indicate a nonincreasing current
sequence. The selected portfolio is built greedily using
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
J_{\mathrm{ws}}(\btheta)
={}&h_{\mathrm{ws}}(\btheta)
-\omega_p p_{\mathrm{soft}}(\btheta)
+\omega_m\mathbb I_{\mathrm{mono}}(\btheta)\\
&+\omega_d d_{\min}(\btheta,\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sel}}),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:portfolio}
\end{equation}
where normalized decision coordinates are denoted by a hat and
\[
d_{\min}(\btheta,\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sel}})=
\begin{cases}
0, & \mathcal C_{\mathrm{sel}}=\varnothing,\\
\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf c\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sel}}}
\|\widehat{\btheta}-\widehat{\mathbf c}\|_2,
& \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
The first selected candidate therefore receives no artificial diversity
bonus. Random protocol-domain-valid points complete the initial design, and
only simulator-evaluated candidates are appended to the optimization database
$\bD$.

\subsection{Objective Transformation and ParEGO Backbone}

Charging time and the degradation proxy are represented logarithmically, while
temperature rise remains on its physical scale:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\tilde{\mathbf f}(\btheta)&=
[\ell_t(\btheta),\,\Delta T_p(\btheta),\,\ell_D(\btheta)]^\top,\\
\ell_t(\btheta)&=\log_{10}\!\max(t_c/t_{\mathrm{ref}},\varepsilon_t),\\
\ell_D(\btheta)&=\log_{10}\!\max(D_{\mathrm{chg}}/D_{\mathrm{ref}},
\varepsilon_D).
\end{aligned}
\]
The same transformation is applied to LLMBO-MO and ParEGO. For objective
$i$, let $R_{i,t}^{\mathrm{hist}}$ be the current observed range and define the
global transformed range by
$R_i^{\mathrm{glob}}=\tilde y_i^{\mathrm{ref}}
-\tilde y_i^{\mathrm{ideal}}$, where the ideal and reference coordinates are
mapped through the same objective transformation. The scale used by the
implementation is
\[
s_{i,t}=
\begin{cases}
R_i^{\mathrm{glob}},&
R_{i,t}^{\mathrm{hist}}<\rho_R R_i^{\mathrm{glob}},\\
R_{i,t}^{\mathrm{hist}},&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
This fallback uses the full global range when the early observed range is too
narrow.

Following ParEGO~\cite{ref4}, the three objectives ($m=3$) are combined through
an augmented Tchebycheff target,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\bar f_{i,t}(\btheta)
&=\frac{|\tilde f_i(\btheta)-\tilde z_{i,t}^{\ast}|}{s_{i,t}},\\
g_t(\btheta\mid\bw)
&=\max_i w_i\bar f_{i,t}(\btheta)\\
&\quad+\rho_{\mathrm{aug}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}
w_i\bar f_{i,t}(\btheta),
\end{aligned}
\]
with $\bw\geq\mathbf0$ and $\mathbf1^\top\bw=1$. The weight set is initialized
on a Das--Dennis simplex lattice and dispersed by projected Riesz-energy
relaxation~\cite{ref15}; weights are visited without replacement before
reshuffling.

Standardized scalar targets are modeled by an ARD Mat\'ern-$5/2$ GP with a
white-noise term on normalized decision variables. Kernel parameters are
estimated by marginal likelihood with multiple restarts. The fitted posterior
mean $\mu_t$, standard deviation $\sigma_t$, and covariance are the only
statistical quantities used by the acquisition function.

\subsection{Weight-Conditioned LLM Region Preference}

During the guidance horizon, the LLM receives $\bw^{(t)}$, current objective
scales, selected evaluated protocols, and recent optimization feedback. Its
schema permits a raw-coordinate point, a hyperrectangular region, or no
preference. The response is parsed, clipped to the variable bounds, repaired
to $\Theta_{\mathrm{proto}}$, and restricted by width and volume checks. A
point preference is converted to a local box before the same validation.

Anchors drawn from an accepted box first act as external restarts for numerical
EI maximization. They may additionally enter the bounded coupling below.
These two roles are distinct: accepting a region does not force a GP-mean
adjustment, and rejecting the coupling leaves the restart-augmented EI search
well defined.

\subsection{Bounded Posterior-Covariance Preference Coupling}

Let $\bG_t=\{\mathbf g_{j,t}\}_{j=1}^{M}$ be the accepted anchors. Each anchor
is scored by its EI, posterior uncertainty, and novelty relative to the
evaluated design. With $z(\cdot)$ denoting a clipped across-anchor
standardization,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
n_{j,t}
&=\min_{\mathbf x\in\bD_t}
\|\widehat{\mathbf g}_{j,t}-\widehat{\mathbf x}\|_2,\\
s_{j,t}
&=\eta_{\mathrm{EI}}z\!\left(
\log\max\{\mathrm{EI}_{j,t},\varepsilon_{\mathrm{EI}}\}\right)
+\eta_\sigma z(\sigma_{j,t})+\eta_n z(n_{j,t}),\\
v_{j,t}
&=\frac{\exp(s_{j,t}/T_v)}
{\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}\exp(s_{\ell,t}/T_v)} .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:anchor_weights}
\end{equation}
If anchor scoring is numerically unavailable, the implementation uses
$v_{j,t}=1/M$. Thus $\mathbf v_t\geq\mathbf0$ and
$\mathbf1^\top\mathbf v_t=1$.

Let $\boldsymbol\Sigma_{GG,t}$ be the posterior anchor covariance and
$\boldsymbol\Sigma_{\theta G,t}$ the candidate-to-anchor covariance. Covariance
jitter $\varepsilon_K$ and variance floor $\varepsilon_v$ define
\[
\begin{aligned}
V_{G,t}
&=\mathbf v_t^\top
(\boldsymbol\Sigma_{GG,t}+\varepsilon_K\mathbf I)\mathbf v_t,\\
\rho_t(\btheta)
&=\frac{\boldsymbol\Sigma_{\theta G,t}\mathbf v_t}
{\sigma_t(\btheta)\sqrt{\max\{V_{G,t},\varepsilon_v\}}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The region-quality gate combines anchor concentration and score dispersion:
\[
\begin{aligned}
H_t&=-\sum_{j=1}^{M}v_{j,t}\log(v_{j,t}+\varepsilon_H),\\
\bar H_t&=\frac{H_t}{\max\{\log M,\varepsilon_H\}},\\
q_t&=\operatorname{clip}\!\left[
\eta_H(1-\bar H_t)\right.\\
&\hspace{3em}\left.
+(1-\eta_H)\tanh\!\bigl(\operatorname{std}(\mathbf s_t)\bigr),
\,0,\,1\right].
\end{aligned}
\]
Here $c_t=\operatorname{clip}(h_t^{\mathrm{LLM}},0,1)$ maps the reported LLM
confidence to the unit interval, and $\tau_t\in[0,1]$ is the
optimizer-maintained trust state, updated only from observed optimization
outcomes. Their product with $q_t$ gives $r_t=c_t\tau_tq_t$. The early-window
attenuation $a_t=\operatorname{clip}(1-(t-1)/T_{\mathrm L},0,1)$ then yields
the acquisition-time adjustment
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\delta_t(\btheta)
&=\operatorname{clip}\!\left(
a_t\lambda_{\max}r_t[\rho_t(\btheta)]_+,0,\delta_{\max}
\right),\\
\mu_t^{\mathrm L}(\btheta)
&=\mu_t(\btheta)-\delta_t(\btheta),\qquad
(\sigma_t^{\mathrm L})^2(\btheta)=\sigma_t^2(\btheta).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:bounded_coupling}
\end{equation}
Because the scalar target is minimized, reducing the mean near positively
correlated anchors makes those regions more attractive to EI. The explicit
clipping guarantees
$0\leq\delta_t(\btheta)\leq\delta_{\max}$ in standardized target units.
The operation does not alter fitted targets or posterior uncertainty and does
not create pseudo-observations. Table~\ref{tab:protocol_settings} lists only
the core settings needed to instantiate these operators.

\subsection{Complete Optimization Procedure}

Let $\mu_t^{\mathrm A}$ equal $\mu_t^{\mathrm L}$ when every coupling check
passes and $\mu_t$ otherwise. Standard minimization EI is evaluated using this
active mean. The candidate pool combines the incumbent, Gaussian and uniform
samples, multi-start L-BFGS-B solutions, and accepted regional restarts.
Candidates are repaired to $\Theta_{\mathrm{proto}}$ before simulation;
path-dependent feasibility is known only after evaluation.

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{LLMBO-MO}
\label{alg:llmbo}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Simulator $\bS$, LLM $\bL$, BO budget $T$, weights $\bW$,
guidance horizon $T_{\mathrm L}$
\Ensure Evaluated nondominated set $\bP$
\State Query $\bL$ for a warm-start pool; repair and rank by
\eqref{eq:portfolio}
\State Add selected LLM proposals and random protocol-domain-valid points
\State Evaluate the initial portfolio and initialize $\bD$
\For{$t=1$ to $T$}
    \State Select $\bw^{(t)}$; transform and re-scalarize feasible data
    \State Fit the GP to $g_t(\cdot\mid\bw^{(t)})$
    \If{$t\leq T_{\mathrm L}$}
        \State Query $\bL$ for a point/region and validate its anchors
        \State Add accepted anchors as restarts; attempt \eqref{eq:bounded_coupling}
    \EndIf
    \State Maximize EI using $\mu_t^{\mathrm A}$ and select $\btheta_{t+1}$
    \State Evaluate $\bS(\btheta_{t+1})$; update $\bD$ and feasible set $\bP$
\EndFor
\State \Return $\bP$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
