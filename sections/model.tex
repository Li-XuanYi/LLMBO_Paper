\section{Electrochemical--Thermal Simulation and Degradation Proxy}
\label{sec:model_section}
\label{sec:model}

Every candidate protocol is evaluated by the same coupled numerical model.
This common evaluator maps the protocol vector $\btheta$ to the three
objectives in Eq.~\eqref{eq:cmop} and to a feasibility flag; the optimizer does
not access its internal states when selecting the next protocol.

\subsection{Electrochemical Model}

The electrochemical evaluator is a Single Particle Model with electrolyte
dynamics (SPMe). It retains solid-phase diffusion, electrolyte transport, and
interfacial reaction kinetics at lower cost than a full porous-electrode
model~\cite{ref6}. Successive constant-current stages are simulated
sequentially, so the terminal electrochemical state of one stage initializes
the next. The implementation uses PyBaMM~\cite{ref7} and applies the same
solver configuration to every optimization variant.

The default studies use the Chen2020 parameter set for the LG INR21700-M50
cell~\cite{ref8}; cross-parameterization evaluation uses Ecker2015~\cite{ref9}.
Cell-specific capacity, voltage limits, and operating bounds are fixed within
each matched comparison. The electrochemical model supplies the voltage trace,
heat-generation terms, and completion status required by the thermal and
constraint calculations.

\subsection{Thermal Model}

The thermal evaluator uses a spatially lumped energy balance,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
C_{\mathrm{th}}\frac{\mathrm dT}{\mathrm dt}
&=\dot Q_{\mathrm{gen}}-hA(T-T_{\mathrm{amb}}),\\
\Delta T_p(\btheta)
&=\max_t T(t)-T_{\mathrm{init}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:thermal_model}
\end{equation}
Here $C_{\mathrm{th}}$ is the effective thermal capacitance,
$\dot Q_{\mathrm{gen}}$ collects electrochemical, ohmic, and reversible heat,
and $hA$ is the lumped heat-transfer coefficient. The quantities
$T_{\mathrm{amb}}$ and $T_{\mathrm{init}}$ denote the ambient and initial cell
temperatures. Because the model is spatially lumped, $\Delta T_p$ is a
cell-level peak-temperature-rise metric; local temperature gradients are
outside the selected model resolution.

\subsection{Protocol-Level Degradation Proxy}
\label{sec:aging}

The third objective is a control-oriented severity proxy, not a calibrated
estimate of long-horizon capacity fade. Let $\bar s$, $\bar T$, and $\bar I$
denote time-averaged SOC, temperature, and current over a completed charge,
with $\bar s$ represented in the simulator's percentage-SOC convention. The
implementation evaluates
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\chi(\bar s,\bar T,\bar I)
&=(a_s\bar s+b_s)
\exp\!\left(\frac{\beta_I\bar I-E_a}{R\bar T}\right),\\
D_{\mathrm{chg}}(\btheta)
&=\kappa_D Q_{\mathrm{eff}}
\left(\frac{\chi(\bar s,\bar T,\bar I)}{q_a}\right)^{1/\gamma}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:aging}
\end{equation}
The coefficients $a_s$, $b_s$, $\beta_I$, $E_a$, $R$, $q_a$, $\gamma$, and
$\kappa_D$ are fixed for all optimizers and are not re-estimated from the
reported optimization results. This algebraic construction is related to the
severity-factor family of semi-empirical aging models~\cite{refSuri}.

We report $D_{\mathrm{chg}}$ in arbitrary proxy units (a.u.). A smaller value
indicates less severe trajectory-averaged operating conditions under this
common proxy; it does not represent percentage capacity loss, remaining useful
life, or an independently calibrated lifetime prediction. This boundary is
maintained throughout the objective plots, result tables, and conclusions.
