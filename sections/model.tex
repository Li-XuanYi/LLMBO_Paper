\section{Electrochemical--Thermal--Aging Model}
\label{sec:model_section}

\subsection{Electrochemical Model}
\label{sec:model}

The electrochemical dynamics are described by the Single Particle Model with
electrolyte dynamics (SPMe). The SPMe retains solid-phase diffusion,
electrolyte transport, and interfacial reaction kinetics while reducing the
computational cost relative to a full porous-electrode model. The terminal
voltage is calculated as
\begin{equation}
U=U_{\mathrm{eq}}+\eta_r+\eta_d+\Delta\Phi,
\label{eq:voltage}
\end{equation}
where $U$ is the terminal voltage, $U_{\mathrm{eq}}$ is the equilibrium
potential, $\eta_r$ is the ohmic overpotential, $\eta_d$ is the concentration
overpotential, and $\Delta\Phi$ is the average potential difference in the
electrolyte. The SOC is obtained from the solid-phase lithium concentration
predicted by the SPMe.

\subsection{Thermal Model}

The cylindrical cell is described by a lumped thermal model,
\begin{equation}
\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}\frac{\partial T}{\partial t}=\bar Q-\frac{hA}{V}(T-T_a),
\label{eq:thermal_balance}
\end{equation}
where $\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is the effective volumetric heat capacity, $T$ is
the cell temperature, $\bar Q$ is the average heat-generation rate, $h$ is the
heat-transfer coefficient, $A$ is the heat-dissipation area, $V$ is the cell
volume, and $T_a$ is the ambient temperature. The effective volumetric heat
capacity is
\begin{equation}
\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}=\frac{\sum_k\rho_kC_{p,k}L_k}{\sum_kL_k},
\label{eq:rho_eff}
\end{equation}
where $\rho_k$, $C_{p,k}$, and $L_k$ are the density, specific heat capacity,
and thickness of component $k$, respectively. The index $k$ covers the negative
current collector, negative electrode, separator, positive electrode, and
positive current collector.

The thermal objective in Eq.~\eqref{eq:peak_temp} is the maximum temperature
rise during charging,
\begin{equation}
T_p(\theta)=\max_{0\le t\le t_c(\theta)}[T(t)-T_0],
\label{eq:peak_temp}
\end{equation}
where $T_0$ is the initial cell temperature.

\subsection{Aging Model}
\label{sec:aging}

Charging-induced degradation is evaluated using the same semi-empirical aging
model as EIMO. The normalized capacity-loss indicator is
\begin{equation}
Q_s(\xi,Ah)=\sigma_{\mathrm{fun}}(\xi)Ah^z,
\label{eq:aging}
\end{equation}
where $\xi=(\mathrm{SOC},I,T)$ contains the average SOC, current, and temperature
during charging, $Ah$ is the charge throughput, $z$ is the power-law exponent,
and $\sigma_{\mathrm{fun}}$ is the severity-factor function. The severity factor
is
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{\mathrm{fun}}(\xi)=(\alpha\,\mathrm{SOC}+\beta)\exp\!\left(\frac{-E_a+\eta_a I}{R_{\mathrm{gas}}(273.15+T)}\right),
\label{eq:severity}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha$ and $\beta$ describe the SOC dependence, $\eta_a$ describes the
current dependence, $E_a$ is the activation energy, and $R_{\mathrm{gas}}$ is
the universal gas constant.

The quantity $Q_s$ is used as a relative degradation indicator for comparing
charging protocols, rather than as a direct prediction of battery end-of-life.
A smaller $Q_s$ therefore indicates a smaller degradation contribution from one
charging process.

Together, the electrochemical, thermal, and aging models provide the objective
values $\{t_c,T_p,Q_s\}$ and determine whether a protocol satisfies the voltage
and temperature constraints. These simulator outputs are the only objective and
feasibility information used by the optimizer. Since each new protocol requires
one battery simulation, indiscriminate exploration is costly. This directly
motivates the LLMBO-MO framework in the next section: Bayesian optimization
remains responsible for numerical search, while the LLM is used only to improve
where the limited simulation budget is spent.
