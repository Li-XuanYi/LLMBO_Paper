\section{Battery Simulation Model}
\label{sec:model_section}

Each candidate protocol is evaluated by a common battery-model pipeline
consisting of an electrochemical--thermal simulator and a protocol-level
degradation proxy.
Using the same evaluator for all optimization methods isolates differences
caused by the search algorithms rather than by the objective model.

\subsection{Electrochemical--Thermal Model}
\label{sec:model}

The electrochemical dynamics are represented by a Single Particle Model with
electrolyte dynamics (SPMe).
The SPMe retains solid-phase diffusion, electrolyte transport, and interfacial
reaction kinetics at substantially lower computational cost than a full
porous-electrode model~\cite{ref6}.
The implementation uses PyBaMM~\cite{ref7}.
A spatially lumped thermal model is coupled to the electrochemical simulation.
The thermal balance and the reported temperature-rise objective are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
C_{\mathrm{th}}\frac{\mathrm dT}{\mathrm dt}
&=\dot Q_{\mathrm{gen}}-hA(T-T_{\mathrm{amb}}),\\
\Delta T_p(\btheta)
&=\max_t T(t)-T_{\mathrm{init}},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:thermal}
\end{equation}
where $C_{\mathrm{th}}$ is the effective thermal capacitance,
$\dot Q_{\mathrm{gen}}$ collects electrochemical, ohmic, and reversible heat,
and $hA$ is the lumped heat-transfer coefficient.
Because the thermal model is spatially lumped, $\Delta T_p$ represents the peak
cell-level temperature rise and does not resolve local temperature gradients or
thermomechanical loading.

The Chen2020 parameter set is used for the LG INR21700-M50 case~\cite{ref8},
while Ecker2015 provides a second battery parameterization~\cite{ref9}.
Cell-specific capacity, voltage, and temperature limits are applied, while the
protocol representation and optimization procedure remain unchanged.
Successive charging stages are simulated sequentially so that the terminal
electrochemical and thermal state of one stage initializes the next.

\subsection{Protocol-Level Degradation Model}
\label{sec:aging}

A single protocol simulation does not resolve long-horizon capacity fade.
Accordingly, the third objective is a control-oriented degradation proxy rather
than a direct prediction of capacity lost during one charge.
Let $\bar s$, $\bar T$, and $\bar I$ denote the time-averaged SOC, temperature,
and current over the completed charging trajectory.
The implementation evaluates
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\chi(\bar s,\bar T,\bar I)
&=(a_s\bar s+b_s)
\exp\!\left(\frac{\beta_I\bar I-E_a}{R\bar T}\right),\\
D_{\mathrm{chg}}(\btheta)
&=\kappa_D Q_{\mathrm{eff}}
\left(\frac{\chi(\bar s,\bar T,\bar I)}{q_a}\right)^{1/\gamma}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:aging}
\end{equation}
Here $a_s$, $b_s$, $\beta_I$, $E_a$, $R$, $q_a$, $\gamma$, and $\kappa_D$
are fixed proxy coefficients shared by all optimization methods.
The construction is related to the severity-factor family of control-oriented
semi-empirical aging models~\cite{refSuri}.

The scale factor $\kappa_D$ preserves the implemented objective scale; therefore
$D_{\mathrm{chg}}$ is reported in arbitrary proxy units rather than as
percentage capacity loss.
Because the proxy uses trajectory averages, it does not separately resolve
short high-SOC, high-current, or high-temperature excursions.
This limitation is retained when interpreting the Pareto protocols.

In summary, the battery model maps each protocol $\btheta$ to
$\mathbf f(\btheta)$ together with a feasibility flag.
For BO, this mapping is treated as an expensive black box.
Only simulator-evaluated objective values are used for surrogate fitting and
Pareto-set construction.
