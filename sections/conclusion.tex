\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper proposed LLMBO-MO for multiobjective battery fast-charging protocol
design under a limited simulation budget. The framework combines
decomposition-based multiobjective Bayesian optimization with LLM-informed
initialization and a posterior-covariance Region-Lift mechanism, while
retaining battery-model evaluations as the sole source of objective values,
feasibility, GP fitting, and Pareto updates.

Under a budget of 56 simulator evaluations, LLMBO-MO achieved a mean final HV
of \ChenLLMBOMean\,$\pm$\,\ChenLLMBOStd\ on Chen2020, outperforming ParEGO by
\ChenFinalGain, with a similar advantage observed on Ecker2015. Ablation
results further show that battery-specific LLM-informed initialization is the
main source of the observed improvement, whereas the independent contribution
of Region-Lift requires further controlled validation. These results indicate
that LLM knowledge is most effective as an early-stage search prior, while BO
should remain the primary numerical optimizer.

The current study is limited to simulation-based evaluation with two battery
parameterizations. Future work will therefore investigate Region-Lift through
controlled multi-seed experiments, extend the framework to broader battery
chemistries and operating conditions, and validate the proposed method on
physical cells with experimentally measured degradation.
