\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented LLMBO-MO, a decomposition-based multiobjective Bayesian
optimization method for battery fast-charging protocol design under a limited
simulation budget.
The method retains a numerical MOBO backbone for multiobjective trade-off
selection, scalarization, GP surrogate modeling, EI optimization, simulator
evaluation, and Pareto updating, while introducing LLM knowledge through two
bounded search mechanisms.
A screened and diversity-aware LLM-informed initialization improves the initial
portfolio when simulator observations are scarce, and weight-conditioned
regional guidance subsequently provides local acquisition-search preferences
during an early optimization window.
Neither mechanism replaces the battery simulator or creates synthetic objective
observations.

The numerical results show that LLMBO-MO improves fixed-budget Pareto search on
the primary Chen2020 case, with an approximately \ChenDisplayedGain\ final HV
advantage over ParEGO under 56 simulator evaluations, and favorable behavior is
also observed under the Ecker2015 parameterization.
The initialization study further supports the benefit of battery-specific LLM
knowledge in the low-data regime, while the component ablation shows that the
complete LLMBO-MO configuration provides the strongest short-budget result.
Together, these results support using LLM knowledge as a bounded search prior
inside a multiobjective Bayesian optimization algorithm rather than as a
replacement for simulator-grounded numerical optimization.

Future work will extend the evaluation to physical-cell experiments,
experimentally grounded degradation endpoints, broader operating conditions,
and controlled studies of LLM backends and prompt robustness.
