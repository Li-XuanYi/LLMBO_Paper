\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper proposed LLMBO-MO, an LLM-informed multiobjective Bayesian
optimization framework for multistage battery fast-charging protocol design
under limited simulation budgets. The design task is formulated as a
constrained multiobjective problem balancing charging time, peak temperature
rise, and charging-induced degradation, and is solved by decomposition-based
BO, in which successive Tchebycheff scalarizations are modeled by GP
surrogates and sampled by maximizing EI. First, LLM-informed initialization
uses charging-variable definitions, operating constraints, and battery-domain
knowledge to propose initial protocols; candidate screening and
diversity-aware selection are then combined with random valid protocols to
construct the initial simulation design. Second, during the early search, the
LLM provides a preferred region conditioned on the active scalarization
weight. Third, Region-Lift uses GP posterior cross-covariance to translate
the regional preference into a bounded adjustment of the mean used to compute
EI, and standard EI is used when no valid regional preference is available.

Under a budget of 56 battery-model evaluations, LLMBO-MO achieved the highest
final mean HV among all compared methods on both battery parameterizations.
On Chen2020, the mean final HV was \ChenLLMBOMean, compared with
\ChenParEGOMean\ for ParEGO, corresponding to a \ChenFinalGain\ increase; on
Ecker2015, the corresponding values were \EckerLLMBOMean\ and
\EckerParEGOMean. The initialization study showed that battery-specific
prompting improved short-budget optimization more than generic prompting,
and the component study showed that LLM-informed initialization accounted for
most of the improvement, while regional guidance further increased the mean
final HV from \AblationWarmMean\ to \AblationFullMean. Comparisons across LLM
backends further indicated that the final performance is robust to the
choice of backend.

It should be noted that the optimized protocols have been validated only in
simulation based on the electrochemical--thermal--aging model, and
discrepancies between model predictions and real battery behavior may still
exist. Future work will assess regional guidance over wider operating
conditions, validate the resulting protocols on physical cells with measured
degradation, and extend the framework to other cell chemistries and
pack-level charging.
