\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented LLMBO-MO for incorporating contextual LLM knowledge into
expensive multiobjective battery optimization without weakening the numerical
evidence path. Its key principle is an asymmetric interface: screened LLM
preferences guide initialization and early candidate placement, whereas only
simulator-evaluated protocols update the GP surrogate and Pareto set. Bounded
guidance and numerical fallbacks preserve the authority of the underlying BO
procedure.

Experiments on two battery parameterizations show modest improvements in final
hypervolume. The accompanying analyses identify informed initialization as the
main individual contributor and show that prompt and objective preprocessing
choices influence optimization behavior. Together, these results support the
use of LLM knowledge as controlled search guidance rather than as numerical
evidence.

The present study remains simulation based and uses a trajectory-averaged
degradation proxy in arbitrary units. Calibrated lifetime assessment, broader
acquisition comparisons, and physical replay therefore remain important
directions for future validation.
