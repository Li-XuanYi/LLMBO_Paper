\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented LLMBO-MO, an LLM-guided extension of a ParEGO-style
decomposition-based multiobjective Bayesian optimization framework for battery
fast-charging protocol design under a limited simulation budget.
The numerical MOBO backbone performs trade-off decomposition, GP surrogate
modeling, expected-improvement optimization, simulator evaluation, and Pareto
updating.
LLM knowledge is introduced only through two search mechanisms:
a protocol-domain-screened and diversity-aware initialization, and a
weight-conditioned regional mechanism that supplies additional acquisition
restarts during an early optimization window.
All selected protocols are evaluated by the battery simulator before they are
used for surrogate fitting or Pareto updating.

Under 56 simulator evaluations, LLMBO-MO attains a mean final HV of
\ChenLLMBOMean\,$\pm$\,\ChenLLMBOStd\ on Chen2020, compared with
\ChenParEGOMean\,$\pm$\,\ChenParEGOStd\ for the backbone-matched
ParEGO, corresponding to a \ChenFinalGain\ improvement; a
\EckerFinalGain\ improvement is observed under the Ecker2015
parameterization.
LLMBO-MO also outperforms NSGA-II, DISK, and PIMD under the same
budget.
The matched initialization study localizes the source of the gain:
battery-specific LLM information improves early search when simulator
observations are scarce, whereas a domain-agnostic prompt of comparable
detail does not.
In the component ablation, LLM-informed initialization accounts for
most of the improvement, and the complete framework achieves both the
highest mean HV and the smallest dispersion, indicating that regional
guidance acts as a secondary refinement of the early acquisition
search.
The resulting Pareto set spans interpretable operating compromises,
from a speed-favouring staircase schedule to a durability-favouring
uniform schedule.
Overall, LLM knowledge is most reliably useful as early-stage search
guidance within a numerical MOBO process.

Two limitations should be kept in mind.
The evaluation is simulation based: the SPMe electrochemical and lumped
thermal model in Section~\ref{sec:model_section} captures the dominant
dynamics of lithium-ion fast charging but does not resolve localized
degradation, lithium plating, or thermal gradients, and the third
objective is a trajectory-averaged control-oriented degradation proxy
rather than a measured capacity-fade endpoint.
In addition, the results are obtained with a single LLM backend on a
single battery chemistry family, so they characterize the proposed
framework rather than its sensitivity to the choice of language model
or chemistry.

Future work will extend the evaluation to stronger matched MOBO
baselines (notably qEHVI and qNEHVI), controlled comparisons across
LLM backends and prompt realizations, broader battery operating
conditions, and physical-cell experiments with experimentally grounded
degradation endpoints.
