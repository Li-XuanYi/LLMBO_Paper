\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this paper, an LLM-augmented multiobjective Bayesian optimization method,
called LLMBO-MO, was developed for battery fast-charging protocol design under
a limited simulator-evaluation budget.
The method is motivated by two characteristics of the problem: BO is
particularly sensitive to initialization when task-specific observations are
scarce, while LLM-generated numerical outputs cannot be regarded as verified
battery evidence.
Accordingly, LLMBO-MO uses the LLM primarily to guide search locations.
A protocol-domain-screened and diversity-aware warm start introduces
battery-domain knowledge before sequential optimization.
Temporary weight-conditioned regional guidance is retained as an auxiliary
option for biasing early acquisition search.
All objective values, feasibility decisions, GP targets, and Pareto relations
remain determined by the battery simulator.

The numerical results show that LLMBO-MO obtains the highest observed mean HV
among the Chen2020 comparison methods under 56 simulator evaluations,
with a mean HV of \ChenLLMBOMean\ compared with \ChenParEGOMean\ for ParEGO.
The observed advantage also persists under the Ecker2015 parameterization.
More importantly, the controlled prompt study shows that the battery-specific
warm start improves mean HV by \PromptHVGain\ over random initialization after
multiplicity correction, while the component study identifies the warm start as
the more stable source of improvement.
The component results do not establish a systematic independent gain from regional
guidance, indicating that LLM-derived knowledge is most reliably useful in the
evidence-scarce early stage rather than as persistent intervention throughout
the complete BO process.

The present conclusions are limited to SPMe-based simulation, two battery
parameterizations, the adopted trajectory-averaged degradation proxy, and the
available simulation runs.
Future work should include synchronized multi-seed comparisons with stronger
multiobjective BO baselines, controlled studies of LLM backends and prompt
robustness, and physical-cell validation with experimentally grounded
degradation endpoints.
These studies will determine whether the early-stage benefit observed here
persists under broader battery conditions and real experimental uncertainty.
