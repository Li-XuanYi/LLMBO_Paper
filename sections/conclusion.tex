\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this paper, an LLM-augmented multiobjective Bayesian optimization method,
called LLMBO-MO, was developed for battery fast-charging protocol design under
a limited simulator-evaluation budget.
The method is motivated by two characteristics of the problem: BO is
particularly sensitive to initialization when task-specific observations are
scarce, while LLM-generated numerical outputs cannot be regarded as verified
battery evidence.
Accordingly, LLMBO-MO uses the LLM primarily to guide search locations.
A protocol-domain-screened and diversity-aware warm start introduces
battery-domain knowledge before sequential optimization.
Bounded weight-conditioned regional guidance then refines acquisition search
during the same evidence-scarce early stage.
All objective values, feasibility decisions, GP targets, and Pareto relations
remain determined by the battery simulator.

The numerical results show a fixed-budget advantage for LLMBO-MO.
Under 56 simulator evaluations, the descriptive five-run Chen2020 endpoint
mean is \ChenLLMBOMean\ for LLMBO-MO and \ChenParEGOMean\ for ParEGO,
corresponding to a \ChenParEGOGain\ advantage. Separately, the archived
seed-8409 search trajectories displayed in the convergence figure end at
\ChenDisplayedLLMBO\ and \ChenDisplayedParEGO, respectively, a
\ChenDisplayedGain\ plot-level gap. The favorable behavior also persists in
the five-run Ecker2015 comparison.
More importantly, the controlled prompt study shows that the battery-specific
warm start improves mean HV by \PromptHVGain\ over random initialization after
multiplicity correction.
The matched three-seed short-budget component ablation further shows that the
complete LLMBO-MO configuration achieves the highest mean HV
(\AblationFullMean), the smallest observed dispersion
($\pm\AblationFullStd$), and the best outcome in two of the three seeds. The
warm start provides the dominant initial improvement, while regional guidance
acts as a complementary refinement that gives the complete configuration its
best overall short-budget result.
These results support concentrating LLM intervention in the evidence-scarce
early stage, where domain knowledge can shape search efficiently while the
simulator continues to determine all numerical optimization evidence.

The present conclusions are established within SPMe-based simulation using two
battery parameterizations and the adopted trajectory-averaged degradation proxy.
Future work will extend the evaluation to broader multiobjective acquisition
strategies, controlled LLM-backend and prompt studies, and physical-cell
validation with experimentally grounded degradation endpoints.
These extensions will test the generality of the observed early-stage advantage
under broader battery conditions and real experimental uncertainty.
