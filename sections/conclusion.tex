\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented LLMBO-MO, an LLM-guided extension of a ParEGO-style
decomposition-based multiobjective Bayesian optimization framework for battery
fast-charging protocol design under a limited simulation budget.
The numerical MOBO backbone performs trade-off decomposition, GP surrogate
modeling, expected-improvement optimization, simulator evaluation, and Pareto
updating.
LLM knowledge is introduced only through two search mechanisms:
a protocol-domain-screened and diversity-aware initialization, and a
weight-conditioned regional mechanism that supplies additional acquisition
restarts during an early optimization window.
All selected protocols are evaluated by the battery simulator before they are
used for surrogate fitting or Pareto updating.

Under 56 simulator evaluations and five seeds, LLMBO-MO attains a mean
final HV of \ChenLLMBOMean\,$\pm$\,\ChenLLMBOStd\ on Chen2020, compared
with \ChenParEGOMean\,$\pm$\,\ChenParEGOStd\ for ParEGO, corresponding
to a \ChenFinalGain\ improvement.
A similar final-HV advantage is observed under the Ecker2015
parameterization.
The matched initialization study provides the clearest mechanism-level
evidence: battery-specific LLM information improves early search when
simulator observations are scarce.
The three-seed component ablation does not establish an independent
systematic benefit from regional guidance, although the combined
LLMBO-MO variant achieves the highest observed mean HV among the four
short-budget variants.
These results indicate that LLM knowledge is most reliably useful as
early-stage search guidance within a numerical MOBO process.

\emph{Despite these results, several limitations should be kept in mind.}
First, the evaluation is simulation based: the SPMe electrochemical and
lumped thermal model in Section~\ref{sec:model_section} captures the
dominant dynamics of lithium-ion fast charging but does not resolve
localized degradation, lithium plating, or thermal gradients, and the
conclusions therefore do not transfer directly to physical cells.
Second, the third objective is a control-oriented trajectory-averaged
degradation proxy rather than a measured capacity-fade endpoint, so the
Pareto protocols should be interpreted in proxy units rather than as
quantitative cycle-life predictions.
Third, the five-seed main comparison is descriptive: the reported
means and standard deviations characterize the observed seed
distribution, and the relative improvement over ParEGO is small enough
that an additional inferential test is needed before the difference
should be called statistically reliable (paired test statistics are
reported in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).
Fourth, the three-seed regional-guidance ablation is statistically
inconclusive, and the regional mechanism should therefore be read as
a secondary early-search refinement rather than as a peer
contribution to the LLM-informed initialization.
Fifth, the present results are obtained with a single LLM backend
(V4-Flash) on a single battery chemistry family; a controlled
cross-LLM robustness study and additional parameterizations are
required before the method can be claimed to be LLM-backend-agnostic
or chemistry-agnostic.
Finally, the Ecker2015 experiment is interpreted as a second-battery
parameterization check rather than as a formal cross-battery transfer
study, because the two parameterizations share the same simulator
family and the same five seeds.

Future work will extend the evaluation to stronger matched MOBO
baselines (notably qEHVI and qNEHVI), controlled comparisons across
LLM backends and prompt realizations, broader battery operating
conditions, and physical-cell experiments with experimentally grounded
degradation endpoints.
