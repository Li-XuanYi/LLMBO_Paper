\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper addressed the challenge of integrating large-language-model (LLM)
knowledge into multiobjective Bayesian optimization for battery fast-charging
design, where evaluation budgets are tightly limited by the cost of
electrochemical--thermal simulation.
We proposed LLMBO-MO, a framework built on the insight that LLM-derived
preferences can guide \emph{where} the optimizer searches, provided that only
simulator-evaluated protocols determine \emph{what} enters the Pareto set.
This separation is enforced through an asymmetric two-touchpoint architecture:
a protocol-domain-screened warm-start that supplies diverse initial candidates
without per-cell calibration, and bounded regional guidance whose influence is
controlled by confidence and covariance gates, early-window attenuation, and
an explicit pointwise cap in standardized target units.

Under a matched budget of 56 simulator evaluations and five fixed seeds,
LLMBO-MO attained higher mean final hypervolume than ParEGO on two SPMe
parameterizations, with a 1.7\% mean improvement on Chen2020 (up to 5.1\% in a
paired seed) and a 17.8\% mean improvement on Ecker2015.
A component decomposition revealed that the screened LLM warm-start
alone accounts for the majority of the mean performance gain, while bounded
regional guidance provides a smaller positive mean contribution.
A ten-seed prompt-design ablation further confirmed the statistical reliability
of LLM-derived initialization ($p < 0.01$), establishing that prompt content is
a first-order performance factor.

The practical message of this study is that language-model guidance can be
incorporated through a verification-aware interface: simulator evaluations
remain the source of training targets, advisory influence is explicitly
bounded, and gate-rejected guidance recovers the unguided acquisition.
This design pattern is not specific to battery charging and may
apply to other domains where expensive simulators or physical experiments
define the evidence standard.

The present evidence spans two SPMe parameterizations under a controlled
simulator budget.
The next stage is to validate selected Pareto protocols through physical replay
and extend the matched-budget study across additional acquisition families,
cell conditions, and LLM backends.
