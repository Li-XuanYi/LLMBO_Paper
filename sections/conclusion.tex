\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper proposed LLMBO-MO for multiobjective battery fast-charging protocol
design under a limited simulation budget. The method retains a
decomposition-based multiobjective Bayesian optimization framework as the
numerical optimizer and introduces LLM knowledge only during the data-scarce
stage of the search. LLM-informed initialization converts battery-domain
knowledge into a screened and diverse set of initial charging protocols, while
the posterior-covariance Region-Lift mechanism uses an accepted,
weight-conditioned regional preference to adjust acquisition ranking during
the early BO iterations. In both mechanisms, the LLM affects only the search
preference; objective values, feasibility, GP fitting, and Pareto updates are
determined exclusively from battery-model evaluations.

Under a budget of 56 simulator evaluations, LLMBO-MO achieves a mean final HV
of \ChenLLMBOMean\,$\pm$\,\ChenLLMBOStd\ on Chen2020, compared with
\ChenParEGOMean\,$\pm$\,\ChenParEGOStd\ for ParEGO, corresponding to a
\ChenFinalGain\ improvement. A similar advantage is observed under the
Ecker2015 parameterization, where the final HV improves by
\EckerFinalGain. LLMBO-MO also attains a higher final HV than NSGA-II, DISK,
and PIMD under the same evaluation budget. The initialization study further
shows that battery-specific prompt information provides a clear benefit over
random initialization and a domain-agnostic prompt. The component ablation
indicates that initialization accounts for most of the observed improvement,
whereas the current experiments do not provide sufficient evidence to claim a
separate systematic gain from Region-Lift. The representative Pareto protocols
also exhibit the expected trade-offs among charging time, temperature rise,
and the aging indicator. These results suggest that LLM knowledge is most
useful as an early search prior, while the numerical BO process should remain
responsible for the subsequent optimization.

The present study is limited to simulation-based evaluation. The adopted
electrochemical--thermal--aging model captures the main charging dynamics but
does not represent all spatially localized degradation and thermal effects, and
$Q_s$ is a model-based aging indicator rather than a measured capacity-fade
endpoint. In addition, the numerical validation covers two battery
parameterizations, while the comparison across LLM backends is limited to a
single-seed descriptive study. Future work will therefore focus on controlled
multi-seed ablations of Region-Lift, broader studies of LLM backends and prompt
designs, extension to a wider range of battery chemistries and operating
conditions, and validation on physical cells with experimentally measured
degradation.
