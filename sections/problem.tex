\section{Fast-Charging Optimization Problem}
\label{sec:problem}

This section defines the protocol parameterization, the three competing
objectives, and the physical constraints that together form the constrained
multiobjective optimization problem.
The coupled electrochemical--thermal simulator that evaluates each candidate
protocol is described in Sections~\ref{sec:model} and~\ref{sec:aging}.
Figure~\ref{fig:charging_problem} summarizes the formulation: protocol-feasible
coordinates are mapped through the battery simulator to objective values and
constraint checks, and a constrained Pareto search updates the protocol
proposal.

\subsection{SOC-Domain Multistage Protocol Representation}

A charging protocol is a sequence of constant-current stages that takes the
battery from an initial state of charge $s_0$ to a target $s_f$.
Following established practice in BO-based charging design~\cite{ref14,ref13},
each stage is parameterized by its current level and the SOC span it delivers,
rather than by a fixed duration.
The SOC-domain representation makes switching boundaries explicit: the
delivered capacity in each stage depends on the current level, so stage
duration is a dependent quantity determined by transferred charge rather than
an independent decision variable.
This avoids the coupling between current and time that would complicate
constraint handling and numerical stability in a time-domain representation.

For a $K$-stage protocol, let $I_k$ and $\Delta s_k$ denote the current and
SOC span of stage $k$.
The independent decision vector, the dependent terminal span, and the total
charging time are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\btheta
&=[I_1,\ldots,I_K,\;\Delta s_1,\ldots,\Delta s_{K-1}]^\top,\\[2pt]
\Delta s_K
&=s_f-s_0-\sum_{k=1}^{K-1}\Delta s_k,\\[2pt]
t_c(\btheta)&=\kappa_t\,Q_{\mathrm{eff}}
\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{\Delta s_k}{I_k^{\mathrm{cell}}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:protocol}
\end{equation}
In Eq.~\eqref{eq:protocol}, $Q_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is the effective cell capacity,
$I_k^{\mathrm{cell}}=\phi_{\mathrm{cell}}(I_k)$ maps a protocol-level current
coordinate to the physical cell current via a fixed, cell-specific convention,
and $\kappa_t$ converts charge to time units.
The protocol domain $\Theta_{\mathrm{proto}}$ contains all box-feasible current
and SOC-span coordinates satisfying $\Delta s_K\geq\Delta s_K^{\min}$, which
excludes degenerate terminal stages while guaranteeing completion of the
prescribed SOC interval.
A stricter preferred span $\Delta s_K^{\mathrm{soft}}$ is used only during LLM
warm-start candidate ranking (Section~\ref{sec:method}) and does not enter the
simulator constraint set.

This indexed representation accommodates any finite $K$; the optimization
method does not depend on a particular stage count.
Following~\cite{ref14,ref13}, $K=3$ is used in all experiments.

\subsection{Objectives and Constraints}

Three competing quantities are minimized.
\textbf{Charging time} $t_c(\btheta)$ is computed analytically from the
protocol coordinates via Eq.~\eqref{eq:protocol}.
\textbf{Peak temperature rise} $\Delta T_p(\btheta)$ is the maximum cell
temperature during the charge minus the initial temperature, obtained from the
coupled SPMe--thermal simulation (Section~\ref{sec:model}).
\textbf{Degradation proxy} $D_{\mathrm{chg}}(\btheta)$ is a control-oriented
algebraic severity factor evaluated on trajectory-averaged SOC, temperature,
and current; its construction is detailed in Section~\ref{sec:aging}.
Smaller values of $D_{\mathrm{chg}}$ indicate less severe average operating
conditions under the common proxy, not a prediction of capacity loss in
absolute units.

The constrained multiobjective problem is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{\btheta\in\Theta_{\mathrm{proto}}}\quad
&\mathbf f(\btheta)=
\begin{bmatrix}
t_c(\btheta)\\[2pt]
\Delta T_p(\btheta)\\[2pt]
D_{\mathrm{chg}}(\btheta)
\end{bmatrix},\\
\mathrm{s.t.}\quad
&V_{\mathrm{peak}}(\btheta)\le V_{\max},\\
&T_{\mathrm{peak}}(\btheta)\le T_{\max}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cmop}
\end{equation}
In Eq.~\eqref{eq:cmop}, the protocol domain $\Theta_{\mathrm{proto}}$ enforces current bounds and
completion of $[s_0,s_f]$.
The simulator checks the path-dependent voltage and temperature limits
$V_{\max}$ and $T_{\max}$, which are fixed for each cell parameterization.
A protocol that violates either limit or fails to complete the SOC interval
is marked infeasible and excluded from the evaluated Pareto set.

The three objectives are inherently conflicting.
Higher currents reduce $t_c$ but increase both $\Delta T_p$ (through Joule
heating) and $D_{\mathrm{chg}}$ (through accelerated degradation kinetics at
elevated temperature and SOC).
The Pareto front therefore represents the set of protocols for which no
objective can be improved without worsening another, and the optimization
goal is to approximate this front under a tightly limited number of simulator
evaluations.
