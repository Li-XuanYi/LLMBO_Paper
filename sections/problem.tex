\section{Fast-Charging Optimization Problem}
\label{sec:problem}

As shown in \autoref{fig:charging_problem}, the charging-design problem maps a
multistage current protocol to battery states, objective values, and operational
constraint checks.
The decision variables, optimization objectives, and constraints are introduced
below.
\input{figures/fig_charging_design_problem}

\subsection{Multistage Constant-Current Protocol}

A multistage constant-current (MCC) protocol divides the charging process into
$K$ stages.
Let $I_k$ and $\Delta s_k$ denote the current and delivered SOC span of stage
$k$, respectively.
The battery is charged from an initial SOC $s_0$ to a target SOC $s_f$.
Following established BO-based charging formulations~\cite{ref14,ref13},
SOC spans rather than stage durations are used as decision variables.
This choice makes the switching boundaries explicit in the SOC domain and
avoids a redundant current--time parameterization because the duration of each
stage is determined by its current and delivered charge.

The independent decision vector, dependent terminal SOC span, and total charging
time are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\btheta
&=[I_1,\ldots,I_K,\Delta s_1,\ldots,\Delta s_{K-1}]^\top,\\
\Delta s_K
&=s_f-s_0-\sum_{k=1}^{K-1}\Delta s_k,\\
t_c(\btheta)
&=\kappa_t Q_{\mathrm{eff}}
\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{\Delta s_k}{I_k^{\mathrm{cell}}},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:protocol}
\end{equation}
where $Q_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is the effective cell capacity,
$I_k^{\mathrm{cell}}=\phi_{\mathrm{cell}}(I_k)$ maps the protocol-level current
coordinate to the physical cell current used by the simulator, and $\kappa_t$
converts charge to time units.
The protocol domain $\Theta_{\mathrm{proto}}$ contains box-feasible current and
SOC-span coordinates satisfying $\Delta s_K\geq\Delta s_K^{\min}$, which
prevents a degenerate terminal stage and guarantees completion of the prescribed
SOC interval.
Following previous charging-design studies~\cite{ref14,ref13}, $K=3$ is used
throughout the experiments.

\subsection{Multiobjective Charging Optimization Problem}

Fast charging, thermal safety, and degradation mitigation are competing design
requirements.
Accordingly, the three minimized objectives are charging time
$t_c(\btheta)$, peak temperature rise $\Delta T_p(\btheta)$, and the
protocol-level degradation proxy $D_{\mathrm{chg}}(\btheta)$.
The constrained multiobjective optimization problem is formulated as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{\btheta\in\Theta_{\mathrm{proto}}}\quad
&\mathbf f(\btheta)=
\begin{bmatrix}
t_c(\btheta)\\
\Delta T_p(\btheta)\\
D_{\mathrm{chg}}(\btheta)
\end{bmatrix},\\
\mathrm{s.t.}\quad
&V_{\mathrm{peak}}(\btheta)\leq V_{\max},\\
&T_{\mathrm{peak}}(\btheta)\leq T_{\max}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cmop}
\end{equation}
The protocol domain enforces the current and SOC-coordinate bounds, whereas the
battery simulator checks the path-dependent voltage and temperature limits.
Any protocol that violates a limit or fails to complete the target SOC interval
is marked infeasible and excluded from the evaluated Pareto set.

The three objectives are intrinsically conflicting.
More aggressive currents reduce charging time but generally increase heat
generation and degradation severity.
Moreover, the preferred trade-off varies with user demand, and a single fixed
weight vector cannot represent all possible preferences.
The optimization target is therefore a feasible Pareto set rather than one
preselected charging schedule.
The main computational difficulty considered in this work is that this Pareto
set must be approximated with a tightly limited number of battery-model
evaluations.
