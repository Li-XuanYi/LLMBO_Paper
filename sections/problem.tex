\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}

Fast-charging design seeks a set of feasible charging protocols that balances
charging speed, thermal safety, and degradation.
Let $\btheta$ denote a multistage constant-current charging protocol and let
$\Theta_{\mathrm{proto}}$ denote its admissible protocol domain.
The charging-design problem is formulated directly as the constrained
multiobjective problem
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{\btheta\in\Theta_{\mathrm{proto}}}\quad
&\mathbf f(\btheta)=
\begin{bmatrix}
 t_c(\btheta)\\
 \Delta T_p(\btheta)\\
 D_{\mathrm{chg}}(\btheta)
\end{bmatrix},\\
\mathrm{s.t.}\quad
&I_{\min}\le I(t;\btheta)\le I_{\max},\\
&s_0\le \mathrm{SOC}(t;\btheta)\le s_f,\\
&V(t;\btheta)\le V_{\max},\\
&T(t;\btheta)\le T_{\max}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cmop}
\end{equation}
Here $t_c$ is charging time, $\Delta T_p$ is peak temperature rise, and
$D_{\mathrm{chg}}$ is the protocol-level degradation proxy defined in
Section~\ref{sec:aging}.
The three objectives are conflicting: aggressive charging can reduce $t_c$ but
generally increases thermal and degradation severity.
Because the preferred trade-off varies with user demand, the optimization target
is a feasible Pareto set rather than a solution associated with one fixed
preference vector.

\paragraph{Principal notation:}
$\btheta$ denotes the charging protocol, $\mathbf f(\btheta)$ the three-objective
vector, $\bD$ the simulator-evaluated database, $\bP$ the feasible Pareto
archive, $\bw^{(t)}$ the decomposition weight, and
$g_t(\btheta\mid\bw^{(t)})$ the scalarized objective optimized by the GP--EI
subproblem.  The battery simulator is denoted by $\bS$.

\subsection{Multistage Constant-Current Protocol}

A multistage constant-current (MCC) protocol divides the charging process into
$K$ stages.
Let $I_k$ and $\Delta s_k$ denote the applied current and delivered SOC span of
stage $k$, respectively.
The battery is charged from an initial SOC $s_0$ to a target SOC $s_f$.
Following established BO-based charging formulations~\cite{ref14,ref13}, SOC
spans rather than stage durations are used as decision variables, so that stage
boundaries are explicit in the SOC domain and the current--time representation
is not redundant.

The independent decision vector, dependent terminal SOC span, and charging time
are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\btheta
&=[I_1,\ldots,I_K,\Delta s_1,\ldots,\Delta s_{K-1}]^\top,\\
\Delta s_K
&=s_f-s_0-\sum_{k=1}^{K-1}\Delta s_k,\\
t_c(\btheta)
&=\kappa_t Q_{\mathrm{eff}}
\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{\Delta s_k}{I_k^{\mathrm{cell}}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:protocol}
\end{equation}
Here $Q_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is the effective capacity,
$I_k^{\mathrm{cell}}=\phi_{\mathrm{cell}}(I_k)$ maps the protocol current
coordinate to the physical cell current used by the simulator, and $\kappa_t$
converts charge to time units.
The domain $\Theta_{\mathrm{proto}}$ enforces the current and SOC-span bounds and
requires $\Delta s_K\ge\Delta s_K^{\min}$ to avoid a degenerate terminal stage.
Following previous charging-design studies~\cite{ref14,ref13}, $K=3$ is used in
the experiments.

\subsection{Expensive Black-Box Evaluation}

For a given protocol, the battery model returns the objective vector and a
feasibility flag,
\begin{equation}
\bS(\btheta)\longrightarrow
\bigl(\mathbf f(\btheta),\,\mathbb I_{\mathrm{feas}}(\btheta)\bigr).
\label{eq:sim_map}
\end{equation}
The protocol domain handles the explicit decision-variable bounds, whereas the
simulator checks the path-dependent voltage and temperature constraints in
\eqref{eq:cmop}.
A protocol violating these limits or failing to complete the prescribed SOC
interval is excluded from the feasible Pareto archive.
Because every evaluation of \eqref{eq:sim_map} requires an electrochemical--
thermal simulation, the central optimization challenge is to approximate the
Pareto set using as few simulator calls as possible.
