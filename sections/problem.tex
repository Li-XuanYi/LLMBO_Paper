\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}

The charging-design problem is formulated as the selection of a multistage
constant-current protocol that balances charging speed, thermal safety, and
degradation while satisfying operating constraints.
Fig.~\ref{fig:charging_problem} summarizes the protocol variables, optimization
objectives, and feasibility conditions.

\input{figures/fig_charging_design_problem}

\subsection{Multistage Constant-Current Protocol}

A multistage constant-current (MCC) protocol divides charging into $K$ stages.
Stage $k$ is characterized by a current coordinate $I_k$ and a delivered
state-of-charge (SOC) span $\Delta s_k$.
The battery is charged from an initial SOC $s_0$ to a target SOC $s_f$.
The independent decision vector and the dependent terminal SOC span are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\btheta
&=[I_1,\ldots,I_K,\Delta s_1,\ldots,\Delta s_{K-1}]^\top,\\
\Delta s_K
&=s_f-s_0-\sum_{k=1}^{K-1}\Delta s_k.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:protocol}
\end{equation}
The admissible domain $\Theta_{\mathrm{proto}}$ imposes box bounds on the
currents and SOC spans and requires
$\Delta s_K\geq\Delta s_K^{\min}$.
This lower bound prevents a degenerate terminal stage and ensures that the
protocol covers the prescribed SOC interval.

Following established BO-based charging formulations~\cite{ref14,ref13}, SOC
spans rather than stage durations are optimized.
If both current and duration were independent variables, the delivered charge
would introduce a strongly coupled and potentially redundant representation.
SOC-span coordinates instead place the switching boundaries directly in the SOC
domain and simplify constraint handling.
The experiments use $K=3$, as in those studies.

Let $I_k^{\mathrm{cell}}=\phi_{\mathrm{cell}}(I_k)$ denote the physical cell
current associated with the protocol coordinate $I_k$.
The charging time is
\begin{equation}
t_c(\btheta)=\kappa_t Q_{\mathrm{eff}}
\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{\Delta s_k}{I_k^{\mathrm{cell}}},
\label{eq:charging_time}
\end{equation}
where $Q_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is the effective capacity and $\kappa_t$ converts
charge to time units.

\subsection{Constrained Multiobjective Charging Design}

The charging-design objectives quantify charging speed, thermal response, and
degradation severity.
For a protocol $\btheta$, the minimized objective vector contains charging time
$t_c(\btheta)$, peak temperature rise $\Delta T_p(\btheta)$, and the
protocol-level degradation proxy $D_{\mathrm{chg}}(\btheta)$.
The latter two objectives are defined by the battery model in
Section~\ref{sec:model_section}.
The constrained multiobjective problem is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{\btheta\in\Theta_{\mathrm{proto}}}\quad
&\mathbf f(\btheta)=
\begin{bmatrix}
 t_c(\btheta)\\
 \Delta T_p(\btheta)\\
 D_{\mathrm{chg}}(\btheta)
\end{bmatrix},\\
\mathrm{s.t.}\quad
&I_{\min}\le I(t;\btheta)\le I_{\max},\\
&s_0\le \mathrm{SOC}(t;\btheta)\le s_f,\\
&V(t;\btheta)\le V_{\max},\\
&T(t;\btheta)\le T_{\max},
\qquad 0\le t\le t_c(\btheta).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cmop}
\end{equation}
The protocol domain enforces the explicit current and SOC-coordinate bounds.
The battery simulator checks the path-dependent voltage and temperature limits.
A protocol that violates either limit or fails to reach $s_f$ is infeasible and
is excluded from the Pareto set.

The objectives are inherently conflicting.
Higher currents can shorten charging time but generally increase heat generation
and degradation severity.
The preferred compromise varies across operating scenarios.
The optimization target is therefore a feasible Pareto set that preserves these
trade-offs rather than a single protocol selected using a fixed preference
vector.

\subsection{Expensive Black-Box Evaluation}

For each candidate protocol, the battery model returns the objective vector and
a feasibility indicator,
\begin{equation}
\bS(\btheta)\longrightarrow
\bigl(\mathbf f(\btheta),\,\mathbb I_{\mathrm{feas}}(\btheta)\bigr).
\label{eq:sim_map}
\end{equation}
Each call to $\bS$ requires a coupled electrochemical--thermal simulation and
evaluation of the degradation proxy described in Section~\ref{sec:model_section}.
The resulting optimization challenge is to approximate the feasible Pareto set
with as few simulator calls as possible.
