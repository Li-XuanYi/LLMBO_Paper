\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}

The fast-charging design problem is formulated as a constrained
multiobjective optimization problem. A candidate solution is a multistage
constant-current (MCC) charging protocol, and its performance is evaluated in
terms of charging time, thermal response, and charging-induced aging.
Fig.~\ref{fig:charging_problem} summarizes the decision variables, objectives,
and operating constraints.

\subsection{Multistage Constant-Current Protocol}

A $K$-stage MCC protocol divides the charging process into $K$ constant-current
stages. Stage $k$ is described by the charging current $I_k$ and the
corresponding SOC increment $\Delta s_k$. The battery is charged from the
initial SOC $s_0$ to the target SOC $s_f$. The independent decision variables
are collected in
\begin{equation}
\theta=[I_1,\ldots,I_K,\Delta s_1,\ldots,\Delta s_{K-1}]^\top 
\label{eq:protocol}
\end{equation}
where the SOC increment of the final stage is determined by
\begin{equation}
\Delta s_K=s_f-s_0-\sum_{k=1}^{K-1}\Delta s_k 
\label{eq:terminal_soc}
\end{equation}
Each current and SOC increment is restricted to a prescribed interval, and
$\Delta s_K$ is required to remain above a small positive lower bound so that
the final charging stage is well defined.

Following previous BO-based charging studies~\cite{nref7,nref8}, SOC
increments are optimized instead of stage durations. Once $I_k$ and
$\Delta s_k$ are specified, the duration of stage $k$ is determined by the
delivered charge. The total charging time is therefore
\begin{equation}
t_c(\theta)=3600Q_{\mathrm{eff}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{\Delta s_k}{I_k}
\label{eq:charging_time}
\end{equation}
where $Q_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is the effective cell capacity in Ah. The experiments use $K=3$.

\subsection{Constrained Multiobjective Charging Design}

For a charging protocol $\theta$, the three minimized objectives are charging
time $t_c(\theta)$, peak temperature rise $T_p(\theta)$, and aging indicator
$Q_s(\theta)$:
\begin{equation}
\min_{\theta}\quad
\mathbf f(\theta)=[t_c(\theta),\,T_p(\theta),\,Q_s(\theta)]^\top 
\label{eq:cmop}
\end{equation}
The latter two objectives are defined by the battery model in
Section~\ref{sec:model_section}. In addition to the decision-variable bounds,
the simulated charging trajectory must satisfy
\begin{equation}
U(t;\theta)\le U_{\max},\quad
T(t;\theta)\le T_{\max},\quad
0\le t\le t_c(\theta)
\label{eq:operating_constraints}
\end{equation}
where $U_{\max}$ and $T_{\max}$ are the prescribed voltage and temperature
limits. A protocol that violates either limit or fails to reach $s_f$ is
treated as infeasible and is excluded from the Pareto set.

The three objectives are conflicting. Increasing the charging current can
reduce $t_c$, but generally increases the thermal and aging costs. The desired
trade-off also depends on the operating requirement. The optimization target
is therefore a feasible Pareto set rather than a single charging protocol
defined by a fixed preference.
