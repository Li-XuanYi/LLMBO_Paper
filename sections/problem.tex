\section{Fast-Charging Optimization Problem}
\label{sec:problem}

The design loop maps a candidate charging protocol to a current profile,
simulates the resulting battery trajectory, and returns objective and
constraint values to a constrained Pareto search. Figure~\ref{fig:charging_problem}
summarizes this separation between protocol representation, physical
evaluation, and optimization feedback.
\input{figures/fig_charging_design_problem}

\subsection{SOC-Domain Multistage Protocol Representation}

Following established multistage constant-current formulations
for fast-charging design~\cite{ref14,ref13}, a protocol is represented by its
current levels and its SOC allocation rather than by stage durations. The SOC
representation makes switching boundaries explicit, while each duration
remains a dependent quantity determined by transferred charge and current.
For an indexed $K$-stage representation over $[s_0,s_f]$, let
$\Delta s_k$ be the SOC span of stage $k$. The independent coordinates,
dependent terminal span, and total charging time are written compactly as
\[
\begin{aligned}
\btheta
&=[I_1,\ldots,I_K,\Delta s_1,\ldots,\Delta s_{K-1}]^\top,\\
\Delta s_K
&=s_f-s_0-\sum_{k=1}^{K-1}\Delta s_k,\qquad
t_c(\btheta)=\kappa_t Q_{\mathrm{eff}}
\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{\Delta s_k}{I_k^{\mathrm{cell}}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here $Q_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is the effective cell capacity,
$I_k^{\mathrm{cell}}=\phi_{\mathrm{cell}}(I_k)$ is the deterministic mapping
from a protocol coordinate to physical cell current, and $\kappa_t$ is the
charge-to-time unit conversion. The map $\phi_{\mathrm{cell}}$ accounts for
the current convention of each cell parameterization and is fixed before
optimization.

Let $\Theta_{\mathrm{proto}}$ contain the box-feasible current and independent
SOC-span coordinates for which
$\Delta s_K\geq\Delta s_K^{\min}$. The dependent-span identity completes the
prescribed SOC interval, while this lower bound excludes degenerate terminal
stages. This indexed representation applies to any selected finite stage
count; the optimization method does not rely on a particular number of
segments. A stricter preferred span $\Delta s_K^{\mathrm{soft}}$ is used only
to rank LLM warm-start candidates and is not an additional simulator
constraint.

\subsection{Objective and Constraint Formulation}

For a protocol $\btheta$, the simulator returns charging time, peak temperature
rise, and a protocol-level degradation proxy. The constrained multiobjective
problem is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{\btheta\in\Theta_{\mathrm{proto}}}\quad
&\mathbf f(\btheta)=
\begin{bmatrix}
t_c(\btheta)\\
\Delta T_p(\btheta)\\
D_{\mathrm{chg}}(\btheta)
\end{bmatrix},\\
\mathrm{s.t.}\quad
&V_{\mathrm{peak}}(\btheta)\le V_{\max},\\
&T_{\mathrm{peak}}(\btheta)\le T_{\max}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cmop}
\end{equation}
All three objectives are minimized. The protocol domain encodes current bounds
and completion of the prescribed SOC interval, while the simulator checks the
path-dependent voltage and temperature limits $V_{\max}$ and $T_{\max}$ fixed
for the chosen cell model. A protocol that violates either limit or fails to
complete the interval is marked infeasible and excluded from the evaluated
Pareto set.
