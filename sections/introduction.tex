\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Fast and reliable charging is a central requirement for lithium-ion batteries
used in electric vehicles and energy-storage systems.
Increasing the charging current can shorten charging time, but it also
intensifies heat generation and degradation, creating inherent trade-offs among
charging speed, thermal safety, and battery durability~\cite{ref1,ref2,refYang}.
Because the preferred compromise varies across operating scenarios,
charging-protocol design is naturally a constrained multiobjective optimization
problem whose desired output is a set of Pareto-nondominated protocols rather
than a single schedule selected by fixed preferences.

Accurate evaluation of a charging protocol is costly.
Physical cycling requires substantial time and laboratory resources,
particularly when degradation is included.
Physics-based electrochemical--thermal simulation reduces reliance on repeated
experiments, but each candidate still requires the numerical solution of
coupled battery dynamics.
Consequently, the number of simulator evaluations becomes a major computational
bottleneck.
Bayesian optimization (BO) is well suited to this setting because a
probabilistic surrogate and an acquisition function can prioritize informative
evaluations under a limited budget~\cite{ref3,ref11,ref12,ref14}.
For multiple objectives, decomposition-based multiobjective BO (MOBO) can
sequentially explore different trade-off directions and approximate a Pareto
set with relatively few expensive evaluations~\cite{ref13,refZhu}.
Gaussian-process-assisted evolutionary optimization has likewise demonstrated
the value of surrogate modeling for sample-efficient charging
design~\cite{refGPA}.

Existing studies improve BO-based charging design mainly by refining the search
strategy or by incorporating additional task knowledge.
Mesh-adaptive and parallel acquisition strategies improve the allocation of
new evaluations~\cite{refDong,refBLASTS}, whereas digital twins, aging models,
electrochemical constraints, and related physics-based information provide
task-specific numerical guidance~\cite{refDigitalTwin,refPrBO,refYoon}.
These approaches are effective when suitable models or historical numerical
information are available.
They are less direct, however, for incorporating qualitative charging knowledge
such as plausible multistage current patterns, SOC allocation heuristics, and
thermal--degradation trade-offs.
This limitation matters most at the beginning of BO: with only a few evaluated
protocols, the surrogate is highly sensitive to the initial design, and poor
initial samples may consume a substantial fraction of a tight simulation
budget before informative regions are reached.

Large language models (LLMs) provide a possible interface for translating such
textual knowledge into optimization suggestions.
LLAMBO introduced LLM-assisted warm starts, candidate generation, and
surrogate-related reasoning for BO~\cite{ref10}.
Subsequent studies have considered point or region preferences and selective use
of inexpensive LLM predictions~\cite{ref16,refLABO}.
LLM-assisted BO has also been applied to battery parameter
identification~\cite{refKuaiLLMBO}, while broader studies show that the value of
LLM guidance depends strongly on alignment with the target scientific
domain~\cite{refSoberLLMBO}.
For multiobjective fast-charging design, two aspects remain insufficiently
explored.
First, raw LLM suggestions must be converted into a protocol-domain-valid and
diverse initial design before they can provide useful information to BO.
Second, if LLM guidance is retained during sequential search, the recommendation
should reflect the active multiobjective trade-off rather than remain
independent of the current decomposition direction and optimization state.
In either case, LLM suggestions should guide where the optimizer searches,
while simulator evaluations continue to determine objective values and
path-dependent physical feasibility.

This paper proposes \emph{LLMBO-MO}, which augments a ParEGO-style
decomposition-based MOBO backbone with two LLM-guided search mechanisms.
The main contributions are summarized as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item An \emph{LLM-informed initialization} mechanism is developed to
    translate battery-domain textual knowledge into numerical charging
    protocols. Protocol-domain screening, heuristic ranking, and
    diversity-aware selection are used to construct a valid and
    decision-space-diverse initial portfolio, and every selected protocol is
    evaluated by the battery simulator before entering the BO database.

    \item A \emph{weight-conditioned regional guidance} mechanism is developed
    for the early sequential stage. The active decomposition weight and current
    optimization state are used to obtain a bounded point or region preference.
    Accepted regions contribute additional restarts to numerical
    expected-improvement optimization, while the original acquisition search is
    retained as an automatic fallback.

    \item Fixed-budget numerical studies evaluate the complete method and its
    two mechanisms. The main Chen2020 and Ecker2015 comparisons use the same
    five seeds for every method. On Chen2020, LLMBO-MO attains a mean
    final HV of \ChenLLMBOMean\,$\pm$\,\ChenLLMBOStd\ versus
    \ChenParEGOMean\,$\pm$\,\ChenParEGOStd\ for ParEGO after 56 simulator
    evaluations. Additional matched-seed studies isolate the contribution of
    battery-specific initialization and the complementary role of regional
    guidance.
\end{enumerate}
