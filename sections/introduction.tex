\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Lithium-ion batteries are widely used in electric vehicles and energy-storage
systems because of their high energy density and long cycle life.
Nevertheless, long charging time and charging-induced degradation remain two
major obstacles to convenient and durable battery operation.
Increasing the charging current shortens charging time but intensifies heat
generation and electrochemical side reactions, thereby creating inherent
trade-offs among charging speed, thermal behavior, and degradation
severity~\cite{ref1,ref2,refYang}.
At the same time, practical charging protocols must satisfy operational
constraints on current, voltage, temperature, and state of charge (SOC).
Consequently, fast-charging protocol design is naturally a constrained
multiobjective optimization problem whose desired output is a set of
Pareto-nondominated protocols capable of accommodating different user
preferences.

One challenge in charging-process design is the high cost of obtaining reliable
objective evaluations.
Physical cycling experiments are time-consuming, especially when degradation is
included in the evaluation.
Model-based optimization can reduce this experimental burden, but
electrochemical--thermal models still require repeated numerical solution of
coupled dynamic equations.
Previous studies have therefore investigated efficient battery models,
evolutionary optimization, reinforcement learning, and Bayesian optimization
(BO) for charging design.
For example, BO has been used in closed-loop battery experiments and
simulation-based charging optimization~\cite{ref11,ref12,ref14}, while
multiobjective BO has been introduced to search for charging protocols that
balance time, degradation, and operational constraints~\cite{ref13,refZhu}.
Gaussian-process-assisted evolutionary optimization has also been used to
accelerate multiobjective charging-process design and to approximate a diverse
Pareto set~\cite{refGPA}.
These studies show that sample-efficient optimization is a promising direction
for charging design when the evaluation budget is limited.

BO is particularly suitable for expensive black-box optimization because it
constructs a probabilistic surrogate from previously evaluated samples and uses
an acquisition function to select new evaluations~\cite{ref3}.
However, this strength also exposes an important weakness in the early stage of
optimization.
With only a few observations, the surrogate is highly sensitive to the initial
design, and uninformative starting points may consume a substantial fraction of
the available simulation budget before useful structure of the charging problem
is learned.
Recent charging studies have attempted to improve BO efficiency through
mesh-adaptive search, digital twins, parallel acquisition strategies, aging-model
priors, and additional electrochemical constraints
~\cite{refDong,refDigitalTwin,refBLASTS,refPrBO,refYoon}.
These approaches demonstrate the value of prior information, but the injected
knowledge is generally numerical: it comes from calibrated models, measurements,
or structured historical data.
Such information may not always be available for every cell and operating
condition.

Beyond battery charging, learning-based adaptive mechanisms have also been
studied in constrained multiobjective optimization, including the selection of
constraint-handling operators and dynamic response strategies from optimization
history~\cite{refPengTemporal,refPengDynamic}.
These methods demonstrate the value of learned search guidance, but they do not
address how external textual knowledge should be incorporated when objective
evaluations are expensive.

Battery charging also contains substantial qualitative knowledge that is not
naturally represented as numerical observations.
Descriptions of multistage protocol geometry, feasible current ranges, SOC
allocation, and qualitative thermal--degradation trade-offs can all provide
useful clues about promising regions of the design space.
Large language models (LLMs), which encode broad textual knowledge and exhibit
few-shot contextual reasoning, provide a possible interface for converting such
knowledge into optimization guidance.
LLAMBO showed that LLMs can improve BO through zero-shot warm starting,
candidate generation, and surrogate modeling, with pronounced benefits in
sparse-observation regimes~\cite{ref10}.
More recent work has introduced LLM-generated point and region preferences into
the BO loop~\cite{ref16}, while LABO interprets inexpensive LLM predictions as a
low-cost information source that can be combined with expensive experimental
evaluations~\cite{refLABO}.
In battery research, LLM-enhanced BO has also been applied to parameter
identification, where battery-domain prompts are used for warm starting,
surrogate enhancement, and candidate sampling~\cite{refKuaiLLMBO}.
More generally, evaluations of LLM-assisted scientific BO have shown that the
usefulness of language-model priors depends on their alignment with the target
domain~\cite{refSoberLLMBO}.

Despite this progress, directly transferring existing LLM--BO designs to
fast-charging optimization raises an additional issue.
An LLM-generated charging protocol is not a feasibility certificate, and an
LLM-generated objective value is neither a battery measurement nor a
simulator-validated observation.
If such values are admitted directly into the surrogate training set or Pareto
archive, language-model uncertainty becomes entangled with the numerical
evidence used to determine optimization progress.
This distinction is especially important in constrained multiobjective charging
design, because erroneous objective values may influence not only surrogate
fitting but also dominance relationships among protocols.

From the above literature review, three findings are summarized as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Fast-charging design requires efficient multiobjective search under a
    limited simulation budget. BO is well suited to this setting, but its early
    search remains sensitive to a small initial design.

    \item Prior knowledge can improve search efficiency, yet existing
    battery-optimization methods mainly rely on calibrated numerical models,
    measurements, or structured historical data. Qualitative charging knowledge
    remains difficult to incorporate into the BO loop.

    \item LLMs provide a flexible interface to such textual knowledge, but their
    outputs should not be assigned the same evidential status as
    simulator-evaluated objective values in constrained battery optimization.
\end{enumerate}

Based on these observations, this paper proposes LLMBO-MO for multiobjective
battery fast-charging protocol design.
The central idea is to separate \emph{search guidance} from \emph{optimization
evidence}: the LLM is allowed to suggest where the optimizer should search,
whereas only the battery simulator determines objective values, feasibility, GP
training targets, and Pareto dominance.
Before sequential BO begins, the LLM generates a pool of candidate protocols
from a natural-language description of the battery model, decision variables,
objectives, and charging heuristics.
These candidates are repaired to the protocol domain, screened, and selected as
a diversity-aware initial portfolio before simulator evaluation.
During an early guidance window, the LLM may additionally provide
weight-conditioned point or region preferences.
Rejected or unavailable guidance falls back to ordinary numerical expected
improvement, and the LLM ceases to intervene after the guidance window.

The main contributions are summarized as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item An LLM-augmented multiobjective BO framework is developed for
    simulation-limited battery fast-charging design.
    The framework explicitly separates advisory language-model knowledge from
    simulator-validated objective evidence, preventing unevaluated LLM outputs
    from being used as GP targets or Pareto values.

    \item A protocol-domain-screened and diversity-aware LLM warm-start strategy
    is developed to exploit battery-domain textual knowledge in the
    evidence-scarce initialization stage.
    This initialization prior is complemented by bounded, weight-conditioned
    regional guidance that refines early acquisition search while retaining a
    numerical BO fallback and without introducing pseudo-observations.

    \item Comprehensive numerical studies are conducted on the Chen2020 and
    Ecker2015 parameterizations.
    In addition to fixed-budget comparisons with ParEGO, NSGA-II, DISK, and
    PIMD, a controlled prompt study verifies the value of battery-specific
    initialization knowledge, while a matched three-seed component ablation
    shows that the complete LLMBO-MO configuration attains the highest
    short-budget mean HV, the smallest observed dispersion, and the best result
    in two of the three matched seeds.
\end{enumerate}
