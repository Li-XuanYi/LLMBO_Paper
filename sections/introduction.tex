\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Lithium-ion batteries have been widely adopted in electric vehicles (EVs) and
energy-storage systems due to their high energy density and long cycle life
\cite{nref1}.
With the rapid development of EVs, increasing demand has been placed on fast
charging. However, increasing the charging current generally leads to greater
heat generation and accelerated battery degradation, which can adversely
affect both battery safety and service life.
Therefore, achieving an appropriate balance among charging time, safety, and
battery degradation has become an important problem in lithium-ion battery
charging design \cite{nref2,nref3}.
Such a design problem typically involves multiple conflicting objectives and
is subject to operational constraints on charging current, voltage,
temperature, state of charge (SOC), and other battery states.
It can therefore be naturally formulated as a constrained multiobjective
optimization problem (CMOP) \cite{nref2}.

Various optimization methods have been developed for charging-strategy
design.
For example, deep reinforcement learning has been applied to multiobjective
battery charging strategy design \cite{nref5}.
Physics-based optimization methods describe the internal battery dynamics
using electrochemical, thermal, and degradation models, and then optimize
the charging strategy using numerical optimization or model predictive
control (MPC).
For example, Liu \emph{et al.} investigated multiobjective charging
optimization using an electrochemical--thermal--aging model
\cite{nref2}, while Hwang \emph{et al.} combined an enhanced single-particle
model with MPC to optimize charging current while accounting for battery
degradation mechanisms \cite{nref4}.
Although these approaches provide physically interpretable optimization
frameworks, they generally require repeated evaluations of the underlying
battery model.
High-fidelity electrochemical models can involve complex coupled dynamic
equations, making large-scale searches computationally expensive.
Consequently, the number of candidate protocols that can be evaluated within
a practical computational budget is often limited.

Bayesian optimization (BO) is particularly suitable for expensive
black-box optimization problems because it can identify promising solutions
with relatively few evaluations of the true objective functions.
BO typically constructs a probabilistic surrogate model, such as a Gaussian
process (GP), to approximate the objective function and uses an acquisition
function to balance exploitation and exploration \cite{nref6}.
This property is well aligned with battery charging optimization, where the
evaluation of a candidate charging protocol may require computationally
expensive high-fidelity simulations or time-consuming battery experiments.
Attia \emph{et al.} demonstrated that a closed-loop machine-learning
framework based on BO can substantially reduce the number of experiments
required for charging-protocol optimization \cite{nref3}.
Jiang \emph{et al.} further applied BO to lithium-ion battery fast-charging
design and demonstrated its ability to identify high-quality charging
protocols with a limited number of evaluations \cite{nref7}.
Wang and Jiang subsequently extended this line of research to multiobjective
charging design by simultaneously considering charging time and battery
degradation \cite{nref8}.

Despite its sample efficiency, however, BO does not necessarily make
effective use of a limited evaluation budget throughout the entire
optimization process.
In particular, during the initial stage, the surrogate model must be
constructed from only a small number of initial observations.
When these observations contain little useful task-specific prior
information, BO may spend a substantial number of evaluations exploring
low-quality regions before reaching promising parts of the search space,
resulting in a pronounced \emph{cold-start phase}.
Ramachandran \emph{et al.} showed that standard BO in relatively large
search spaces may require exploration of multiple low-quality regions before
approaching the optimum, and that prior knowledge regarding the location of
high-quality solutions can effectively alleviate this issue \cite{nref9}.
This limitation is particularly relevant to battery charging design because
each unproductive evaluation may incur the cost of an additional
high-fidelity simulation or physical experiment.

In addition to initialization, effectively restricting and guiding the
subsequent search region is another important factor affecting BO efficiency.
Conventional BO generally optimizes the acquisition function over the entire
decision space.
For complex search spaces, such global acquisition optimization can lead to
excessive exploration of regions that are unlikely to contain high-quality
solutions.
To address this issue, Eriksson \emph{et al.} proposed TuRBO, which employs
local probabilistic models and trust regions to concentrate the search on
more promising regions and demonstrated improved efficiency on challenging
black-box optimization problems \cite{nref10}.
More recently, Namura and Takemori proposed Regional Expected Improvement
(REI), which identifies regions that are more likely to contain the global
optimum and uses them to determine the trust region for subsequent search
\cite{nref11}.
These studies suggest that if useful information about \emph{promising
regions} can be obtained and incorporated into BO, unnecessary exploration
can be reduced and optimization efficiency can be improved under a limited
evaluation budget.

Recent advances in large language models (LLMs) provide a new opportunity to
incorporate task-related prior knowledge into BO.
LLMs have demonstrated strong capabilities in contextual understanding,
few-shot learning, and knowledge utilization, enabling them to reason about
candidate solutions from natural-language descriptions of a task.
Unlike purely data-driven optimization, which relies primarily on numerical
observations collected during optimization, an LLM can jointly consider
domain descriptions, variable semantics, physical constraints, and previous
optimization information when suggesting potentially promising candidates.
Yang \emph{et al.} proposed Optimization by PROmpting (OPRO), which provides
an LLM with historical candidates and their objective values and uses the LLM
to iteratively generate new optimization candidates \cite{nref13}.
More directly, Liu \emph{et al.} proposed LLAMBO, which integrates LLMs into
BO and investigated their use for \emph{zero-shot warm starting}, surrogate
modeling, and candidate sampling.
Their results demonstrated the potential of LLM-derived prior information
to improve BO performance, particularly during the early stage when only a
small number of observations are available \cite{nref12}.

Despite these developments, the use of LLMs to assist BO for lithium-ion
battery charging design remains insufficiently explored.
Compared with generic black-box or hyperparameter optimization problems,
battery charging protocols have explicit physical meanings, while the
relationships among different charging stages and multiple optimization
objectives contain substantial domain knowledge.
For example, battery-domain knowledge can provide useful information about
plausible charging-current profiles, SOC allocation across charging stages,
and trade-offs among charging time, thermal behavior, and degradation.
How to exploit such knowledge to improve the \emph{initial search quality}
of BO, and further use it to provide \emph{region-level guidance} according
to the current optimization state, remains an open research question.
Under a limited evaluation budget, these two aspects correspond to two
distinct sources of inefficiency: the \emph{cold-start problem} at the
beginning of BO and potentially excessive exploration during the subsequent
global search.

To address these issues, this paper proposes
\emph{LLM-guided Multiobjective Bayesian Optimization (LLMBO-MO)}, a
framework for multiobjective charging-protocol design of lithium-ion
batteries.
The proposed method is built upon a decomposition-based multiobjective
Bayesian optimization framework and uses an LLM as a provider of
domain knowledge and search priors, while retaining BO as the primary
numerical optimization mechanism.
Specifically, an \emph{LLM-informed warm-start} strategy first uses
battery-charging knowledge to generate promising and sufficiently diverse
initial charging protocols, thereby improving the initial state of BO.
Subsequently, a \emph{weight-conditioned regional guidance} mechanism
generates promising search regions according to the current multiobjective
preference and the optimization state.
These regions are then used to guide the early-stage optimization of the
acquisition function, with the objective of reducing unnecessary exploration
of low-quality regions.

The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:

\begin{itemize}
    \item We propose \emph{LLMBO-MO}, an LLM-guided multiobjective Bayesian
    optimization framework for lithium-ion battery charging-protocol design.
    The framework incorporates battery-domain knowledge encoded in an LLM
    into the BO process while preserving BO as the main numerical optimization
    framework, providing additional prior information for charging-protocol
    search under a limited evaluation budget.

    \item We develop an \emph{LLM-informed warm-start} strategy that uses an
    LLM to generate promising and decision-space-diverse initial charging
    protocols based on charging variables, physical constraints, and
    battery-domain knowledge.
    This strategy aims to improve the quality of the initial design compared
    with uninformed initialization and thereby alleviate the cold-start
    problem of BO.

    \item We introduce a \emph{weight-conditioned regional guidance}
    mechanism that identifies promising regions according to the current
    multiobjective decomposition direction and optimization state.
    The resulting regional information is incorporated into early-stage
    acquisition-function optimization to reduce unnecessary exploration of
    low-quality regions.

    \item We conduct systematic multiobjective charging-optimization
    experiments and compare LLMBO-MO with representative multiobjective BO
    methods.
    Ablation experiments are further performed to investigate the respective
    contributions of the LLM-informed warm-start and
    weight-conditioned regional guidance mechanisms under a limited
    simulation-evaluation budget.
\end{itemize}
