\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Lithium-ion batteries are central to electric vehicles and other high-power
energy-storage systems because of their high energy density and long cycle
life~\cite{ref1}. Fast charging improves vehicle availability, but increasing
the charge rate can intensify heat generation, lithium plating, and
solid-electrolyte-interphase growth~\cite{ref2,refYang}. Protocol design must
therefore balance charging time, peak temperature rise, and a degradation
proxy while respecting voltage, temperature, and
state-of-charge (SOC) limits.

The central computational difficulty is the scarcity of informative protocol
evaluations. Physical cycling tests are slow and costly, and even reduced-order
electrochemical--thermal simulation limits how many schedules can be assessed
in one design campaign. Bayesian optimization (BO) is well suited to this
regime because it uses a probabilistic surrogate and an acquisition rule to
allocate expensive evaluations~\cite{ref3}. Closed-loop cell studies and
simulation-based charging design have demonstrated useful performance under
restricted evaluation budgets~\cite{ref11,ref12,ref14}. Multiobjective
extensions expose the trade-off surface instead of permanently collapsing user
preferences into one objective~\cite{ref13}.

A persistent challenge is BO's cold start. Before task-specific observations
accumulate, a small initial design and a generic statistical prior strongly
shape the surrogate and acquisition function. Model-prior-guided charging
optimization can improve this regime~\cite{refPrBO}, but constructing a
calibrated prior for every cell and operating condition is demanding.
LLM-assisted BO offers a complementary interface: recent methods use large
language models (LLMs) for initialization, region preference, or low-fidelity
prediction~\cite{ref10,ref16,refLABO}. In constrained battery design, however,
an LLM response is neither a measurement nor proof of feasibility. The key
question is therefore not whether an LLM can propose a protocol, but how its
contextual preference can be used without transferring numerical authority
away from the simulator and optimizer.

LLMBO-MO answers this question through a deliberately asymmetric interface.
At initialization, repaired and protocol-domain-screened LLM proposals
contribute to a diverse warm-start portfolio. During an early guidance window,
weight-conditioned point or region preferences can supply acquisition
restarts and, after covariance and magnitude checks, a bounded adjustment to
the acquisition mean. Only simulator outputs enter the Gaussian-process (GP)
training database. Unusable guidance has zero numerical effect, and the
optimizer recovers ordinary expected improvement (EI). Augmented
Tchebycheff scalarization distributes the search across charging time, peak
temperature rise, and a protocol-level degradation proxy.

The main contributions are as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A two-touchpoint architecture is formulated for constrained battery
multiobjective BO. It separates language-model preferences from simulator
observations and retains numerical authority over protocol validity,
surrogate fitting, and the evaluated Pareto set.

\item A diversity-aware warm-start objective and a weight-conditioned regional
preference operator are integrated with an explicit, clipped
posterior-covariance coupling. The formulation bounds the influence of
text-derived guidance and reduces exactly to standard EI whenever validation
fails.

\item The complete configuration is evaluated against ParEGO under matched
56-simulation budgets and seeds 8409--8413 on two SPMe parameterizations.
Common within-benchmark hypervolume definitions and a five-seed component
study quantify both configuration-level performance and the relative
contributions of warm starting and regional guidance.
\end{enumerate}
