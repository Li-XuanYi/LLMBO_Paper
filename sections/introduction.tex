\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Fast-charging protocol design seeks a set of current schedules that expose the
trade-off among charging time, thermal rise, and degradation severity while
respecting operational limits. This task is central to electric-vehicle and
high-power storage applications, where higher current improves availability
but intensifies heat generation, lithium plating, and
solid-electrolyte-interphase growth~\cite{ref1,ref2,refYang}. Its main
computational difficulty is the scarcity of informative evaluations: physical
cycling is slow and costly, and even reduced-order electrochemical--thermal
simulation restricts the number of protocols that can be assessed in one
campaign. The resulting problem is a constrained, expensive, multiobjective
search in which the quality of early decisions matters disproportionately.

Bayesian optimization (BO) is a natural numerical backbone for this setting
because it allocates evaluations through a probabilistic surrogate and an
acquisition function~\cite{ref3}. Closed-loop cell studies and simulation
campaigns have demonstrated BO for charging-protocol selection under limited
budgets~\cite{ref11,ref12,ref14}, while multiobjective formulations expose the
trade-off surface instead of committing to a single user preference
beforehand~\cite{ref13}. ParEGO provides a particularly economical backbone by
combining scalarization, one Gaussian process (GP), and expected improvement
(EI)~\cite{ref4}; direct hypervolume methods such as qEHVI and qNEHVI provide
alternative multiobjective acquisitions~\cite{refQEHVI,refQNEHVI}. Across
these methods, however, a cold-start limitation remains: before the surrogate
has accumulated task-specific observations, random initialization and early
acquisition choices can consume a substantial fraction of a small budget.

Existing charging-design accelerators obtain early guidance from calibrated
models, related-task data, or physical measurements. Examples include
semi-empirical model priors~\cite{refPrBO}, mesh-adaptive search~\cite{refDong},
online digital twins~\cite{refDigitalTwin}, parallel acquisition strategies
~\cite{refBLASTS}, and early-life predictors coupled to hypervolume-based
BO~\cite{refZhu}. These approaches are numerically grounded, but their prior
information may require cell-specific data collection or calibration. A
complementary question is whether broad natural-language knowledge can guide
the data-scarce phase without changing the numerical standard by which a
protocol is accepted.

Recent LLM-assisted BO methods assign different authority to language-model
output. LLAMBO uses LLMs for initialization, surrogate reasoning, and candidate
sampling~\cite{ref10}; preference-guided BO alters the surrogate mean using
LLM-specified points or regions~\cite{ref16}; and LABO treats LLM predictions
as a low-fidelity information source~\cite{refLABO}. These designs demonstrate
several useful interfaces, but constrained engineering optimization raises a
specific risk: an unverified numerical prediction or an unbounded preference
shift can enter the evidence path or dominate acquisition. The technical
challenge addressed here is therefore not how to let an LLM replace the
optimizer, but how to translate its contextual suggestions into temporary,
bounded search preferences while leaving all objective and feasibility claims
to the simulator.

We address this challenge with LLMBO-MO, an asymmetric two-touchpoint interface
between an LLM and multiobjective BO. Before optimization, a screened warm
start parses, repairs, ranks, and diversifies LLM-proposed protocols, then
combines accepted proposals with protocol-domain-valid exploration points.
During an early guidance window, a weight-conditioned interface converts an
LLM point or region into acquisition restarts and, only after validation, an
explicitly capped posterior-mean adjustment. Rejected or unusable advice
recovers the corresponding numerical fallback: protocol-domain-valid initial
sampling or ordinary EI. At every stage, only simulator-evaluated protocols
train the GP and enter the Pareto set.

The numerical study evaluates LLMBO-MO on two battery parameterizations under
a common simulation budget. The complete framework obtains higher mean final
hypervolume in both cases. Additional analyses show that the screened warm
start provides the main individual contribution and that prompt design affects
initialization quality. The observed gains are modest and are interpreted as
evidence for the proposed interface rather than uniform superiority.

The main contributions are as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item We formulate an asymmetric LLM--BO interface in which language-model
output can influence \emph{where} to search but cannot determine \emph{what}
constitutes evaluated evidence or enters the Pareto set.

\item We develop two controlled operators for the data-scarce phase: a
protocol-domain-screened, diversity-aware warm start and a weight-conditioned
regional preference operator with geometry checks, finite-horizon attenuation,
and an explicit pointwise shift cap. Both have well-defined numerical
fallbacks when advice is rejected.

\item We evaluate the framework on two SPMe parameterizations and complement
the main comparison with component, prompt, and objective-preprocessing
analyses. The study characterizes both the benefit and the practical limits of
LLM guidance without making hardware or calibrated-lifetime claims.
\end{enumerate}
