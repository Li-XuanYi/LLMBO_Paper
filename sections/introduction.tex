\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Lithium-ion batteries are central to electric vehicles and high-power
energy-storage systems owing to their high energy density and long cycle
life~\cite{ref1}. Fast charging improves vehicle availability, but increasing
the charge rate intensifies heat generation, lithium plating, and
solid-electrolyte-interphase growth, creating a well-known trilemma among
charging speed, thermal safety, and cycle life~\cite{ref2,refYang}.
Protocol design must therefore balance charging time, peak temperature rise,
and a degradation proxy while respecting voltage, temperature, and
state-of-charge (SOC) limits.
The binding computational constraint is the scarcity of informative
evaluations: physical cycling tests are slow and costly, and even
reduced-order electrochemical--thermal simulation limits how many candidate
protocols can be assessed in one design campaign.
Identifying Pareto-optimal protocols under this tightly restricted evaluation
budget remains an open challenge.

Bayesian optimization (BO) addresses sample-efficient black-box optimization
by using a probabilistic surrogate and an acquisition function to allocate
expensive evaluations~\cite{ref3}.
In battery charging design, BO has demonstrated effective protocol
optimization under restricted budgets in both closed-loop cell
studies~\cite{ref11} and simulation-based campaigns~\cite{ref12,ref14}.
Multiobjective extensions expose the full trade-off surface across charging
speed, thermal safety, and degradation, rather than collapsing user
preferences into a single objective~\cite{ref13}.
However, BO's cold-start phase---before sufficient task-specific observations
accumulate---remains a persistent bottleneck.
The initial design and the surrogate prior strongly shape early acquisition
decisions, making informative initialization especially valuable when the
total evaluation budget is small.
Model-prior-guided optimization can mitigate cold start by incorporating a
calibrated electrochemical prior~\cite{refPrBO}, but constructing such a prior
for every cell chemistry and operating condition is itself a substantial
experimental undertaking.

Large language models (LLMs) offer a complementary route to informative
initialization.
Pretrained on internet-scale data, modern LLMs encode broad domain knowledge
and exhibit strong few-shot reasoning, enabling them to propose plausible
solutions from natural-language task descriptions without per-task
calibration.
Recent work has integrated LLMs into BO for initialization, region
preference, and candidate scoring~\cite{ref10,ref16,refLABO}.
In constrained battery design, the key technical challenge is to translate
contextual LLM suggestions into bounded search preferences while retaining
simulator-based objective and feasibility verification.
This separation allows broad language-model knowledge to complement
cell-specific numerical evidence without changing the optimizer's evidence
standard.

LLMBO-MO answers this question through an asymmetric interface that strictly
separates advisory LLM input from evidence-grade simulator output.
The framework has two LLM touchpoints, both confined to an early guidance
window.
The first is a diversity-aware warm-start: the LLM proposes candidate
protocols in natural language, which are parsed into numerical ranges,
repaired for physical consistency, and screened against the protocol domain.
Candidates that pass screening seed a diverse initial portfolio alongside random
samples.
This supplies the BO surrogate with more informative starting points than
pure random initialization, after which simulator evaluation supplies the
objective and feasibility evidence.
In contrast to model-prior-guided approaches, the LLM warm-start requires
no per-cell calibration and relies only on a natural-language description
of the task.

The second touchpoint operates during early BO iterations.
The LLM supplies weight-conditioned preferences that indicate, for a given
scalarization weight, which regions of the protocol space are likely to favor
one objective over another.
These preferences are translated into acquisition restarts and a bounded
adjustment to the acquisition mean, gated by covariance and magnitude checks.
The adjustment combines confidence and covariance gates, early-window
attenuation, and an explicit pointwise cap in standardized target units;
gate-rejected guidance recovers standard expected improvement (EI).
Once the early guidance window closes, the optimizer continues as ordinary
multiobjective BO with no further LLM involvement.
Simulator-evaluated protocols exclusively form the Gaussian-process training
set throughout.

We evaluate LLMBO-MO against ParEGO~\cite{ref4} under a matched budget of
56 simulator evaluations on two SPMe parameterizations---Chen2020 and
Ecker2015---using five fixed seeds (8409--8413) and a common within-benchmark
hypervolume definition.
LLMBO-MO attains higher mean final hypervolume on both parameterizations, with
mean improvements of 1.7\% on Chen2020 (up to 5.1\% in a paired seed) and
17.8\% on Ecker2015.
A component decomposition indicates that the screened LLM warm-start alone
accounts for the majority of the performance gain, while a separate ten-seed
prompt-design ablation confirms the statistical reliability of LLM-derived
initialization ($p < 0.01$).

The main contributions are as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item An asymmetric two-touchpoint architecture for LLM-assisted
multiobjective BO that separates advisory language-model preferences from
simulator-validated evidence. The LLM influences \emph{where} the optimizer
searches but never determines \emph{what} enters the Pareto set.

\item A screened warm-start operator and a bounded regional guidance
operator, each designed with explicit influence limits. Both operators
retain their standard BO counterparts---random initialization and ordinary
EI, respectively---as validation-gated fallbacks for rejected advice.

\item A matched-budget evaluation on two SPMe parameterizations, a five-seed
component decomposition, and a ten-seed prompt-design ablation ($p < 0.01$).
LLMBO-MO attains higher mean final HV on both parameterizations, and the
screened LLM warm-start is identified as the primary individual contributor.
This finding establishes a performance baseline for future LLM--BO integration
in constrained battery design.
\end{enumerate}
