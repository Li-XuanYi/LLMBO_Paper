\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Fast and reliable charging is a central requirement for lithium-ion batteries
used in electric vehicles and energy-storage systems.
Increasing the charging current can shorten charging time, but it also
intensifies heat generation and degradation, creating inherent trade-offs
among charging speed, thermal safety, and battery
durability~\cite{ref1,ref2,refYang}.
Because the preferred compromise varies across operating scenarios,
charging-protocol design is naturally a constrained multiobjective
optimization problem whose desired output is a set of Pareto-nondominated
protocols rather than a single schedule selected by fixed preferences.

Accurate evaluation of a charging protocol is costly.
Physical cycling requires substantial time and laboratory resources,
particularly when degradation is included.
Physics-based electrochemical--thermal simulation reduces reliance on repeated
experiments~\cite{ref6,ref7}, but each candidate still requires the numerical
solution of coupled battery dynamics.
Consequently, the number of simulator evaluations becomes a major
computational bottleneck.
Bayesian optimization (BO) is well suited to this setting because a
probabilistic surrogate and an acquisition function can prioritize informative
evaluations under a limited budget~\cite{ref3,ref11,ref12,ref14}.
For multiple objectives, decomposition-based multiobjective BO (MOBO) can
sequentially explore different trade-off directions and approximate a Pareto
set with relatively few expensive evaluations~\cite{ref4,ref13,refZhu}.

Existing studies improve BO-based charging design mainly by refining the
search strategy or by incorporating additional task knowledge.
Mesh-adaptive and parallel acquisition strategies improve the allocation of
new evaluations~\cite{refDong,refBLASTS}, whereas digital twins, aging
models, electrochemical constraints, and related physics-based information
provide task-specific numerical
guidance~\cite{refDigitalTwin,refPrBO,refYoon}.
These approaches are effective when suitable models or historical numerical
information are available.
They are less direct, however, for incorporating qualitative charging
knowledge such as plausible multistage current patterns, SOC allocation
heuristics, and thermal--degradation trade-offs, which is exactly the
information that practitioners carry when they design a new charging
protocol.
This limitation matters most at the beginning of BO: with only a few
evaluated protocols, the surrogate is highly sensitive to the initial
design, and poor initial samples may consume a substantial fraction of a
tight simulation budget before informative regions are reached.

Large language models (LLMs) provide a possible interface for translating
such textual knowledge into optimization suggestions.
LLAMBO introduced LLM-assisted warm starts, candidate generation, and
surrogate-related reasoning for BO~\cite{ref10}.
Subsequent studies have considered point or region preferences and selective
use of inexpensive LLM predictions~\cite{ref16,refLABO}.
LLM-assisted BO has also been applied to battery parameter
identification~\cite{refKuaiLLMBO}, while broader studies show that the
value of LLM guidance depends strongly on alignment with the target
scientific domain~\cite{refSoberLLMBO}.
For multiobjective fast-charging design, two aspects remain insufficiently
explored.
First, raw LLM suggestions must be converted into a protocol-domain-valid
and diverse initial design before they can provide useful information to BO.
Second, if LLM guidance is retained during sequential search, the
recommendation should reflect the active multiobjective trade-off rather
than remain independent of the current decomposition direction and
optimization state.
In either case, LLM suggestions should guide \emph{where} the optimizer
searches, while simulator evaluations continue to determine objective
values and path-dependent physical feasibility.

The closest prior work in fast-charging protocol design follows a related
but distinct strategy.
EIMO~\cite{refEIMO} transfers \emph{optimization experience} from previously
optimized state-of-health (SOH) levels to a new SOH level through a
transfer acquisition function, and GPA-MOBO~\cite{refGPA} accelerates a
decomposition-based MOBO backbone using a Gaussian-process surrogate with
multiple user-preference directions.
Both target the same application and share the same
decomposition--GP--EI numerical backbone as the present work.
LLMBO-MO differs from them in the \emph{source} of prior information: it
queries an LLM, which requires only battery-domain text, rather than
running EIMO at multiple SOH levels or training a problem-specific
surrogate.
The two families are therefore complementary---EIMO is preferable when
historical optimization data are available at multiple SOH levels, whereas
LLMBO-MO is preferable when only domain knowledge is available in textual
form.

This paper proposes \emph{LLMBO-MO}, which augments a ParEGO-style
decomposition-based MOBO backbone with two LLM-guided search mechanisms.
\emph{The main contributions are organized around a single problem-solution
chain}: each contribution fixes one specific gap in applying LLM guidance
to early-stage multiobjective battery optimization.
The first contribution is a \emph{LLM-informed initialization} that
converts battery-domain textual knowledge into a protocol-domain-valid and
decision-space-diverse initial portfolio; this addresses the data-scarce
stage before sequential BO begins.
The second contribution is a \emph{weight-conditioned regional guidance}
mechanism that supplies additional restarts to early expected-improvement
optimization and is positioned as a secondary early-search refinement,
not as a peer to the initialization; this reflects the modest and
inconclusive 3-seed ablation result reported in
Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.
The third contribution is a fixed-budget numerical validation on two
SPMe parameterizations with matched seeds, which quantifies the combined
mechanism and the relative contribution of each component.

The principal findings and insights are summarized as follows.
\begin{itemize}
    \item A decomposition-based MOBO backbone, when seeded with a
    battery-domain-validated and decision-space-diverse LLM portfolio,
    produces a higher mean final hypervolume than the same backbone
    initialized with random or generic-prompt candidates under matched
    26-evaluation seeds.
    \item The LLM-derived improvement is concentrated in the
    initialization step; the additional bounded regional guidance
    contributes a smaller and statistically inconclusive
    three-seed refinement.
    \item The advantage transfers to a second SPMe parameterization
    but is interpreted as an additional battery-model check
    rather than as a formal cross-battery transfer study.
    \item The LLM is restricted to candidate locations; all objective
    values and Pareto-set entries are simulator-validated, which keeps
    the surrogate training set and the Pareto archive free of unevaluated
    LLM outputs.
\end{itemize}

\emph{Note to practitioners.} LLMBO-MO is best viewed as an early-stage
search aid when (i) the simulator is too expensive for a long BO budget,
(ii) domain knowledge is available in textual form but not as a numerical
prior, and (iii) the practitioner is willing to maintain a small LLM
prompt and response log for reproducibility.
The method adds at most $n_0{+}T_{\mathrm L}$ LLM calls to the optimization
loop (six initialization queries plus a bounded early-window sequence in
the present configuration), so its incremental API cost is modest compared
with the simulator cost.
If the LLM backend is unavailable, the LLM touchpoints degrade gracefully:
the initialization falls back to a random protocol-domain-valid
portfolio, and the regional-guidance branch is skipped, leaving the
underlying ParEGO-style MOBO loop intact.
