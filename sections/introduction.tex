\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Lithium-ion batteries are central to electric vehicles and other high-power
energy-storage systems because of their high energy density and long cycle
life~\cite{ref1}. Fast charging improves vehicle availability, but increasing
the charge rate can intensify heat generation, lithium plating, and
solid-electrolyte-interphase growth~\cite{ref2,refYang}. Charging-protocol
design is therefore not a single speed-maximization task: charging time,
thermal stress, and degradation must be balanced under voltage, temperature,
and state-of-charge (SOC) constraints.

The central computational difficulty is the scarcity of informative protocol
evaluations. Physical cycling tests are slow and costly, and even reduced-order
electrochemical--thermal simulations limit the number of protocols that can be
assessed in one design campaign. Bayesian optimization (BO) is well suited to
this regime because it uses a probabilistic surrogate and an acquisition rule
to allocate expensive evaluations~\cite{ref3}. Closed-loop cell studies and
simulation-based charging design have demonstrated substantial reductions in
the required search budget~\cite{ref11,ref12,ref14}. Multiobjective extensions
further expose the trade-off surface instead of collapsing user preferences
into one permanently fixed objective~\cite{ref13}.

A remaining difficulty is BO's cold start. Before enough task-specific
observations have accumulated, the surrogate and acquisition function are
strongly shaped by a small initial design and generic statistical priors.
Structured battery knowledge can improve this regime, as illustrated by recent
model-prior-guided charging optimization~\cite{refPrBO}, but constructing a
reliable prior model for every cell and operating condition is itself
demanding. LLM-assisted BO offers a complementary interface: recent methods use
large language models (LLMs) for warm starting, region preference, or
low-fidelity prediction~\cite{ref10,ref16,refLABO}. For safety-constrained
battery design, however, an LLM response is neither a calibrated measurement
nor a guarantee of feasibility. The technical challenge is thus to use its
contextual guidance without allowing unverified text-derived values to corrupt
the surrogate or bypass numerical constraint handling.

This paper addresses that challenge with LLMBO-MO, a guarded LLM-augmented
multiobjective BO framework for three-stage fast-charging protocol design. Its
core principle is to treat LLM output as a fallible preference prior. At
initialization, repaired and screened LLM proposals contribute to a diverse
warm-start portfolio. During an early guidance window, weight-conditioned point
or region preferences induce a posterior-covariance mean lift used only for
acquisition. Only simulator outputs enter the Gaussian-process (GP) training
database; malformed, infeasible, or unusable guidance has zero influence and
the optimizer reverts to ordinary expected improvement (EI). Augmented
Tchebycheff scalarization with Riesz-energy weight vectors distributes the
search across the three objectives.

The main contributions are as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A guarded two-touchpoint architecture is formulated for constrained
battery MOBO. The LLM advises initialization and early regional search, while
simulator observations, the GP surrogate, and EI retain authority over every
evaluated protocol.

\item A battery-aware warm-start portfolio and a weight-conditioned
posterior-covariance region lift are integrated with explicit parsing,
feasibility repair, bounded coupling, and reversion to plain EI. This design
prevents unevaluated LLM outputs from becoming surrogate targets.

\item The framework is evaluated under matched 56-simulation budgets on two
SPMe parameterizations. It improves final hypervolume over ParEGO by 9.2\% in
a representative Chen2020 case and by 17.8\% over five Ecker2015 seeds;
four-group ablations isolate the mean contribution of both LLM touchpoints.
\end{enumerate}
