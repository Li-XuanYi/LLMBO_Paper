\section{Numerical Validation and Discussion}
\label{sec:experiments}

This section evaluates LLMBO-MO on the fast-charging problem defined in
Section~\ref{sec:problem}. We first compare the complete method with
representative multiobjective optimizers under the same simulation budget.
We then examine the effect of LLM-informed initialization and regional
guidance, followed by a comparison across LLM backends and an analysis of
representative Pareto-optimal charging protocols.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

The Chen2020 parameterization of the LG INR21700-M50 cell~\cite{ref8} is used
as the primary case, while Ecker2015~\cite{ref9} is additionally considered to
assess robustness across battery parameterizations. For each charging protocol,
the three objectives are charging time $t_c$, peak temperature rise $T_p$, and
aging indicator $Q_s$. The main comparison uses six initial evaluations
followed by 50 BO iterations, giving 56 battery-model evaluations per run. Five
matched trials are used for all methods in the main comparison.

Hypervolume (HV) is adopted as the main multiobjective performance metric. A
larger HV indicates a better nondominated set under the same objective
definitions and reference point. Within each battery parameterization, all
methods use the same simulation budget and HV calculation. The battery and main
experimental settings are summarized in Tables~\ref{tab:battery_settings} and
\ref{tab:algorithm_settings}.

Four competitors are considered. ParEGO is the primary BO baseline because it
shares the decomposition--GP structure of the numerical backbone used in
LLMBO-MO~\cite{ref4}. DISK and PIMD are included as recent surrogate-assisted
multiobjective optimizers~\cite{refDISK,refPIMD}, while NSGA-II provides a
classical evolutionary baseline~\cite{refNSGAII}. All methods are compared
under the same simulator-evaluation budget.

\subsection{Ablation Study}

The main comparison evaluates the complete LLMBO-MO framework. We next examine
how the two forms of LLM assistance contribute to the observed behavior.

\subsubsection{LLM-Informed Initialization}

The first study compares random initialization, a detailed generic prompt, and
a battery-specific prompt. Ten matched trials are used, and all three variants
follow the same numerical BO procedure after initialization. The final HV
values under a 26-evaluation budget are reported in
Table~\ref{tab:prompt_study}.

\begin{table}[!t]
\caption{Effect of Different Initialization Strategies}
\label{tab:prompt_study}
\centering
\footnotesize
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{4.0pt}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lc@{}}
\toprule
Initialization & Final HV (mean $\pm$ SD) $\uparrow$ \\
\midrule
Random & 0.31062 $\pm$ 0.04303 \\
Generic prompt & 0.31371 $\pm$ 0.04675 \\
Battery-specific prompt & \textbf{0.36890 $\pm$ 0.00824} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The generic prompt performs similarly to random initialization. In contrast,
the battery-specific prompt increases the mean final HV by \PromptHVGain\ and
substantially reduces the variation among trials. The paired comparison remains
significant after Holm correction ($p=\PromptHolmP$). This result indicates
that the improvement is associated with battery-specific context rather than
with LLM prompting alone.

\subsubsection{Regional Guidance}

The second study compares four variants under the same short budget: the
numerical BO backbone without LLM assistance, LLM-informed initialization only,
regional guidance only, and the complete LLMBO-MO framework. Each run contains
six initial evaluations followed by ten BO iterations.

\begin{table}[!t]

Figure~\ref{fig:ablation_hv_box} and Table~\ref{tab:ablation} show that
LLM-informed initialization accounts for most of the improvement in this
short-budget study. Adding initialization increases the mean HV from
\AblationBaselineMean\ to \AblationWarmMean, whereas regional guidance alone
produces a smaller change to \AblationRegionMean. Combining both components
gives the highest mean HV of \AblationFullMean\ and the smallest dispersion
among the four variants. These results suggest that domain-informed
initialization is the dominant empirical source of the observed gain, while
regional guidance plays a complementary role during sequential search.

\subsection{Analysis of Pareto-Optimal Charging Protocols}

HV summarizes the overall quality of the nondominated set, whereas the
practical output of the optimizer is a collection of charging protocols. We
therefore select three representative solutions from the Chen2020 Pareto set to
connect the objective-space trade-offs with their charging trajectories.
Figure~\ref{fig:pareto_profiles} shows their locations in the three-objective
space, and Table~\ref{tab:representative_protocols} lists the corresponding
protocol parameters.

\begin{figure}[!t]
\centering

\caption{Representative Pareto-optimal charging protocols on Chen2020.}
\label{fig:pareto_profiles}
\end{figure}

Protocol A lies at the fast-charging end of the Pareto set. Its high early-stage
currents produce the shortest charging time, but also the largest temperature
rise and degradation indicator among the three representative solutions.
Protocol C uses a constant 2~A current and exhibits the opposite behavior, with
the longest charging time and the smallest temperature rise. Protocol B lies
between these two extremes and provides an intermediate compromise. This
ordering is visible directly in the three-objective distribution in
Fig.~\ref{fig:pareto_profiles}.

The corresponding voltage, temperature, current, and SOC trajectories are shown
in Fig.~\ref{fig:abc_charging_profiles}. Protocol A reaches the target SOC
first and exhibits the largest temperature increase, whereas Protocol C charges
more slowly and maintains the lowest temperature. Protocol B gives an
intermediate response. All three protocols remain below the prescribed voltage
limit. These trajectories are consistent with the charging-time, thermal, and
degradation trade-offs formulated in Section~\ref{sec:problem}.

\begin{figure}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{abc_charging_profiles.pdf}
\caption{Charging trajectories of protocols A--C on Chen2020.}
\label{fig:abc_charging_profiles}
\end{figure}

Taken together, the numerical results support three observations. First,
LLMBO-MO achieves the highest final mean HV among the five methods compared on
Chen2020, and its advantage over ParEGO is also observed on Ecker2015. Second,
the initialization experiments show that battery-specific context is the main
empirical source of improvement over generic or random initialization. Third,
the resulting Pareto protocols exhibit physically interpretable trade-offs
between charging time, temperature rise, and degradation, while regional
guidance provides a complementary mechanism for using LLM preferences during
the early sequential search.
