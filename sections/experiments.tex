
\section{Numerical Validation and Discussion}
\label{sec:experiments}

This section evaluates LLMBO-MO for the fast-charging problem introduced in Section~\ref{sec:problem}. All charging protocols are evaluated using the electrochemical--thermal--aging model described in Section~\ref{sec:model_section}. We first compare LLMBO-MO with representative multiobjective optimizers under the same simulation budget. The two LLM-assisted components are then examined separately, followed by an analysis of representative Pareto-optimal charging protocols.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

The Chen2020 parameterization of an LG INR21700-M50 cell is used as the primary case, while Ecker2015 is considered as an additional battery parameterization. For each charging protocol, the three objectives are charging time $t_c$, peak temperature rise $T_p$, and aging indicator $Q_s$. The main comparison uses six initial evaluations followed by 50 optimization iterations, resulting in 56 battery-model evaluations per run. Five matched trials are used for all methods in the main comparison.

Hypervolume (HV) is adopted as the main performance metric. Since the true Pareto front is unknown, HV provides a measure of both convergence and diversity; a larger HV indicates a better nondominated solution set under the same reference point. Within each battery parameterization, all methods use the same objective definitions, simulation budget, and HV calculation. The battery and algorithm settings are summarized in Tables~\ref{tab:battery_settings} and~\ref{tab:algorithm_settings}.

Four representative competitors are considered. ParEGO is selected as the primary BO baseline because it is a widely used decomposition-based method for expensive multiobjective optimization and shares the scalarization--GP structure with the numerical backbone of LLMBO-MO~\cite{ref4}. DISK and PIMD are included as recent surrogate-assisted multiobjective optimizers~\cite{refDISK,refPIMD}, and NSGA-II is used as a classical evolutionary baseline~\cite{ref5}.

\subsection{Ablation Study}

The preceding comparison evaluates the complete LLMBO-MO framework. We next separate the two LLM-assisted components to determine where the improvement comes from.

\subsubsection{LLM-Informed Initialization}

The first study compares three initialization strategies: random initialization, a detailed generic prompt, and a battery-specific prompt. Ten matched trials are used, and all three variants employ the same numerical BO procedure after initialization. The final HV values under a 26-evaluation budget are reported in Table~\ref{tab:prompt_study}.

\begin{table}[!t]
\caption{Effect of Different Initialization Strategies}
\label{tab:prompt_study}
\centering
\footnotesize
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{4.0pt}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lc@{}}
\toprule
Initialization & Final HV (mean $\pm$ SD) $\uparrow$ \\
\midrule
Random & 0.31062 $\pm$ 0.04303 \\
Generic prompt & 0.31371 $\pm$ 0.04675 \\
Battery-specific prompt & \textbf{0.36890 $\pm$ 0.00824} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The generic prompt gives a result close to random initialization. In contrast, the battery-specific prompt increases the mean HV by \PromptHVGain\ and also reduces the variation among trials. The difference remains significant after Holm correction ($p=\PromptHolmP$). This result shows that the improvement is associated with battery-specific information in the prompt rather than with prompting alone.

\subsubsection{Regional Guidance}

A second ablation examines the regional-guidance mechanism. Four variants are compared under the same short budget: the numerical BO backbone without LLM assistance, initialization only, regional guidance only, and the complete LLMBO-MO method. Each run contains six initial evaluations followed by ten BO iterations.

As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:ablation_hv_box}, initialization produces the main improvement: the mean HV increases from \AblationBaselineMean\ to \AblationWarmMean. When both components are used, LLMBO-MO obtains the largest mean HV, \AblationFullMean, with the smallest dispersion in this three-trial comparison. These results identify LLM-informed initialization as the more stable source of improvement, while regional guidance acts as an auxiliary refinement during the early BO iterations.

\subsection{Analysis of Pareto-Optimal Charging Protocols}

Figure~\ref{fig:pareto_profiles} shows their positions in the objective space, and Table~\ref{tab:representative_protocols} lists the corresponding charging parameters.

Protocol A represents the fast-charging end of the Pareto set. Its first two stages use high currents, giving the shortest charging time of 2880~s, but also the largest temperature rise and aging indicator among the three representative solutions. Protocol C uses a constant 2~A current and requires 7200~s to reach the target SOC; in return, it gives the lowest temperature rise. Protocol B lies between these two cases and provides a compromise among the three objectives. These solutions illustrate the expected trade-off: increasing the charging current shortens charging time, but generally increases thermal and aging costs.

The corresponding charging trajectories are plotted in Fig.~\ref{fig:abc_charging_profiles}. Protocol A reaches the target SOC first and exhibits the largest temperature increase. Protocol C charges more slowly and maintains the lowest temperature, while Protocol B gives an intermediate response. All three protocols remain below the prescribed voltage limit.

Overall, the numerical results lead to two observations. First, LLMBO-MO achieves a higher HV than the comparison methods under the same limited simulation budget on both battery parameterizations considered here. Second, the ablation experiments show that most of the improvement is associated with battery-specific initialization, whereas regional guidance provides a smaller additional effect. These conclusions are based on simulation results; the aging objective is a model-based indicator rather than a direct measurement of battery lifetime, and experimental cell validation remains an important topic for future work.
