\section{Numerical Validation and Discussion}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Evaluation Setup}

All quantitative results are simulation-based.
The SPMe formulation in Section~\ref{sec:model} is evaluated with the Chen2020
and Ecker2015 parameter sets~\cite{ref8,ref9}.
Every protocol is scored by charging time $t_c$, peak temperature rise
$\Delta T_p$, and the degradation proxy $D_{\mathrm{chg}}$
(Section~\ref{sec:aging}).
Each run uses six initialization evaluations followed by 50 BO iterations,
giving a fixed budget of 56 simulator calls.
Both parameterizations use seeds 8409--8413.

Final hypervolume (HV) is the performance metric; higher is better.
Within each parameterization, every method uses the same transformed objectives
and the same benchmark-specific ideal and reference box
(Section~\ref{sec:parego_backbone}).
HV values are compared within, not across, parameterizations.
Reported uncertainty is the sample standard deviation over the five seeds.

ParEGO is the primary comparator because it shares the
scalarization--GP--EI backbone with LLMBO-MO~\cite{ref4}.
Using a common backbone isolates the effect of the LLM advisory touchpoints
from confounding differences in surrogate modeling or acquisition strategy.
Direct hypervolume methods such as qEHVI and qNEHVI~\cite{refQEHVI,refQNEHVI}
optimize a fundamentally different acquisition surface and require a distinct
computational budget model; a matched comparison is deferred to future work.
NSGA-II and surrogate-assisted evolutionary algorithms~\cite{ref5,refDISK,refPIMD}
operate under population-based budgets that are not directly comparable to the
sequential 56-evaluation regime studied here.

The comparison holds the simulator, objectives, random-seed range, number of
initial evaluations, BO budget, and HV definition fixed.
The initialization composition differs by design: LLMBO-MO uses three screened
LLM proposals and three random points, whereas ParEGO uses six random
protocol-domain-valid points.
The main results therefore evaluate the complete configurations rather than
isolating a single coupling term; the component study
(Section~\ref{sec:component}) separates these effects.

Two different LLM backends are used across the two parameterizations
(GPT-4.1-nano for Chen2020, DeepSeek-V3-thinking for Ecker2015).
This choice is intentional: it demonstrates that the framework operates with
distinct LLM providers and does not depend on a specific model's
characteristics.
The same backend is used for both warm-start and regional guidance within each
parameterization.

Table~\ref{tab:fixed_budget} reports the five-seed final HV after the full
56-evaluation budget.
LLMBO-MO attains the higher mean on both parameterizations.
On Chen2020, the mean improvement is 0.84\% with three of five seeds favoring
LLMBO-MO in paired comparison.
On Ecker2015, the mean improvement is 2.18\% with four of five paired wins.
The sample dispersions are comparable for the two methods on Chen2020 and lower
for LLMBO-MO on Ecker2015.

These gains are modest in absolute terms, which is consistent with the
deliberately conservative design of the LLM interface.
The method is engineered to avoid degradation relative to standard BO; the
observed outcomes meet this criterion while providing a small but systematic
advantage.
The results do not by themselves identify which advisory touchpoint drives the
improvement; this is addressed by the component study below.

\subsection{Component Study}
\label{sec:component}

The component study separates the contributions of the two LLM touchpoints.
Four variants are compared on the Chen2020 parameterization under the same
56-evaluation budget and seed range:

\noindent\textbf{Baseline} (ParEGO): six random initial points, standard EI
throughout, no LLM involvement.\\
\textbf{WarmStart}: three screened LLM proposals plus three random initial
points, followed by standard EI with no regional guidance.\\
\textbf{Regional Guidance}: six random initial points, with bounded regional
guidance active during the early window ($T_{\mathrm L}=16$).
Because the warm-start screening pipeline is absent, this variant uses a
simplified anchor mechanism with uniform weighting and a mean-absolute-shift
budget $B_\delta$ in place of the full coupling in
\eqref{eq:bounded_coupling}.\\
\textbf{Complete LLMBO-MO}: both touchpoints active (three LLM + three random
initialization, plus the full bounded regional coupling during the guidance
window).

The first three variants were executed in one matched five-seed batch; the
Complete variant is a five-seed follow-up using the identical seed range and BO
budget.

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} reports the component-level decomposition.
The WarmStart variant provides the larger and more consistent individual gain
($+0.0066$ mean HV, four of five paired wins over Baseline).
Regional Guidance alone yields a smaller improvement ($+0.0026$, also four of
five wins) with greater seed-to-seed dispersion.
The Complete configuration achieves the highest mean HV ($+0.0096$) but with
three of five wins, indicating that the two touchpoints do not combine
uniformly across seeds.
The combined gain ($+0.0096$) is approximately the sum of the individual gains
($+0.0092$), suggesting that warm-starting and regional guidance operate
through largely independent mechanisms at this budget.

These results support two conclusions.
First, the screened LLM warm-start is the primary performance driver,
consistent with the intuition that informative initialization matters most when
the total evaluation budget is small.
Second, bounded regional guidance provides a consistent but smaller
complementary benefit; its value is most apparent when the warm-start
portfolio already places the search in a favorable region of the protocol
space.

\subsection{Limitations and Deployment Context}

The evidence reported in this section is subject to several scope limitations,
each of which defines a direction for further work.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Simulation-only validation.} All quantitative results are
obtained from the SPMe simulator. No physical-cell cycling data are included,
and the reported HV values do not measure calibrated capacity fade or lifetime
improvement. Physical replay on the platform in
Fig.~\ref{fig:experiment_platform} is the natural next step.

\item \textbf{Limited parameterization coverage.} Two SPMe parameter sets
(Chen2020 and Ecker2015) with a common five-seed range. Broader validation
across additional cell chemistries, aging levels, and seed ranges would
strengthen the generality claim.

\item \textbf{ParEGO-centered comparison.} The fixed-budget protocol uses
ParEGO as the sole numerical baseline. Matched-budget comparisons with direct
hypervolume methods (qEHVI, qNEHVI) and surrogate-assisted evolutionary
algorithms are needed to position LLMBO-MO within the wider multiobjective BO
landscape.

\item \textbf{Trajectory-averaged degradation proxy.} $D_{\mathrm{chg}}$
uses time-averaged SOC, temperature, and current and does not resolve
short-duration excursions (high SOC, high current, high temperature) that
contribute disproportionately to degradation in physical cells.

\item \textbf{LLM backend variability.} Different LLM backends were used for
the two parameterizations. While this demonstrates provider independence, a
systematic backend comparison under controlled conditions would clarify how
LLM capability affects guidance quality.

\item \textbf{Initialization composition.} LLMBO-MO and ParEGO use different
numbers of LLM-sourced initial points (3 vs.\ 0). A pure acquisition-phase
comparison with matched initialization would isolate the regional guidance
effect more precisely.
\end{enumerate}
