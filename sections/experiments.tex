\section{Numerical Validation and Discussion}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Simulation Setup}

All quantitative results are obtained through simulation. The evaluator
combines the SPMe and lumped thermal model in
Section~\ref{sec:model_section} with the three objectives in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:cmop}. All methods are evaluated under a common simulation
budget and repeated independent runs, and the reported uncertainty is the
sample standard deviation. Figure~\ref{fig:experiment_platform} shows the
intended physical replay interface; no measurement from this platform enters
the reported results.

\begin{figure}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.90\columnwidth]{experiment_platform.jpg}
\caption{Laboratory charging-control platform retained as the intended
interface for future protocol replay. The host computer coordinates current
actuation and synchronized voltage/temperature acquisition; no measurements
from this platform enter the quantitative results in this paper.}
\label{fig:experiment_platform}
\end{figure}

Hypervolume (HV) is used to assess convergence and Pareto-front coverage;
higher values indicate better overall performance. Within each battery
parameterization, the compared methods use the same objective transformation
and reference box. Table~\ref{tab:core_settings} summarizes the main simulation
and optimization settings. Each method retains its native initialization
strategy while using the same total evaluation budget.

meaningful charging trade-offs rather than only a favorable scalar metric.

The selected protocols show the expected conflict among charging speed,
temperature rise, and degradation severity. Aggressive schedules shorten the
charge at the cost of larger thermal and degradation responses, whereas
conservative schedules reduce these responses by accepting longer charging
times. The trajectories reach the target SOC through distinct stage sequences
and remain within the limits of the selected simulation model.

The improvements are modest and are therefore interpreted descriptively.

parameterizations.

\subsubsection{Objective-Preprocessing Sensitivity}
\label{sec:objective_preprocessing}

The preprocessing study compares three objective-scaling strategies under the
same optimization setting. Figure~\ref{fig:objective_normalization} shows that
z-score scaling achieves the highest endpoint mean, min--max scaling remains
competitive, and the unnormalized variant is lower and more variable. The
differences are descriptive and demonstrate that objective preprocessing is an
important implementation choice.

\end{figure}

\subsubsection{Scope and Remaining Validation}

The present evidence is limited to two simulated battery parameterizations and
a restricted set of optimization baselines. The study does not include matched
qEHVI/qNEHVI comparisons, calibrated lifetime prediction, or measurements from
the laboratory platform. Broader algorithmic comparisons and physical replay
are therefore required before drawing conclusions about hardware robustness or
long-term battery life.
