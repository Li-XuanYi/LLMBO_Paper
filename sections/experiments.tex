\section{Numerical Validation and Discussion}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

The numerical study evaluates whether LLMBO-MO improves fixed-budget
multiobjective search and whether the two LLM guidance mechanisms contribute to
that improvement.
The SPMe-based simulator in Section~\ref{sec:model_section} is evaluated with
the Chen2020 and Ecker2015 parameterizations~\cite{ref8,ref9}.
Chen2020 is the primary case for the method comparison and mechanism studies,
while Ecker2015 is used as a second battery parameterization to test whether the
observed optimization behavior persists under a different model configuration.
Each candidate is evaluated by charging time $t_c$, peak temperature rise
$\Delta T_p$, and degradation proxy $D_{\mathrm{chg}}$.

The main BO runs use six initialization evaluations followed by 50 sequential
BO evaluations, giving a total budget of 56 simulator calls.
Hypervolume (HV) is used as the principal multiobjective metric because it
jointly reflects convergence and coverage of the obtained nondominated set;
higher HV indicates a better approximation under a fixed reporting convention.
Within each battery parameterization, all compared methods use the same
objective definitions and HV convention.
The main algorithm settings are listed in Table~\ref{tab:algorithm_settings};
run identifiers and reproducibility details are moved to
Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility} so that the main table focuses on method
parameters, consistent with the experimental presentation used in related
charging-optimization studies.

ParEGO is the primary BO comparator because it is a standard
scalarization-based method for expensive multiobjective optimization and shares
the decomposition--GP structure used by the numerical backbone of LLMBO-MO
~\cite{ref4}.
DISK and PIMD provide recent surrogate-assisted expensive multiobjective
optimization references~\cite{refDISK,refPIMD}, while NSGA-II is included as a
classical evolutionary multiobjective reference~\cite{ref5}.
This comparator family is aligned with the battery fast-charging optimization
literature, where ParEGO and surrogate-assisted multiobjective optimizers are
commonly used to assess convergence and Pareto-set quality.

\subsection{Effectiveness of the Two Knowledge-Guidance Mechanisms}

\subsubsection{LLM-Informed Initialization}

The first mechanism study isolates the value of battery-specific LLM knowledge
in the initialization stage.
A separate Chen2020 experiment uses ten matched runs and a shorter 26-evaluation
budget.
All variants use the same downstream numerical BO procedure and differ only in
the source of the initial candidate portfolio.

\begin{table}[!t]
\caption{Chen2020 Initialization Study over Ten Runs}
\label{tab:prompt_study}
\centering
\footnotesize
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{4.0pt}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lc@{}}
\toprule
Initialization source & Final HV $\uparrow$ \\
\midrule
Random & 0.31062 $\pm$ 0.04303 \\
Detailed generic prompt & 0.31371 $\pm$ 0.04675 \\
Battery-specific LLM prompt & \textbf{0.36890 $\pm$ 0.00824} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The battery-specific LLM initialization improves mean HV over random
initialization by \PromptHVGain.
The detailed generic prompt remains close to the random condition, whereas the
battery-specific prompt produces a clear improvement with substantially lower
dispersion.
The paired comparison remains supported after Holm multiplicity correction
($p=\PromptHolmP$, $d_z=\PromptEffect$).
These results indicate that the useful signal is associated with domain-aligned
charging knowledge rather than prompting alone, supporting the role of the LLM
as an initialization prior for the low-data stage of MOBO.

LLM-informed initialization produces the principal short-budget improvement,
raising mean HV from \AblationBaselineMean\ to \AblationWarmMean.

replacement for the global initialization prior.

($\pm\AblationFullStd$).
The ablation therefore supports the intended division of labor: LLM-informed
initialization identifies a better global starting portfolio, while
weight-conditioned regional guidance provides a bounded local refinement of the
subsequent multiobjective acquisition search.

with more aggressive current schedules reduce charging time but generally

produced by complete battery-model simulations rather than by LLM-generated
objective estimates.

\subsection{Discussion}

The overall comparison, initialization study, and component ablation support a
single algorithmic interpretation.
LLMBO-MO remains a numerical multiobjective BO method, while LLM-derived battery
knowledge is concentrated where the data-driven search is weakest.
The initialization mechanism alters the initial distribution of expensive
evaluations, and the region mechanism supplies a bounded, weight-dependent local
search preference during the early acquisition stage.
After simulator evaluation, both routes return to the same scalarization--GP--EI
and Pareto-update process.

Two additional diagnostics---Pareto-archive growth and objective-preprocessing
sensitivity---are retained in Appendix~\ref{app:diagnostics} because they test
supporting numerical behavior rather than either proposed knowledge-guidance
mechanism.
The present study remains simulation based and uses a trajectory-averaged
control-oriented degradation proxy; the conclusions therefore concern
sample-efficient multiobjective protocol search in the adopted SPMe-based
environment.
Physical-cell validation and broader LLM/backend studies remain important future
extensions.
