\section{Numerical Validation and Discussion}
\label{sec:experiments}

This section evaluates LLMBO-MO on the multiobjective charging problem defined
in Section~\ref{sec:problem}, using the SPMe-based simulator and degradation
model from Section~\ref{sec:model_section}.
Two battery parameterizations are considered: Chen2020 (primary) and Ecker2015
(additional check).
The main result is that LLMBO-MO consistently achieves higher hypervolume than
ParEGO, NSGA-II, DISK, and PIMD under a 56-evaluation budget, with the
LLM-informed initialization being the dominant mechanism.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

The numerical study examines two questions: whether LLMBO-MO improves
fixed-budget multiobjective charging design, and which of the two LLM-guided
search mechanisms accounts for the observed improvement.
Each candidate protocol is evaluated in terms of the three objectives in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:cmop}: charging time $t_c$, peak temperature rise $\Delta T_p$,
and degradation proxy $D_{\mathrm{chg}}$.

The main comparisons use six initialization evaluations followed by 50
sequential BO evaluations, giving a total budget of 56 simulator calls.
For each battery parameterization, the same five matched trials are used for
every compared method.
Hypervolume (HV) is the principal multiobjective performance metric because it
jointly reflects convergence and coverage of the nondominated set; higher HV
indicates a better solution set under a common reference convention.
Within each parameterization, all methods use the same objective definitions,
HV convention, and simulator budget.
The battery and LLMBO-MO settings are summarized in
Tables~\ref{tab:battery_settings} and~\ref{tab:algorithm_settings}, respectively.
The listed cell specifications apply to the primary Chen2020 case; Ecker2015
uses its source parameterization.

ParEGO is the primary BO comparator because it is a standard
scalarization-based method for expensive multiobjective optimization and shares
the decomposition--GP structure used by the numerical backbone of
LLMBO-MO~\cite{ref4}.
DISK and PIMD provide recent surrogate-assisted references for expensive
multiobjective optimization~\cite{refDISK,refPIMD}, while NSGA-II is included as
a classical evolutionary multiobjective baseline~\cite{ref5}.
Together, these methods provide both backbone-matched and complementary
multiobjective references.

\subsection{Effectiveness of the LLM-Guided Search Mechanisms}

\subsubsection{LLM-Informed Initialization}

The first mechanism study isolates the effect of battery-specific textual
knowledge during initialization.
A separate Chen2020 experiment uses ten matched trials and a shorter
26-evaluation budget.
All variants share the same downstream numerical BO procedure and differ only
in the source of the initial candidate portfolio.

Battery-specific LLM initialization improves mean final HV over random

prompt content rather than from prompting alone, supporting the use of
LLM-informed initialization in the data-scarce stage of MOBO.

\subsection{Pareto Charging-Protocol Analysis}

HV summarizes optimization quality, but the practical output of the optimizer is
a set of charging protocols in the form of Eq.~\eqref{eq:protocol}.
Figure~\ref{fig:pareto_profiles} and Table~\ref{tab:representative_protocols}
therefore examine representative simulator-evaluated nondominated solutions and
their objective-space trade-offs.

The representative protocols exhibit the expected physical trade-offs.
More aggressive current schedules shorten the charging horizon but
generally produce larger temperature excursions and higher
degradation-proxy values.
\emph{Protocol~A} (6.000/5.000/3.000~A, $\Delta s = 0.400/0.300/0.100$)
sits at the speed-favouring corner of the Pareto front: the first two
stages operate near the current upper bound, which drives
$t_c = 2880$~s but also produces the largest peak temperature rise
($\Delta T_p = 7.567$~K) and the largest degradation-proxy value
($D_{\mathrm{chg}} = 1.261$).
The $\Delta T_p$ rise is dominated by the high current in the first
two stages, whereas the $D_{\mathrm{chg}}$ rise is driven jointly by
the high current and the high average SOC at which this high current
is delivered.
\emph{Protocol~C} (2.000/2.000/2.000~A, $\Delta s = 0.245/0.153/0.403$)
sits at the durability-favouring corner: a constant current below the
3~A lower edge of the upper bracket, with the bulk of the SOC span
allocated to the third stage, gives $t_c = 7200$~s but reduces
$\Delta T_p$ to $1.528$~K and $D_{\mathrm{chg}}$ to $0.640$.
\emph{Protocol~B} marks the intermediate trade-off, in which the
current sequence is reshaped from the high-to-low staircase of A
toward the more uniform schedule of C; the deformation of the current
sequence is the dominant physical lever, whereas the SOC allocation
shifts play a secondary role in this regime.
Gentler schedules move toward the lower-temperature and
lower-degradation regions of the Pareto front at the cost of longer
charging time.
The objective-space geometry in Fig.~\ref{fig:pareto_profiles} makes this
speed--temperature--degradation trade-off explicit.

\begin{figure}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{abc_charging_profiles.pdf}
\caption{Simulated Chen2020 charging trajectories of representative protocols
A--C: (a) terminal voltage, (b) cell temperature, (c) applied current, and
(d) state of charge. The red dashed line marks the voltage limit.}
\label{fig:abc_charging_profiles}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:abc_charging_profiles} shows how the selected current schedules
produce these objective-space compromises. Protocol~A reaches the target SOC
in 2880~s but gives the largest temperature rise; protocol~B provides an
intermediate 5199-s trajectory; and the uniform 2-A protocol~C limits the
temperature rise to 1.528~K at the cost of a 7200-s charge. All three
trajectories reach SOC~0.8 without exceeding the voltage limit.

\subsection{Discussion}

The results support the design principles stated in
Section~\ref{sec:motivation}.

\subsubsection{Where the gain comes from}

Both battery parameterizations show the same behaviour: LLMBO-MO
reaches a higher final HV than the backbone-matched ParEGO under an
identical 56-evaluation budget, and also outperforms NSGA-II, DISK,
and PIMD.
The advantage emerges in the early-to-middle portion of the budget and
is retained as the numerical GP--EI loop proceeds.
This is exactly the intended division of labour: LLM guidance improves
how the scarce early simulator evaluations are allocated, while
objective evaluation, surrogate fitting, and Pareto updating remain
numerical.

The initialization study localizes the effect.
The battery-specific prompt raises mean final HV by \PromptHVGain{}
over random initialization, whereas a detailed but domain-agnostic
prompt stays close to the random condition.
The benefit therefore comes from battery knowledge rather than from
prompt length or from the mere presence of an LLM.
In the component ablation, LLM-informed initialization accounts for
most of the improvement and the complete framework attains both the
highest mean HV and the smallest dispersion, which is consistent with
regional guidance acting as a secondary refinement of the early
acquisition search rather than as an independent driver.

\subsubsection{Physical interpretation of the Pareto protocols}

The representative protocols in
Table~\ref{tab:representative_protocols} span three physically
distinct compromises: \emph{Protocol~A} occupies the speed-favouring
corner (high current, high $\Delta T_p$, high $D_{\mathrm{chg}}$),
\emph{Protocol~C} the durability-favouring corner (uniform low
current, low $\Delta T_p$, low $D_{\mathrm{chg}}$), and
\emph{Protocol~B} an intermediate deformation in which the
high-to-low staircase of A is progressively flattened toward the
uniform schedule of C while the SOC allocation moves the
heat-generation load from the first stage to the third.
The direction of this deformation reflects the LLM initialization
portfolio, which is dominated by staircase-shaped protocols: the
GP--EI loop first explores the neighbourhood of these anchors and then
discovers the more uniform schedules that dominate the
durability-favouring region of the front.

\subsubsection{Scope of the conclusions}

Two boundaries should be kept in mind.
All results are simulation based, and the degradation objective is a
trajectory-averaged control-oriented proxy rather than a measured
capacity-fade endpoint.
In addition, a single LLM backend is used, so the comparison
characterizes the proposed framework rather than the sensitivity of
LLM-guided BO to the choice of language model.
Broader MOBO baselines, cross-LLM studies, and physical-cell
validation are left for future work.
