\section{Numerical Validation and Discussion}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Evaluation Setup}

All quantitative evidence in this paper is simulation based.
The SPMe formulation in Section~\ref{sec:model} is evaluated with the
Chen2020 and Ecker2015 parameter sets~\cite{ref8,ref9}.
Chen2020 is the primary parameterization used for the method comparison and
diagnostic studies; Ecker2015 is a secondary parameterization used to examine
whether the observed behavior persists under a different battery model
configuration.
Each protocol is scored by charging time $t_c$, peak temperature rise
$\Delta T_p$, and the degradation proxy $D_{\mathrm{chg}}$
(Section~\ref{sec:aging}).
BO-based runs use six initialization evaluations followed by 50 optimization
iterations, for a total of 56 simulator calls.

Hypervolume (HV) is the evaluation metric; higher is better.
All methods within one battery parameterization use the same transformed
objectives and a fixed benchmark-specific reporting convention.
The convention is common to the methods being compared but differs between
Chen2020 and Ecker2015, so absolute HV values are interpreted only within one
parameterization.
Reported uncertainty is one sample standard deviation.

Five repeated runs are summarized for each method.
For the Chen2020 LLMBO-MO and ParEGO comparison, seed 8409 corresponds to the
trajectory shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:chen_hv_5way}, while seeds 8410--8413 are
drawn from compatible runs with the same parameterization, method preset,
56-call budget, and HV definition.
Because these runs were not generated in one synchronized execution batch, the
five-seed comparison is interpreted descriptively and is not used for an
inferential significance claim.

ParEGO is the primary BO comparator because it shares the
scalarization--GP backbone with LLMBO-MO~\cite{ref4}.
The Chen2020 comparison additionally includes NSGA-II and the surrogate-assisted
evolutionary algorithms DISK and PIMD~\cite{ref5,refDISK,refPIMD} under the
same 56-evaluation cap.
Matched qEHVI and qNEHVI runs are not available; their absence is
treated as an evaluation limitation rather than as evidence against those
methods~\cite{refQEHVI,refQNEHVI}.

The numerical validation first compares fixed-budget optimization performance,
then examines initialization evidence and component contributions.
Pareto-set interpretation and a bounded discussion follow; complete supporting
diagnostics are retained in Appendix~\ref{app:diagnostics}.

\subsection{Effectiveness of the Proposed Knowledge Guidance}

\subsubsection{Initialization Prior}

The initialization study examines whether battery-specific LLM knowledge improves
the stage in which BO has the least task-specific evidence.
A separate Chen2020 prompt study uses ten seeds and
a shorter 26-evaluation budget.
All variants share the same downstream BO procedure and differ only in the
initial-candidate source, so the comparison targets the value of the
initialization prior rather than later regional guidance.

\begin{table}[!t]
\caption{Chen2020 Warm-Start Prompt Study over Ten Seeds}
\label{tab:prompt_study}
\centering
\footnotesize
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{4.0pt}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lc@{}}
\toprule
Initialization source & Final HV $\uparrow$ \\
\midrule
Random & 0.31062 $\pm$ 0.04303 \\

\bottomrule

The experimental battery prompt improves mean HV over random initialization by
\PromptHVGain.
The paired contrast remains nonzero after Holm correction
($p=\PromptHolmP$) and has standardized effect size
$d_z=\PromptEffect$.
The result shows that the gain is not explained by prompting alone: the
detailed generic prompt is close to random initialization, whereas the
battery-specific prompt produces the clear improvement.
This supports the proposed use of an LLM as a carrier of domain-specific
initialization knowledge in the low-data regime.
Because this study uses a shorter budget, it is treated as mechanism evidence
for the cold start rather than as a substitute for the 56-evaluation main
comparison.

\label{sec:component}

\subsubsection{Component Evidence}

The component study separates the contribution of the screened warm start from
that of continued regional guidance.
The values in \autoref{tab:ablation} are interpreted descriptively because the
regional variants also differ in their external acquisition restarts.  This
confound prevents the regional effect from being isolated completely.

\begin{table}[!t]
\caption{Chen2020 Component Study over Five Runs}
\label{tab:ablation}
\centering
\footnotesize
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{3.3pt}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
\toprule
Variant & Final HV $\uparrow$ & Difference from plain BO \\
\midrule
Plain BO & 0.3836 $\pm$ 0.0113 & -- \\
Warm start & \textbf{0.3902 $\pm$ 0.0087} & +0.0066 \\
Regional guidance & 0.3862 $\pm$ 0.0125 & +0.0026 \\
Complete LLMBO-MO & 0.3900 $\pm$ 0.0067 & +0.0064 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The screened warm start provides the largest isolated mean change and reduces
dispersion relative to plain BO.
Regional guidance alone has a smaller positive mean difference, while the
complete configuration is essentially level with warm start alone.
The component evidence therefore supports the screened warm start as the more
stable contributor but does not establish an additive or systematic regional
benefit.
This result is important to the interpretation of LLMBO-MO: the available data
favor a bounded early-stage prior over persistent LLM intervention once
simulator evidence begins to accumulate.
Accordingly, regional guidance is treated as an auxiliary extension rather than
as a co-equal source of the reported gain.

\subsection{Discussion}

Two supporting diagnostics test numerical behavior outside the central LLM
claim.
The Pareto archive remains descriptively richer for LLMBO-MO through most of
the recorded iterations, although archive count is neither HV nor an
equal-simulator-call efficiency measure.
The objective-scaling study shows that balancing heterogeneous objective
magnitudes reduces final dispersion relative to no normalization, while the
ordering between min--max and Z-score scaling is small at the final budget.
The complete diagnostic figures, table, and interpretation are provided in
Appendix~\ref{app:diagnostics} so that they do not obscure the mechanism
evidence above.

The experimental scope imposes several boundaries on the comparison.
All results are simulation based; the Chen2020 summary combines compatible
runs rather than one synchronized batch; matched qEHVI and qNEHVI results are
unavailable; and the LLM backend is not identical across all runs.
Consequently, the evidence does not establish state-of-the-art MOBO performance
or isolate cross-parameterization transfer.

The interpretive scope is likewise narrower than physical battery validation.
$D_{\mathrm{chg}}$ is an uncalibrated trajectory-averaged proxy rather than a
measured capacity-fade endpoint.
The archive-growth axis counts algorithmic iterations rather than equal
simulator calls, and the component comparison contains an acquisition-restart
confound.
Accordingly, the present data support knowledge-informed initialization but do
not establish an independent systematic benefit from regional guidance or a
claim that every LLM-assisted trajectory outperforms standard BO.
