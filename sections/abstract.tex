\begin{abstract}
Fast, safe, and durable charging of lithium-ion batteries requires balancing
charging speed, thermal stress, and degradation, which makes
charging-protocol design a constrained multiobjective optimization problem.
Bayesian optimization (BO) is attractive in this setting because it can
approximate a Pareto set with a limited number of expensive
simulator evaluations, but its early search is highly sensitive to the small
initial design and to the absence of task-specific structure.
This paper proposes LLMBO-MO, a large language model (LLM)-guided extension of
a ParEGO-style decomposition-based multiobjective BO backbone that addresses
this early-search gap.
The proposed method introduces two LLM touchpoints that are kept strictly
separable from the numerical surrogate: (i) an LLM-informed initialization
that converts qualitative battery knowledge into a protocol-domain-valid and
decision-space-diverse initial portfolio, and (ii) a weight-conditioned
regional guidance mechanism that uses the active decomposition direction and
the current optimization state to add region-informed restarts to early
expected-improvement optimization, while the original numerical search is
retained as an automatic fallback.
All selected protocols are evaluated by the battery simulator before they
enter the surrogate training set or the Pareto archive, and path-dependent
voltage and temperature feasibility is determined by the simulator rather
than by the LLM.
Under 56 simulator evaluations and five seeds, LLMBO-MO attains a mean final
hypervolume of \ChenLLMBOMean\,$\pm$\,\ChenLLMBOStd\ on Chen2020, compared
with \ChenParEGOMean\,$\pm$\,\ChenParEGOStd\ for ParEGO, a \ChenFinalGain\
relative improvement; a similar advantage is observed on Ecker2015.
Mechanism studies show that the gain is concentrated in the
LLM-informed initialization, whereas regional guidance acts as a
secondary early-search refinement.
The conclusions are limited to simulation-based evaluation, a control-oriented
degradation proxy, a single LLM backend, and matched-seed fixed-budget
comparisons against a backbone-matched comparator set.
\end{abstract}
