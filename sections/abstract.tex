\begin{abstract}
Fast-charging protocol design requires balancing charging time, peak
temperature rise, and degradation under an evaluation budget constrained by
electrochemical--thermal simulation cost.
Bayesian optimization (BO) is suitable for this regime, but its early search
is sensitive to a small initial design, while direct use of large-language-
model (LLM) predictions would introduce unverified numerical evidence.
We propose LLMBO-MO, an asymmetric multiobjective BO framework that uses an LLM
only to propose and prioritize search locations.
The framework combines a protocol-domain-screened warm start with temporary
weight-conditioned regional preferences; the simulator remains the sole
objective oracle, and only evaluated protocols train the Gaussian-process
surrogate.
Under 56 simulator evaluations and five archived seeds, LLMBO-MO obtains a
mean hypervolume (HV) of \ChenLLMBOMean\ on Chen2020, compared with
\ChenParEGOMean\ for ParEGO, and exceeds NSGA-II, DISK, and PIMD by
\ChenNSGAGain--\ChenPIMDGain.
On Ecker2015, the observed HV advantage is \EckerMidGain\ at evaluation 30 and
\EckerFinalGain\ at the final budget.
A Chen2020 sensitivity study finds similar final HV for min--max and Z-score
preprocessing, whereas omitting normalization produces lower mean HV and much
larger dispersion.
A separate archive-growth diagnostic maintains \OptimalLLMBOFinal\ feasible
nondominated protocols after 50 iterations, compared with
\OptimalParEGOFinal\ for ParEGO.
The ten-seed prompt study shows a mean HV increase of \PromptHVGain\
over random initialization after Holm correction ($p=\PromptHolmP$).
The component evidence identifies screened warm starting as the more stable
contributor, whereas an independent benefit from regional coupling is not
established.
These results support a restricted-evidence design pattern for injecting
language-model priors into simulation-driven engineering optimization without
treating generated text as physical evidence.
\end{abstract}
