\begin{abstract}
Fast-charging protocol design for lithium-ion batteries requires balancing
charging time, peak temperature rise, and a degradation proxy under tightly
limited evaluation budgets, because each candidate protocol must be assessed
through costly electrochemical--thermal simulation.
Bayesian optimization (BO) is well suited to this sample-scarce regime, but
its early efficiency depends strongly on informative initialization and
acquisition guidance.
Large language models (LLMs) offer domain-informed priors for early search,
provided that their guidance is integrated through a simulator-grounded
interface.
We propose LLMBO-MO, a multiobjective BO framework with two advisory
touchpoints: a protocol-domain-screened warm-start that supplies diverse
initial candidates, and early regional guidance with validation gates and an
explicit pointwise shift cap.
Simulator-evaluated protocols alone update the Gaussian-process surrogate.
Under a matched budget of 56 simulator evaluations and five fixed seeds,
LLMBO-MO attains higher mean final hypervolume than ParEGO on two SPMe
parameterizations, with a 1.7\% mean improvement on Chen2020 (up to 5.1\% in a
paired seed) and a 17.8\% mean improvement on Ecker2015.
A component study identifies the screened warm-start as the primary performance
driver, while a ten-seed prompt-design ablation provides independent
statistical support for LLM-derived initialization ($p < 0.01$).
The results establish a validation-gated, simulation-grounded interface for
integrating language-model guidance into data-scarce battery optimization.
\end{abstract}
