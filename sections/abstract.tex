\begin{abstract}
Designing fast-charging protocols for lithium-ion batteries requires balancing
charging time, thermal stress, and degradation under safety and operational
constraints. Bayesian optimization is well suited to this expensive black-box
problem, but standard data-driven implementations typically rely on numerical
observations collected during the search and do not explicitly encode
battery-domain knowledge. This paper
proposes LLMBO-MO, a multiobjective Bayesian optimization framework in which a
large language model introduces domain knowledge at two controlled stages,
while the probabilistic surrogate and acquisition function remain the decision
core. At initialization, the language model overgenerates charging protocols
that are validated, repaired, and selected as a diverse warm-start portfolio.
During optimization, it provides a preferred point or bounded region that is
translated into a confidence-scaled local adjustment of the Gaussian-process
mean used by expected improvement; invalid guidance triggers a fallback to the
unmodified acquisition rule. Augmented Tchebycheff scalarization with Riesz
energy-based weight vectors converts the three objectives into sequential
scalar subproblems. Experiments using coupled electrochemical, thermal, and
aging simulators based on the single-particle model with electrolyte show that,
compared with ParEGO under the same 56-evaluation budget, the proposed framework
increases final hypervolume from 0.3523 to 0.3848 in a representative Chen2020
case. Across five Ecker2015 seeds, it increases mean hypervolume from 1.5866
to 1.8684 and reduces the standard deviation from 0.0116 to 0.0024. Ablation
results show positive mean contributions from both language-model touchpoints,
with their combination yielding the highest mean hypervolume.
\end{abstract}
