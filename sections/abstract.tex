\begin{abstract}
Fast-charging protocol design is an expensive constrained multiobjective
problem that must balance charging time, thermal stress, and a degradation
indicator. Conventional Bayesian optimization (BO) is sample efficient, but
its cold-start decisions depend on few task-specific observations and generic
statistical priors. This paper presents LLMBO-MO, a multiobjective BO framework
that treats large-language-model (LLM) output as a fallible preference prior
rather than as an objective observation or a final decision. The LLM first
supports a diverse, constraint-screened warm start and then supplies
weight-conditioned point or region preferences during early optimization.
Simulator evaluations alone update the Gaussian-process surrogate, while a
posterior-covariance mean lift gives valid guidance bounded influence at
acquisition time; missing or invalid guidance reverts to standard expected
improvement. Augmented Tchebycheff scalarization distributes the search across
the three objectives. Under matched 56-evaluation budgets on coupled
electrochemical--thermal simulators with a protocol-level degradation proxy,
LLMBO-MO improves final hypervolume over ParEGO by 9.2\% in a representative
Chen2020 case and by 17.8\% on average across five Ecker2015 seeds. In the
latter comparison, the hypervolume standard deviation decreases from 0.0116 to
0.0024. Ablations show positive mean contributions from both LLM touchpoints,
with their combination attaining the highest mean hypervolume. The evidence is
simulation-based and supports guarded LLM guidance as a promising route to
more sample-efficient Pareto search.
\end{abstract}
