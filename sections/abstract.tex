\begin{abstract}
Fast-charging protocol design for lithium-ion batteries must balance charging
time, temperature rise, and degradation severity under a limited evaluation
budget. Bayesian optimization (BO) is well suited to this setting, but its
early search is sensitive to initialization and to guidance introduced before
sufficient task-specific evidence is available. We propose LLMBO-MO, a
multiobjective BO framework that uses large-language-model (LLM) knowledge as
bounded search advice while reserving objective and feasibility evaluation for
the battery simulator. A screened warm start and an early preference interface
guide candidate placement without replacing the numerical optimization path.
Experiments on two battery parameterizations show modest improvements in final
hypervolume, while component and prompt analyses support the value of informed
initialization. These results demonstrate a verification-aware way to combine
contextual LLM knowledge with data-scarce battery optimization.
\end{abstract}
