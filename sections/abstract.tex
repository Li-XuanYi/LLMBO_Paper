\begin{abstract}
Fast-charging protocol design for lithium-ion batteries is a constrained
multiobjective optimization problem that must balance charging time, thermal
stress, and degradation under a limited evaluation budget.
Bayesian optimization (BO) is attractive for this setting because it can reduce
the number of expensive electrochemical--thermal simulations.
However, its early search is strongly influenced by a small initial design,
exactly when task-specific observations are scarce.
Large language models (LLMs) offer a possible source of battery-domain prior
knowledge, but their generated numerical outputs cannot be regarded as
simulator-validated physical evidence.
To address these two issues, this paper proposes LLMBO-MO, a multiobjective BO
framework that separates LLM-based search guidance from numerical optimization
evidence.
First, a protocol-domain-screened and diversity-aware LLM warm start is
developed to construct a more informative initial design from textual battery
knowledge.
A bounded weight-conditioned regional-guidance stage then refines acquisition
search during the early BO stage.
In both cases, the simulator remains the sole objective oracle and only
evaluated protocols are used for Gaussian-process fitting and Pareto dominance.
Under a budget of 56 simulator evaluations, the descriptive five-run Chen2020
endpoint mean is \ChenLLMBOMean\ for LLMBO-MO versus \ChenParEGOMean\ for
ParEGO, a \ChenParEGOGain\ advantage; the archived seed-8409 trajectories
displayed in the convergence figure end \ChenDisplayedGain\ apart
(\ChenDisplayedLLMBO\ versus \ChenDisplayedParEGO).
Favorable behavior also persists in the five-run Ecker2015 comparison.
A controlled ten-seed initialization study further shows an HV improvement of
\PromptHVGain\ over random initialization under a Holm-corrected paired test
($p=\PromptHolmP$).
A matched three-seed short-budget ablation further shows that the complete
LLMBO-MO configuration achieves the highest mean HV (\AblationFullMean), wins
two of the three matched seeds, and has the smallest observed dispersion
($\pm\AblationFullStd$).
The ablation indicates complementary roles for the two LLM touchpoints: the warm
start supplies the principal early performance gain, while bounded regional
refinement yields the best overall short-budget result when combined with that
initialization prior. All optimization evidence remains simulator grounded.
\end{abstract}
