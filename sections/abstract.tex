\begin{abstract}
Lithium-ion batteries are widely used in electric vehicles and energy-storage systems, making efficient and reliable fast charging increasingly important.
As charging requirements become more diverse, charging strategies must balance charging speed, safety, and battery degradation under different user preferences.
Bayesian optimization (BO) is well suited to such expensive black-box optimization because it constructs a probabilistic surrogate model and uses an acquisition function to select informative candidates, thereby reducing the number of simulator evaluations required.
However, two limitations remain when observations are scarce.
First, uninformed initialization can yield low-quality initial observations and prolong the cold-start phase.
Second, the subsequent BO search lacks an explicit mechanism to exploit battery-domain knowledge about promising regions under the active multiobjective preference.
In recent years, large language models (LLMs) have become popular,which can leverage domain knowledge and reason over the current optimization context, providing a natural source of task-specific prior information.
Accordingly, this paper proposes LLMBO-MO, an LLM-guided multiobjective Bayesian optimization framework for lithium-ion battery charging design.
Motivated by these advances, this paper proposes LLMBO-MO, an LLM-guided
multiobjective Bayesian optimization framework for lithium-ion battery
charging design.
LLMBO-MO first employs an LLM-informed initialization strategy to generate
promising and diverse initial charging protocols from battery-domain
knowledge.
It then introduces a posterior-covariance Region-Lift mechanism that converts
weight-conditioned LLM regional preferences into bounded acquisition-time mean
shifts, thereby changing candidate ranking.
Extensive experiments demonstrate that LLMBO-MO consistently outperforms
conventional BO methods and achieves a 9.3\% higher final hypervolume than
ParEGO.
\end{abstract}
