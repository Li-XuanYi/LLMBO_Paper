\begin{abstract}
Fast-charging protocol design is an expensive constrained multiobjective
problem involving charging time, peak temperature rise, and a degradation
proxy. This paper presents LLMBO-MO, a multiobjective Bayesian optimization
framework that uses large-language-model (LLM) output as an advisory
preference while preserving numerical control of the optimization loop. The
LLM proposes a diverse, protocol-domain-screened warm start and
weight-conditioned regions during an early guidance window. Accepted regions
can provide acquisition restarts and a bounded posterior-covariance adjustment
to the acquisition mean. Only electrochemical--thermal simulator outputs train
the Gaussian-process surrogate, and rejected guidance recovers ordinary
expected improvement exactly. Under matched budgets of 56 simulator
evaluations, five-seed experiments on the Chen2020 and Ecker2015
parameterizations yield mean final-hypervolume improvements of 0.84\% and
2.18\% over ParEGO, with paired wins in three and four of five seeds,
respectively. A component study further shows that the screened warm start
provides the more consistent individual gain, while bounded regional guidance
can complement it. The results demonstrate a conservative interface for using
language-model guidance in data-scarce battery protocol optimization without
treating generated content as physical evidence.
\end{abstract}
