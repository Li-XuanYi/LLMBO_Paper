\begin{abstract}
Fast-charging protocol design for lithium-ion batteries is a constrained
multiobjective optimization problem that must balance charging time, thermal
stress, and degradation under a limited evaluation budget.
Bayesian optimization (BO) is attractive for this setting because it can reduce
the number of expensive electrochemical--thermal simulations.
However, its early search is strongly influenced by a small initial design,
exactly when task-specific observations are scarce.
Large language models (LLMs) offer a possible source of battery-domain prior
knowledge, but their generated numerical outputs cannot be regarded as
simulator-validated physical evidence.
To address these two issues, this paper proposes LLMBO-MO, a multiobjective BO
framework that separates LLM-based search guidance from numerical optimization
evidence.
First, a protocol-domain-screened and diversity-aware LLM warm start is
developed to construct a more informative initial design from textual battery
knowledge.
As an auxiliary extension, temporary weight-conditioned regional guidance may
redirect acquisition search during the early BO stage.
In both cases, the simulator remains the sole objective oracle and only
evaluated protocols are used for Gaussian-process fitting and Pareto dominance.
Under a budget of 56 simulator evaluations, LLMBO-MO obtains a mean
hypervolume (HV) of \ChenLLMBOMean\ on Chen2020, compared with
\ChenParEGOMean\ for ParEGO, and the observed advantage persists under the
Ecker2015 parameterization.
A controlled ten-seed initialization study further shows an HV improvement of
\PromptHVGain\ over random initialization after Holm correction
($p=\PromptHolmP$).
Component analysis identifies the screened warm start as the more stable source
of improvement, whereas an independent systematic benefit from regional
guidance is not established.
These results indicate that LLM-derived domain knowledge is most useful as
bounded early-stage search guidance rather than as a substitute for
simulator-grounded numerical evidence.
\end{abstract}
