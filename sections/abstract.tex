\begin{abstract}
Fast-charging protocol design for lithium-ion batteries requires balancing
charging time, peak temperature rise, and a degradation proxy under tightly
limited evaluation budgets, because each candidate protocol must be assessed
through costly electrochemical--thermal simulation.
Bayesian optimization (BO) is well suited to this sample-scarce regime, but
its cold-start phase---where only a few initial observations shape the
surrogate---can waste precious evaluations on unpromising regions.
Large language models (LLMs) encode broad domain knowledge that could inform
early search decisions, yet their outputs are neither physical measurements
nor feasibility guarantees, making direct reliance on LLM proposals risky in
safety-constrained battery design.
Our key insight is to treat the LLM as an advisory preference source while
retaining the simulator as the sole arbiter of physical evidence.
We propose LLMBO-MO, a multiobjective BO framework with two advisory
touchpoints: a protocol-domain-screened warm-start that supplies diverse
initial candidates, and bounded regional guidance whose influence reverts
exactly to standard expected improvement when the LLM advice is invalid.
Only simulator-evaluated protocols train the Gaussian-process surrogate.
Under a matched budget of 56 simulator evaluations and five fixed seeds,
LLMBO-MO yields consistent hypervolume improvement over ParEGO on two SPMe
parameterizations, with a component study revealing that screened
warm-starting provides the dominant gain while regional guidance offers a
complementary benefit.
The results demonstrate a conservative, risk-bounded interface for
integrating language-model guidance into data-scarce battery optimization
without treating generated text as physical evidence.
\end{abstract}
