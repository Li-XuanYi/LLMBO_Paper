\begin{abstract}
Fast-charging protocol design is important for improving the usability and
lifetime of lithium-ion batteries, but it requires balancing charging speed,
thermal safety, and degradation under strict operating constraints.
Model-based evaluation can avoid extensive physical cycling experiments, yet
repeated electrochemical--thermal simulations remain computationally expensive.
Multiobjective Bayesian optimization (MOBO) is attractive in this setting
because it can approximate a Pareto set with a limited number of costly
evaluations; however, its early search can be inefficient when only sparse
observations are available.
This paper proposes LLMBO-MO, a decomposition-based multiobjective Bayesian
optimization method that augments the numerical MOBO loop with two bounded
large-language-model (LLM) guidance mechanisms.
First, a protocol-domain-screened and diversity-aware LLM-informed
initialization converts battery-domain knowledge into a more informative set of
initial charging protocols.
Second, a weight-conditioned regional-guidance mechanism uses the current
multiobjective trade-off direction and optimization state to provide bounded
search regions for early acquisition optimization, with an automatic fallback
to the original numerical search.
Both mechanisms modify only where the optimizer searches; objective values,
constraint satisfaction, GP training targets, and Pareto updates are determined
exclusively by simulator-evaluated protocols.
Under a budget of 56 simulator evaluations, LLMBO-MO achieves an approximately
\ChenDisplayedGain\ higher final hypervolume than ParEGO on Chen2020, and the
advantage persists on the Ecker2015 parameterization.
Additional mechanism studies further show that battery-specific LLM
initialization substantially improves the low-data search stage and that the
complete LLMBO-MO configuration provides the strongest short-budget result.
\end{abstract}
