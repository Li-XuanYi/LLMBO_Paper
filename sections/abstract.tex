\begin{abstract}
Lithium-ion batteries are widely used in electric vehicles and energy-storage systems, making efficient and reliable fast charging increasingly important.
As charging requirements become more diverse, charging strategies must balance charging speed, safety, and battery degradation under different user preferences.
Bayesian optimization (BO) has shown strong potential for battery charging
design because it can identify high-quality solutions with a limited number
of expensive evaluations.
However, when observations are sparse, BO can suffer from uninformed
initialization and insufficient task-specific guidance during early-stage
search, leaving valuable battery-domain knowledge underutilized.
Recent advances in large language models (LLMs), particularly their
contextual reasoning, few-shot learning, and domain-knowledge capabilities,
provide a promising means of complementing BO in such low-data regimes.
Motivated by these advances, this paper proposes LLMBO-MO, an LLM-guided
multiobjective Bayesian optimization framework for lithium-ion battery
charging design.
LLMBO-MO first employs an LLM-informed initialization strategy to generate
promising and diverse initial charging protocols from battery-domain
knowledge.
It then introduces a posterior-covariance Region-Lift mechanism that converts
weight-conditioned LLM regional preferences into bounded acquisition-time mean
shifts, thereby changing candidate ranking without inserting pseudo-observations
or altering GP uncertainty.
Extensive experiments demonstrate that LLMBO-MO consistently outperforms
conventional BO methods and achieves a 9.3\% higher final hypervolume than
ParEGO.
\end{abstract}
